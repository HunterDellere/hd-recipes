  note: 'Gong bao is defined by its "lychee flavor" (li zhi wei): sour and sweet in near balance, with the sour arriving first, over the scorched-chili and numbing-peppercorn base.   - 'Turn the range hood on high. Toasting dried chilies in hot oil puts capsaicin in the air, which makes everyone in the room cough.'

steps:
  - text: 'Marinate. In a bowl, combine the 450 g diced chicken thigh with the 2 g / ½ tsp kosher salt, the 10 g / 2 tsp light soy sauce, and the 10 g / 2 tsp Shaoxing wine; mix by hand for 30 seconds until the meat feels tacky. Add the 8 g / 1 tbsp potato starch and the 15 ml / 1 tbsp cold water and mix until each piece wears a thin, even coat. This is the seasoning stage for the meat: the salt and soy penetrate the small dice in 15 minutes, and the starch film protects the surface in the hot wok and grabs the sauce later. Rest 15 minutes at room temperature.'
    time_min: 16
  - text: 'Mix the sauce. In a small bowl, stir together the 18 g / 1½ tbsp granulated sugar, the 22 g / 1½ tbsp Chinkiang vinegar, the 7 g / 1¼ tsp light soy sauce, the 7 g / 1¼ tsp dark soy sauce, the 3 g / 1 tsp potato starch, the 30 ml / 2 tbsp chicken stock, and the 5 g / 1 tsp toasted sesame oil until the sugar and starch dissolve. Taste it: it should be sharply sweet-sour and slightly salty. If your vinegar is mild, add up to 5 g / 1 tsp more; the sour should lead.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Bloom the chilies and peppercorns. Heat a wok over high heat until a drop of water vaporizes on contact, then add the 40 g / 3 tbsp neutral oil and swirl. Lower the heat to medium, add the 6 g / about 12 snipped dried chilies and the 3 g / 1½ tsp Sichuan peppercorns, and stir constantly for 15 to 20 seconds, until the chilies darken to a deep red-brown and the oil smells toasty and numbing. Watch the color, not the clock: black chilies are bitter and must be discarded with the oil. The fat is extracting fat-soluble capsaicin and the peppercorn aromatics; this oil seasons everything that follows.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Stir-fry the chicken. Immediately turn the heat to high and add the marinated 450 g chicken. Spread it out, let it sit 30 seconds to take some color, then toss constantly until the pieces separate and turn opaque, about 2 minutes.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Add the aromatics. Add the 15 g / 4 cloves sliced garlic, the 15 g sliced ginger, and the 60 g scallion pieces and stir-fry 1 to 2 minutes, until fragrant and the chicken is cooked through: a cut piece shows no pink, and an instant-read thermometer in the largest piece reads at least 74 °C / 165 °F. Thigh tolerates (and is better at) a few degrees more.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Glaze. Stir the sauce again (the starch settles) and pour all of it, about 90 g, into the wok. Toss for 20 to 30 seconds until it thickens to a shiny glaze that coats the chicken with almost nothing left pooling in the wok. Starch gelatinizes near boiling, so the wok must be hot enough to bubble the sauce immediately.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Finish and serve. Off the heat, toss in the 80 g / ½ cup roasted peanuts and serve at once over the 450 g / 3 cups jasmine rice. Taste one bite with rice before serving: it should hit sour, then sweet, then salt, with numbing heat underneath. The chilies are for flavor and are not usually eaten.'
    time_min: 1

notes: |
  What makes it gong bao. Three things: chilies and Sichuan peppercorns scorched in oil until fragrant (hu la, "scorched chili" flavor), the sweet-sour "lychee" sauce in which vinegar slightly leads sugar, and peanuts added at the very end for crunch. Bell peppers, celery, zucchini, and a thick sweet sauce belong to the American-Chinese version.

  About the marinade. The velveting page describes the Cantonese baking-soda treatment for lean cuts and advises against using it on thigh. This recipe uses the lighter Sichuan starch marinade instead: salt, soy, wine, starch, no baking soda. Thigh does not need tenderizing; the starch coat is there for seasoning, a protected surface in the wok, and sauce cling.

  Why thigh. It stays juicy across a wider temperature window than breast, which matters in a stir-fry where you cannot easily take temperatures mid-toss. Cook it past 74 °C / 165 °F; it remains tender well beyond that.

  Heat level. The dish is meant to be fragrant and numbing with moderate heat. Discarding the seeds and stopping the chilies at red-brown keeps it from turning harsh. To make it hotter, add more chilies rather than toasting them longer.
