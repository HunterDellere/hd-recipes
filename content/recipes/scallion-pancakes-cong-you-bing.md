steps:
  - text: 'Make the hot-water dough. In a bowl, whisk the 300 g / 2⅓ cups flour for the dough with the 3 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt. Pour in the 120 ml / ½ cup boiling water while stirring with chopsticks or a wooden spoon, until shaggy flakes form. Boiling water gelatinizes part of the starch and weakens gluten, which makes a dough that rolls thin without springing back and fries up tender-chewy instead of tough. Add the 80 ml / ⅓ cup room-temperature water and stir until no dry flour remains.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Knead and rest. When the dough is cool enough to handle, knead it in the bowl or on the counter for 3 to 4 minutes, until smooth and soft (it will be slightly tacky; dust with up to 10 g / 1 tbsp flour only if it sticks badly). Cover the bowl with a damp kitchen towel or an upturned plate and rest at room temperature for at least 30 minutes, up to 2 hours. Resting relaxes the gluten so the dough will roll paper-thin.'
    time_min: 35
  - text: 'Make the oil paste (you mian). In a small heatproof bowl, combine the 25 g / 3 tbsp flour for the paste, the 5 g / ¾ tsp fine sea salt, and the 1 g / ½ tsp ground white pepper. Heat the 50 g / ¼ cup neutral oil in a small saucepan until it shimmers (about 180 °C / 350 °F; a scallion slice dropped in sizzles hard at once), pour it over the flour mixture, and stir to a smooth, loose paste. The hot oil lightly toasts the flour. This paste carries all of the seasoning for the pancake interior, so taste a dab: it should be distinctly salty.'
    technique: 'hot-oil-finish'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Make the dip. Stir together the 30 g / 2 tbsp light soy sauce, the 30 g / 2 tbsp Chinkiang vinegar, and the 3 g / ¾ tsp granulated sugar until the sugar dissolves. Add the 5 g ginger matchsticks and, if using, the 15 g / 1 tbsp chili oil. Set aside so the ginger infuses.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Roll the sheets. Cut the rested dough into quarters, about 125 g each, and keep the pieces you are not working with under the damp towel. Lightly oil the counter and a rolling pin with a few drops from the 60 g frying oil (oil instead of flour, so the surface stays tacky). Roll one piece into a rectangle about 20 by 35 cm, thin enough that you can almost see the counter through it.'
    time_min: 6
  - text: 'Laminate and coil. Spread a quarter of the oil paste (about 20 g / 1½ tbsp) over the sheet in a thin even layer, leaving a 1 cm border. Scatter a quarter of the scallions (30 g) evenly over it. Starting from a long side, roll the sheet up tightly into a long rope, pinching the seam closed. Gently stretch the rope a little longer, then coil it like a snail shell and tuck the outer end underneath. Repeat with the remaining 3 pieces, cover the coils with the towel, and rest 15 minutes.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Flatten. Press each coil flat with your palm, then roll it gently into a round about 18 cm across and 5 mm thick. Roll from the center outward with light pressure; a little scallion breaking through the surface is fine, but pressing hard fuses the layers you just built.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Pan-fry. Heat a 25 to 30 cm cast-iron or carbon-steel skillet over medium heat and add 15 g / 1 tbsp of the frying oil. Lay in one pancake and cook 2 to 3 minutes, until the underside is deep golden with darker spots. Flip with a metal spatula and cook the second side 2 to 3 minutes. Too high a heat browns the outside before the inner layers cook through; medium lets the steam from the dough puff the layers apart.'
    time_min: 6
  - text: 'Separate the layers. While the pancake is still in the pan, use two metal spatulas (or a spatula and a wooden spoon) to push the edges toward the center a few times, as if scrunching it. The pancake will compress and spring back with its layers loosened and visible. Flip once more for 30 seconds to crisp the newly exposed edges. Move to a wire rack set over a sheet pan and keep warm in a 95 °C / 200 °F oven while you fry the rest, adding 15 g / 1 tbsp oil for each pancake.'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Serve. Cut each pancake into 6 wedges with a sharp knife or kitchen shears and serve hot with the dip, which brings the acid (the 30 g black vinegar) and the glutamate (the 30 g light soy) that the pancake itself does not carry.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Why the oil paste matters. Spreading plain oil between the layers works poorly: when the coil is flattened, the oil is squeezed toward the edges and the layers fuse. A flour-and-oil paste stays where you spread it, keeping the dough sheets separated so they puff apart in the pan. It is also where the salt and pepper go, so the interior is seasoned evenly.

  Why hot water. Mixing part of the flour with boiling water pre-cooks some of its starch and limits gluten development. The result rolls very thin without shrinking back and stays tender and chewy after frying, instead of turning bready or tough.

  Seasoning stages. The dough gets a little salt for flavor; the oil paste is deliberately salty because it seasons the inside; the dip adds soy for savoriness and black vinegar for acid. Taste the paste before you spread it, because once the pancakes are rolled there is no adjusting.

  Vegan claim assumes plain neutral oil in the paste and a chili oil without animal fat.
