before_you_start:
  - 'Take the 75 g butter for the dough and the 120 g butter for the filling out of the refrigerator 1 to 2 hours ahead. The filling butter must be soft enough to spread like thick frosting; cold butter tears the dough.'
  - 'Grind the cardamom before anything else. Crack the pods, pick out the black seeds and grind them in a mortar and pestle or spice grinder; pre-ground cardamom has lost most of its aroma and is the most common reason homemade versions taste flat.'
  - 'Timeline: about 20 minutes of mixing and kneading, a 60 to 90 minute first rise, 30 minutes of shaping, a 45 to 60 minute second rise, and 12 to 14 minutes in the oven. For fresh buns in the morning, let the dough do its first rise slowly in the refrigerator overnight (8 to 16 hours), covered; shape it straight from the fridge (cold dough is easier to knot) and allow 75 to 90 minutes for the second rise.'

steps:
  - text: 'Mix the dough. In the bowl of a stand mixer, stir the 240 ml / 1 cup lukewarm whole milk, the 7 g / 2¼ tsp instant yeast and the 50 g / 1 egg together. Add the 450 g / 3½ cups all-purpose flour, the 60 g / 5 tbsp granulated sugar, the 5 g / ¾ tsp fine sea salt and 5 g / about 2½ tsp of the ground cardamom. Mix with the dough hook on low speed for 2 minutes until no dry flour remains. The dough will be stiff.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Knead on medium-low speed for 6 minutes, until the dough is smooth and pulls away from the bowl. Then add the 75 g / 5 tbsp softened dough butter a tablespoon at a time, letting each piece disappear before adding the next, and knead another 6 to 8 minutes. The dough will look greasy and break apart when the butter goes in, then come back together smooth, soft and slightly tacky. Building the gluten before the fat goes in is what lets an enriched dough rise high; the fat coats gluten strands that have not yet formed. Check with a windowpane test: a small piece stretched between your fingers should go thin enough to see light through before it tears. By hand, knead 10 minutes before the butter and 10 minutes after.'
    technique: 'enriched-dough'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Shape the dough into a ball, put it in a lightly buttered bowl, and cover with a fitted lid or a damp kitchen towel. Let it rise in a warm spot (24 to 26 °C / 75 to 79 °F) for 60 to 90 minutes, until doubled. Or refrigerate it overnight (see Before you start).'
    time_min: 75
  - text: 'While the dough rises, make the filling. Beat the 120 g / 1 stick plus 1 tbsp very soft filling butter with the 75 g / 6 tbsp granulated sugar, the 6 g / about 1 tbsp remaining ground cardamom and the 1 g / pinch fine sea salt, using a wooden spoon, until smooth and spreadable. Leave it at room temperature. Line two sheet pans with parchment.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Roll and fill. Turn the dough onto a lightly floured counter and roll it into a 30 by 45 cm / 12 by 18-inch rectangle with a long side facing you. Spread the filling evenly over the whole surface with an offset spatula, right to the edges. Fold the bottom third up over the middle, then the top third down over that, like a letter, so you have a long three-layer strip about 10 by 45 cm / 4 by 18 inches. Press it gently with the rolling pin to seal the layers and widen it slightly to about 12 cm / 5 inches. If the filling has turned soft and greasy, slide the strip onto a sheet pan and chill it for 15 minutes; cold layers cut and twist cleanly.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Cut the strips. With a sharp knife or pizza wheel, cut the folded dough across its width into 14 strips, each about 3 cm / 1¼ inches wide and 12 cm / 5 inches long. Then cut each strip lengthwise into two legs, leaving 1.5 cm / ½ inch at the top still joined.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Knot the buns. Work with one strip at a time and keep the rest covered with a towel. Hold the joined end and twist the two legs around each other three or four times, stretching gently as you go, until you have a twisted rope about 25 cm / 10 inches long. Lay the rope over the first two fingers of your non-dominant hand with the joined end held under your thumb, and wrap the rope once around both fingers, crossing over the joined end. Wrap it around a second time, then bring the loose end over the top and push it down through the middle of the coil, from top to bottom. Slide the knot off your fingers and tuck the loose end firmly underneath so the bun sits on it. The finished bun should look like a tight twisted ball with layers of filling showing in the grooves. Set the buns 5 cm / 2 inches apart on the lined pans, 7 per pan. Some filling will leak; that is expected and it caramelizes in the oven.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Cover the buns loosely with damp kitchen towels and let them rise for 45 to 60 minutes (75 to 90 minutes if the dough came from the refrigerator), until puffy and about 1½ times their size. A finger pressed gently into the side should leave a dent that springs back slowly. Heat the oven to 200 °C / 400 °F with racks in the upper and lower thirds about 20 minutes before they are ready.'
    time_min: 50
  - text: 'Brush the buns with the 50 g / 1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp water, then scatter the 40 g / 3 tbsp Swedish pearl sugar over the tops. Bake for 12 to 14 minutes, swapping and rotating the pans halfway, until deep golden brown and the internal temperature reads 88 to 90 °C / 190 to 195 °F. Pale buns are underbaked and doughy at the core; the filling should be bubbling and dark at the edges.'
    time_min: 14
  - text: 'Cool on the pans for 5 minutes, then move to a wire rack. They are best within a few hours. Freeze any extras once fully cool, in a glass container or wrapped in beeswax wrap inside a lidded tin, for up to 1 month, and refresh them in a 160 °C / 325 °F oven for 5 to 6 minutes.'
    time_min: 10

notes: |
  Why fresh cardamom matters. The aroma of green cardamom comes from volatile compounds, mainly 1,8-cineole and alpha-terpinyl acetate, held inside the seeds. Once ground they start evaporating within hours, which is why jarred ground cardamom tastes dusty and medicinal compared with seeds you crush yourself. Grind them the day you bake. A coarser grind in the filling, with some visible flecks, is traditional and gives little bursts of flavor.

  The fold-and-cut method. Rolling up a log and slicing it, as for American cinnamon rolls, gives spirals. Folding the filled dough into three layers and cutting strips instead gives a strip with filling sandwiched between dough layers, which twists into a rope with filling exposed along every groove. That is what makes the knot caramelize at the edges while the middle stays soft.

  Sugar level and yeast. At about 13% sugar to flour, this dough sits just under the point where standard instant yeast slows noticeably, so it does not need osmotolerant yeast. If you sweeten the dough further, expect a slower rise.

  Salt. The dough is salted at about 1.1% of the flour weight, the standard for a sweet dough; without it the cardamom and butter taste flat and the buns read only sweet. The pinch in the filling does the same for the butter-sugar layer.
