  note: 'Butter chicken is a 20th-century dish credited to the Moti Mahal restaurant in Delhi, founded by cooks from Peshawar after Partition: leftover tandoori chicken simmered in a buttery tomato gravy so it would not dry out. The broiler stands in for the tandoor here; the char it builds is what gives the gravy its faint smokiness.'

before_you_start:
  - 'Marinate the chicken at least 4 hours and up to 24 hours ahead, covered in a glass or ceramic dish in the refrigerator. Under 4 hours the salt and yogurt only reach the surface; past 24 hours the yogurt acid starts to make the meat mealy.'
  - 'Pull the chicken from the fridge 20 minutes before broiling, and make the gravy while the broiler heats. The gravy takes about 35 minutes and holds happily; the chicken goes into it at the end.'
  - 'Canned whole peeled tomatoes are the right choice from autumn through spring, when supermarket fresh tomatoes are pale and watery. One 28-oz can is exactly the amount needed.'

steps:
  - text: 'Marinate. In a glass or ceramic bowl, stir together the 120 g / ½ cup Greek yogurt, 20 g / 4 tsp ginger-garlic paste, 15 ml / 1 tbsp lemon juice, 5 g / 2 tsp Kashmiri chili powder, 2 g / 1 tsp garam masala, 2 g / 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 g / ¼ tsp turmeric, 7 g / 1¾ tsp kosher salt, and 15 ml / 1 tbsp neutral oil into a thick red paste. Add the 800 g chicken pieces and turn until every surface is coated. The salt seasons the meat through and helps it hold moisture under the broiler; the yogurt and lemon tenderize the surface and give the char something to cling to. Cover with a fitted lid or a plate and refrigerate 4 to 24 hours.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Start the gravy. Melt the 30 g / 2 tbsp butter for the gravy base in a heavy saucepan over medium heat. Add the 4 cracked cardamom pods, 4 cloves, the 3 g / 1-inch cinnamon stick, and the 1 bay leaf; fry 30 seconds until they smell fragrant and the butter foams around them. Add the 25 g / 5 tsp ginger-garlic paste and fry, stirring with a wooden spoon, 1 minute until the raw smell is gone and it just begins to color.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Simmer the tomatoes and cashews. Add the 800 g / 28-oz can hand-crushed tomatoes, then swirl the 120 ml / ½ cup water in the can and add it. Stir in the 50 g / ⅓ cup raw cashews, 5 g / 2 tsp Kashmiri chili powder, and 5 g / 1¼ tsp kosher salt. Bring to a simmer, cover partway, and cook 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomatoes have collapsed and a cashew crushes easily against the side of the pan. Salting now lets the tomatoes season while they cook, rather than tasting salted on top.'
    time_min: 22
  - text: 'Heat the broiler. While the gravy simmers, set an oven rack so the top of the chicken will sit about 10 cm / 4 inches below the broiler element and heat the broiler on high for 10 minutes. Line a rimmed sheet pan with foil and set a wire rack on it.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Char the chicken. Lift the 800 g chicken out of the marinade, letting the excess drip off, and spread the pieces on the rack with space between them; crowded pieces steam rather than char. Brush with half of the 15 g / 1 tbsp melted ghee. Broil 8 minutes, until blackened at the edges and in spots. Flip, brush with the remaining ghee, and broil 5 more minutes. The chicken is done when the thickest piece reads at least 74 °C / 165 °F on an instant-read thermometer; thighs stay juicy well past that, up to about 85 °C / 185 °F, so err toward more char. Rest the chicken on the rack while you finish the gravy.'
    time_min: 14
  - text: 'Blend the gravy. Fish out the 3 g cinnamon stick and the 1 bay leaf with tongs. Transfer the gravy to a blender, vent the lid (remove the center cap and cover the opening with a folded kitchen towel so steam can escape), and blend on high 1 minute until completely smooth and pale orange-red. For the most velvety result, press it through a fine sieve back into the saucepan with the back of a ladle; with a high-speed blender this step can be skipped.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Enrich and reduce. Return the gravy to medium-low heat and stir in the 30 g / 2 tbsp butter for enriching and the 8 g / 1 tsp honey. Simmer, stirring often (it spits), 8 minutes, until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon and small beads of orange butterfat appear on the surface. Taste for salt; it should taste fully seasoned and bright, with the honey only taking the raw edge off the tomato.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Combine and simmer. Add the 800 g charred chicken and any juices from the sheet pan to the gravy. Simmer gently 5 minutes so the chicken absorbs some of the gravy and the char flavors it. Crush the 3 g / 1 tbsp kasuri methi between your palms directly over the pot (rubbing breaks the dried leaves and releases their aroma), then add the 2 g / 1 tsp garam masala for finishing. Both go in late so their volatile aromatics survive.'
    time_min: 6
  - text: 'Finish. Stir in the 120 ml / ½ cup heavy cream and heat through without boiling hard. Off the heat, stir in the 15 g / 1 tbsp cold butter until it melts into a glossy surface. Taste and add the 5 ml / 1 tsp lemon juice if the gravy reads heavy; it should be rich, tangy, and gently warm rather than sweet. Serve with a swirl of cream if you like.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Why the char matters. Butter chicken began as a way to use leftover tandoori chicken, so the chicken should arrive in the gravy already blackened in spots. That char adds bitter, smoky Maillard and pyrolysis notes that the sweet-sour gravy needs; chicken poached directly in the gravy produces a flatter, sweeter dish. A broiler on high with the chicken close to the element is the closest home stand-in for a tandoor, and the rack lets heat reach the underside so the pieces do not stew in their own juices.

  Why thighs. Thigh meat has more fat and connective tissue than breast, so it stays juicy well above the 74 °C / 165 °F safe temperature and through the final simmer. Breast works (see substitutions) but leaves almost no margin for error.

  Building the gravy. The tomatoes and cashews simmer together, then blend into one smooth emulsion: the cashews thicken it and add a mild, creamy body, while the butter and cream are added after blending so they stay glossy rather than cooking into the solids. The gravy should taste tangy first and rich second. Many restaurant versions read sugary; the honey here is only enough to soften canned-tomato acidity, so add it gradually if your tomatoes are already sweet.

  Umami and acid. Cooked-down tomato is the glutamate source and also the main acid, backed by the yogurt and lemon in the marinade. The final squeeze of lemon is a correction, not a feature: add it only if the cream and butter have dulled the tomato.

  Kasuri methi and garam masala go in last. Both carry volatile aromatics that fade with long cooking, so they are added in the final minutes, as the South Asian tradition does with garam masala generally.

  Make ahead. The gravy keeps 4 days refrigerated or 3 months frozen (without the cream). Broil the chicken fresh on the day for the best char, or refrigerate cooked chicken in the gravy for up to 3 days; it reheats well over low heat with a splash of water.
