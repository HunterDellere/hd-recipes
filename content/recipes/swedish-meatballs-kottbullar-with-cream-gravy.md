steps:
  - text: 'Pressgurka. Toss the 350 g / 1 thinly sliced cucumber with the 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt in a glass bowl. Set a plate on top and weigh it down with a heavy jar for 15 minutes; the salt draws out water so the slices turn supple and take up the brine instead of diluting it. Meanwhile, stir the 35 ml / 2⅓ tbsp white vinegar, 25 g / 2 tbsp sugar and 25 ml / 1⅔ tbsp water together until the sugar dissolves. Squeeze the cucumber firmly by handfuls, discard the liquid, and toss the slices with the brine and the 5 g / 2 tbsp chopped parsley. Cover and refrigerate at least 30 minutes.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Panade and onion. Melt the 10 g / 2 tsp onion butter in a small pan over medium-low heat and cook the 100 g / 1 small grated onion with its juice for 5 minutes, until soft and no longer sharp; let it cool. In a large bowl, stir the 30 g / ⅓ cup breadcrumbs into the 100 ml whole milk and leave 5 minutes to swell into a paste. The soaked crumbs hold milk inside the meatball as it cooks, so the texture stays soft and juicy instead of tightening.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Mix. Add the cooled onion, the 250 g ground beef, 250 g ground pork, 50 g / 1 egg, 7 g / 2⅓ tsp kosher salt, 1 g / ½ tsp allspice and 1 g / ½ tsp white pepper to the panade. Mix with one hand for about 1 minute, just until evenly combined and slightly sticky. Mix too little and the meatballs crumble; knead much longer and they turn bouncy and dense. To check seasoning, fry a pinch of the mixture, taste, and adjust before shaping.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Shape. With hands dipped in cold water, roll the mixture into small balls about 3 cm / 1¼ in across, about 20 g each; you should get around 36. Set them on a parchment-lined tray. Small meatballs are the Swedish standard: more browned surface per bite and they cook through quickly.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Brown. Heat the oven to 90 °C / 200 °F to keep the cooked meatballs warm. In a wide skillet over medium heat, heat half of the 15 g neutral oil and half of the 30 g butter (about 7 g oil and 15 g / 1 tbsp butter) until the butter foams. Add half the meatballs in a single layer with space between them. Brown 8 minutes, shaking the pan every minute or so to roll them, until deep brown all over. Move to an ovenproof dish in the oven and brown the second half in the remaining 8 g oil and 15 g / 1 tbsp butter. Do not wash the pan; the browned bits are the base of the gravy.'
    technique: 'maillard-sear'
    time_min: 18
  - text: 'Gravy. Pour off all but about 20 g / 1½ tbsp of the fat. Over medium heat, whisk the 15 g / 2 tbsp flour into the fat and cook 1 minute, until it smells toasty. Whisk in the 300 ml / 1¼ cups beef broth gradually, scraping up the browned bits, then the 150 ml / ⅔ cup cream and the 10 g / 2 tsp soy sauce. Simmer 5 minutes, whisking, until the gravy coats a spoon. Taste and add a pinch of kosher salt and white pepper if needed; the soy and broth carry salt, so taste before adding any.'
    technique: 'deglazing'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Finish. Return the meatballs to the gravy and simmer gently 2 minutes, turning them to coat. A meatball cut open should be cooked through with no pink, and read 71 °C / 160 °F in the center. Serve with potatoes, about 30 g / 2 tbsp of the 120 g lingonberry preserves per plate, and the drained pressgurka from the 350 g cucumber on the side.'
    time_min: 3

notes: |
  The panade. Breadcrumbs soaked in milk sit between the meat proteins and keep them from bonding tightly as they cook, and they hold water that would otherwise run out. That is the difference between a soft, fine-textured Swedish meatball and a firm, springy one. Keep the mixing brief for the same reason.

  Allspice and white pepper. Allspice is the defining spice of köttbullar: warm and clove-like, but in a quantity you should notice only as a background. White pepper gives heat without black flecks. Grind allspice fresh from whole berries if you can; ground allspice loses its aroma within months.

  Umami and acid. The browned fond, the beef broth and the soy sauce in the gravy supply glutamate (soy in cream gravy is ordinary Swedish practice, sold there as kinesisk soja). The acid lives on the side of the plate: the vinegar in the pressgurka and the tart lingonberries. Each forkful is meant to combine meat, gravy and one or the other, which is why neither is optional.

  Lingonberries. In the US, Swedish lingonberry preserves are sold at IKEA food markets, Scandinavian delis and many large supermarkets; frozen lingonberries are rare outside Scandinavian shops. If you find frozen berries, stir 200 g berries with 70 g granulated sugar until dissolved (rårörda lingon, stirred lingonberries) and refrigerate; it keeps a week.

  Pressgurka timing. It is crisp and sharp after 30 minutes and softer and sweeter after a day. It keeps 3 days refrigerated in a glass container.
