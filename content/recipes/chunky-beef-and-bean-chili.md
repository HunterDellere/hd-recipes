before_you_start:
  - 'Salt the beef at least 1 hour and up to 24 hours ahead (covered, in the fridge). The salt seasons the small pieces all the way through and helps the leaner ones hold moisture during the braise.'
  - 'Look over the package before salting. Chili-cut packs vary: cut any piece larger than 2.5 cm in half so everything finishes together, and pull off any gristle or silverskin. If the meat is mostly deep red with little white marbling, it is round rather than chuck; plan on the shorter end of the braise and check it early.'
  - 'Cook the beans a day ahead: about 220 g / 1 heaping cup dried kidney beans gives the 500 g cooked beans needed here (see Make it from scratch). They need an overnight soak first, so start them two nights before if you are also salting the beef overnight. Canned beans work if you have to.'
  - 'Start the chiles soaking before you render the bacon. They need 20 minutes and you will be busy at the stove.'

steps:
  - text: 'Salt the beef. Trim and halve any oversized pieces of the 1360 g chili-cut beef (see Before you start), then toss with the 14 g / 1½ tbsp kosher salt in a large bowl. Cover with a plate or fitted lid and refrigerate at least 1 hour and up to 24 hours.'
    time_min: 60
  - text: 'Toast the chiles and cumin. Heat a dry cast-iron skillet over medium heat. Press the 40 g ancho and 30 g guajillo chiles flat against the pan with a metal spatula, about 15 seconds per side, until they smell toasty and turn a shade darker. Pull them the moment they smell sharp or show black spots; scorched chiles turn the whole pot bitter. Toast the 4 g / 2 tsp cumin seeds in the same pan for about 1 minute, shaking, then tip them into the blender jar.'
    technique: 'dry-toasting'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Soak. Put the chiles in a heatproof bowl, pour over the 750 ml boiling water, and weigh them down with a small plate. Soak 20 minutes until soft and pliable, then drain and discard the soaking water, which holds most of the bitterness from the skins.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Render the bacon while the chiles soak. Heat the oven to 150 °C / 300 °F. Put the 115 g diced bacon in a large Dutch oven with 60 ml / ¼ cup water over medium heat. The water keeps the bacon from scorching while the fat melts out; once it evaporates the bacon fries. Cook about 10 minutes, stirring, until crisp and deep brown. Lift it out with a slotted spoon onto a paper towel and keep it for serving. You want about 45 ml / 3 tbsp fat in the pot; if there is less, add the 15 ml / 1 tbsp neutral oil.'
    technique: 'lardon-rendering'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Brown the beef. Pat the salted beef dry. Raise the heat to medium-high. Add a third of the beef in a single layer with space between pieces and leave it alone for 3 minutes, until the underside is deep brown, then turn and brown a second side, about 2 minutes more. Transfer to a bowl and repeat in two more batches. Small pieces brown fast and overcook fast: the goal is a crust and a dark fond on the pot, not cooked-through meat.'
    technique: 'maillard-sear'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Blend the purée. Put the drained chiles in the blender with the toasted cumin, the 30 g / 2 chipotles in adobo with their sauce, the 25 g / 6 cloves garlic, the 2 g / 2 tsp Mexican oregano, and the 375 ml / 1½ cups blending beef broth. Blend on high for 2 full minutes until completely smooth.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Build the base. Lower the heat to medium. Add the 300 g diced onion, the 150 g diced poblano, and the 2 g / ½ tsp kosher salt for the vegetables; the salt pulls out water that lifts the fond. Cook 7 minutes, scraping the bottom with a wooden spoon, until soft and golden. Add the 30 g / 2 tbsp tomato paste and stir 2 minutes until it darkens to brick red. Pour in the chile purée and cook, stirring constantly, for 5 minutes, until it thickens to a paste that spatters. Frying the purée in the fat cooks off the raw chile edge and deepens the color.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Deglaze and braise. Pour in the 355 ml beer and scrape up anything still stuck to the pot. Simmer 3 minutes to cook off the alcohol. Add the 400 g hand-crushed tomatoes with their juices, the 250 ml / 1 cup braising beef broth, the 15 ml / 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, the 6 g / 1½ tsp dark brown sugar, and the beef with its juices. Bring to a simmer, cover with the lid slightly ajar, and move to the 150 °C / 300 °F oven. Braise 1½ to 2 hours, stirring once halfway, until a piece of beef crushes easily against the side of the pot. The oven holds the pot below a boil, which is what keeps leaner pieces from tightening and drying out.'
    technique: 'deglazing'
    time_min: 105
  - text: 'Add the beans and thicken. Move the pot to the stovetop over medium heat. Stir in the 500 g drained kidney beans. Whisk the 20 g / 2½ tbsp masa harina into the 90 ml / 6 tbsp cool water until smooth, stir it into the chili, and simmer uncovered 15 minutes, stirring often. The beans are already cooked; 15 minutes lets them take on the gravy without splitting. If the chili is thicker than you like, loosen it with a splash of the bean cooking liquid.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Finish and season. Off the heat, stir in the 15 ml / 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar. Taste a piece of beef with some gravy and add up to 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt if it tastes flat. The vinegar should not taste sour; it sharpens the chile and cuts the bacon fat.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Serve. Ladle into bowls and top each with some of the reserved crisp bacon, the 120 g grated cheddar, a spoonful of the 120 g sour cream, and the 30 g sliced scallions. Put the 2 limes, in wedges, on the table.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  Why chili-cut beef needs its own method. Chili-cut packs are trimmings from the chuck and sometimes the round, cut small. Chuck has the fat and collagen to survive hours in the pot; round has little of either and turns dry and stringy if it boils. Small pieces also have far more surface area per gram than 2.5 cm cubes, so they brown quickly and finish braising in about half the time. The method follows from that: salt ahead for moisture retention, brown hard but briefly, and braise covered in a 150 °C oven where the liquid stays below a boil.

  Three sources of smoke and depth. Bacon fat for browning, chipotle in the purée, and the fond from the beef. Ancho supplies dried-fruit sweetness and body, guajillo supplies red color and tang, and chipotle supplies heat and smoke. Worcestershire sauce, tomato paste, and browned beef carry the glutamate; cider vinegar and lime are the acids.

  Beans go in late. Cooked beans simmered for 2 more hours break down, thicken the gravy unevenly, and lose their shape. Added in the last 15 minutes, they hold together and still pick up the chile flavor.

  Better the next day. Overnight in the fridge the chile flavor rounds out and the fat sets on top where it can be lifted off. Reheat gently and adjust with a splash of water or broth, since it thickens as it sits.
