    ref: 'safety/egg-doneness'
    why: 'The center of this cake is a just-set egg custard and is meant to stay soft. Its temperature climbs slowly through the last part of the bake and keeps rising from residual heat for several minutes after the cake leaves the oven, so it holds above 60 °C / 140 °F for longer than the few minutes the egg-pasteurization curve calls for, but it never reaches the 71 °C single-reading number. For pregnancy, immunocompromise, the very young or the very old, use pasteurized in-shell eggs; the cake bakes identically.'

before_you_start:
  - 'Set the 907 g cream cheese, the 6 eggs and the 480 ml heavy cream on the counter 1 to 2 hours ahead. Cold cream cheese leaves lumps that no amount of mixing removes without whipping in air, and air is what makes the cake rise, crack and sink.'
  - 'Budget at least 2 hours of cooling at room temperature plus 4 hours in the refrigerator (overnight is better) before slicing. The center finishes setting as it cools; cut warm, it runs across the plate. Take it out of the refrigerator 1 hour before serving so the middle softens back to cream.'

steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F (conventional, not convection) with a rack in the middle. Take two 40 cm / 16-inch squares of parchment, crumple them into tight balls, then flatten them out; crumpled paper folds into the pan without fighting back. Butter is unnecessary. Press the two sheets, one crossed over the other, into a 23 cm / 9-inch springform with sides at least 7.5 cm / 3 inches tall, pleating them against the walls and leaving 5 cm / 2 inches of paper standing above the rim. The batter rises well above the pan edge in the oven and the paper collar holds it. Set the pan on a rimmed sheet tray to catch any butterfat that seeps through the springform seam.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'In a large bowl, beat the 907 g / 2 lb room-temperature cream cheese with the 350 g / 1¾ cups granulated sugar and the 3 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt, using a stand mixer with the paddle on low speed or a wooden spoon, for about 2 minutes until completely smooth and the sugar no longer feels gritty. Scrape the bowl and paddle with a metal bench scraper or spoon. Every lump left at this stage stays in the finished cake.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Add the 300 g / 6 eggs one at a time on low speed, mixing each until it disappears before adding the next. Keep the speed low throughout: the goal is to combine, not to aerate. A whipped batter souffles in the hot oven, cracks deeply and falls into a dense ring.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Pour in the 480 ml / 2 cups heavy cream and mix on low until uniform. Sift the 16 g / 2 tbsp all-purpose flour over the surface and mix just until it disappears. The flour is a small starch insurance that lets the center hold a soft slice instead of collapsing; more than this turns the custard cakey. The batter will be thin, about the consistency of heavy cream itself. Pass it through a fine sieve into the lined pan if you see any flecks of cheese.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Bake for 50 to 60 minutes. The cake will puff high above the rim, and the top will go from golden to deep mahogany to nearly black in patches; that scorch is the flavor of the dish (caramelized sugar and browned milk protein against the plain cream below), so do not pull it early for color. It is done when the outer 5 cm / 2 inches look set and the center wobbles freely like a just-set custard when you nudge the pan, with an internal temperature of about 65 °C / 150 °F at the center. If the top is still only golden at 50 minutes, move the pan to the top rack for the last 5 minutes.'
    time_min: 55
  - text: 'Cool the cake in its pan on a wire rack at room temperature for 2 hours. It will fall and the top will wrinkle and crack at the edges as it settles; this is the look of the dish. Then refrigerate it, still in the pan and loosely covered with a beeswax wrap or an inverted plate, for at least 4 hours or overnight.'
    time_min: 360
  - text: 'One hour before serving, take the cake out of the refrigerator. Release the springform ring, lift the cake by its parchment onto a plate, and peel the paper away from the sides (leave the base sheet under it). Slice with a long thin knife dipped in hot water and wiped between cuts. Serve plain: the burnt top, the firmer ring and the soft center are the whole dessert.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  Why it works with no crust and no water bath. A New York cheesecake is baked low and slow in a water bath so the whole custard sets evenly and the surface stays pale. This cake runs the opposite logic: a hot oven sets and caramelizes the outside fast while the center lags behind, so you get three textures in one slice. Pulling it while the center still wobbles is the whole technique; residual heat finishes it to a soft set as it cools.

  Why the parchment is crumpled. Flat parchment will not follow the curve of the pan wall and leaves gaps that the thin batter fills, which then fuse to the metal. Crumpling breaks the paper''s stiffness so it pleats tightly, and the irregular folds print the rustic edge the cake is known for.

  Salt and acid. This is a sweet custard, so there is no glutamate here, but balance still matters: the cream cheese supplies lactic tang, and the 3 g of salt keeps 350 g of sugar from tasting one-note. The cake at La Viña is unsalted; if you want that version exactly, leave it out.

  Storage. Treat the cake like any custard: keep it refrigerated, covered, for up to 4 days.
