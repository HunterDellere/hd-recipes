steps:
  - text: 'Build the sandwiches. Trim the crusts from the 4 slices (200 g) of bread; crusts stay leathery when fried, and a cut edge lets the egg seal the sandwich shut. Spread 20 g / about 1 tbsp of the 40 g peanut butter over one slice for each sandwich, stopping 5 mm short of the edges, top with a second slice, and press the edges together firmly.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Beat the egg. In a shallow dish wide enough for a sandwich, beat the 100 g / 2 eggs with the 0.5 g pinch fine sea salt until completely uniform, with no strands of white left. Streaks of white fry into rubbery patches. The salt sharpens the sweetness of the syrup later.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Heat the oil. Pour the 280 g / 1¼ cups neutral oil into a 20 cm / 8-inch skillet and heat over medium-high to 170 °C / 340 °F on an instant-read thermometer. Without one, a scrap of bread crust should sizzle steadily and brown in about 30 seconds.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Dip. Lay one sandwich in the egg for about 5 seconds per side, then stand it on each of its four edges for a second or two, so the whole surface is coated. Do not soak it longer: the outside should be coated while the center stays mostly dry, because egg that reaches the middle does not set and leaves a soggy core.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Fry. Lower the sandwich into the oil with a metal spatula and fry for about 90 seconds, spooning hot oil over the top the whole time so the upper side puffs and sets. Flip and fry another 90 seconds, until both faces are deep golden and the sandwich has puffed. Using tongs, hold it on each edge in the oil for about 10 seconds to seal and brown the sides. Drain on a wire rack for 30 seconds. Dip and fry the second sandwich the same way, letting the oil return to 170 °C / 340 °F first.'
    technique: 'deep-fry'
    time_min: 6
  - text: 'Serve. Set each sandwich on a plate, lay a 15 g slab of the 30 g cold butter on top, and pour 20 g / 1 tbsp of the 40 g golden syrup over each. Eat right away with a knife and fork, while the butter is still melting into the crust.'
    time_min: 1

notes: |
  Why fry in oil, not butter. The Western French toast soaks the bread through in custard and cooks it slowly in butter. The Hong Kong version coats only the outside in plain egg and fries it hot, so the egg sets into a puffed, crisp shell around soft bread and warm, loose peanut butter. Butter in the pan would burn at this temperature, so the butter goes on top at the end.

  Why the bread matters. Thick, soft, square white bread (the Hong Kong bakery loaf or a Japanese milk loaf) gives the right crumb: soft enough to compress around the filling, tight enough not to absorb a lot of oil. Thin supermarket sandwich bread cooks through before the outside browns.

  Not savory. This is a sweet breakfast dish, so it carries no glutamate source or finishing acid. The pinch of salt in the egg and the salt in the peanut butter balance the syrup.

  Serve with Hong Kong milk tea or coffee; the bitterness of strong tea cuts the richness.
