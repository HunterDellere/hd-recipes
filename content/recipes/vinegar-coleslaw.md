steps:
  - text: 'Salt the vegetables. Toss the 450 g shredded cabbage and 80 g grated carrot with the 6 g / 1½ tsp kosher salt in a colander set over a bowl. Let stand 20 minutes. The salt draws water out of the cut surfaces by osmosis; that water would otherwise dilute the dressing and pool at the bottom of the bowl. The cabbage softens slightly at the edges but keeps its crunch.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Make the dressing. In a large bowl, whisk the 45 g / 3 tbsp apple cider vinegar, 12 g / 1 tbsp granulated sugar, 5 g / 1 tsp dijon mustard, 1 g / ½ tsp celery seed, and 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper until the sugar dissolves. Whisk in the 30 g / 2 tbsp neutral oil.'
    technique: 'vinaigrette-emulsion'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Squeeze and dress. Grab the cabbage by handfuls and squeeze firmly to press out the released liquid, then drop it into the dressing. Add the 15 g / 2 scallions and toss well. Taste a strand: it should be clearly sour, lightly sweet, and seasoned without needing more salt (the brief salting leaves about 1% behind). If it reads too sharp, add sugar by the ½ teaspoon.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Rest. Refrigerate 20 to 30 minutes, tossing once, so the dressing seasons the cabbage through. Toss again just before serving.'
    time_min: 25

notes: |
  The pre-salt is the step most slaw recipes skip, and it is why theirs are watery after an hour. Salting first means the slaw is still crunchy on day 3.

  This is a side, so there is no glutamate source on purpose: its job on the plate is acid and crunch against something rich and savory.
