source:
  name: 'Hyderabadi (Deccan) tradition'
  note: 'Hyderabad biryani comes in two methods. Kacchi (raw) layers raw marinated meat under parboiled rice and cooks both together in one sealed dum. Pakki (cooked) cooks the meat in its masala first and uses the dum to finish the rice. Kacchi is the more celebrated, but its success depends on judging the heat under a sealed pot you cannot open, and an under-cooked layer of chicken is invisible until it is served. Pakki gives the same layered, saffron-streaked result with the chicken safety and doneness settled before the lid goes on, so it is the method used here.'

steps:
  - text: 'Fry the birista. Heat the 250 ml / 1 cup neutral oil in a wide, heavy pot (the Dutch oven you will cook the biryani in works) over medium-high heat until a slice of onion sizzles briskly on contact, about 175 °C / 350 °F. Add the 600 g / 3 sliced onions; the pot will look crowded, and the onions will shrink fast. Fry, stirring every minute or two with a wooden spoon, for about 20 minutes. They soften and steam first, then turn pale gold, then brown quickly. Pull them out with a slotted spoon when they are an even deep golden brown, one shade lighter than you want, because they keep darkening off the heat. Spread them thin on a tray lined with paper towels; they crisp as they cool. Pour the oil through a sieve into a heatproof glass jar and keep it: it is now onion oil, full of flavor.'
    technique: 'deep-fry'
    time_min: 25
  - text: 'Marinate the chicken. In a large glass or ceramic dish, mix the 250 g / 1 cup whisked yogurt, 30 g / 2 tbsp ginger-garlic paste, 15 g / 3 slit green chilies, 6 g / 2½ tsp Kashmiri chili powder, 3 g / 1½ tsp ground coriander, 1 g / ¼ tsp turmeric, 5 g / 2 tsp garam masala, 12 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt, 30 ml / 2 tbsp lemon juice, the 15 g / ½ cup chopped mint, and the 15 g / ½ cup chopped cilantro. Crush 60 g / about ⅔ cup of the birista into the bowl with your hands and add 45 ml / 3 tbsp of the onion oil. Add the 1200 g chicken and rub the marinade into every piece. Cover with a fitted lid and refrigerate 2 hours to overnight. The salt and lemon season the meat to the bone; the crushed onions dissolve into the masala and give it its sweet, deep color.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Cook the chicken (pakki). Put 30 ml / 2 tbsp of the onion oil in the Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the 1200 g chicken with every bit of its marinade and spread it out. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook 20 minutes over medium-low heat, stirring once halfway, scraping the bottom with the wooden spoon so the yogurt does not catch. Uncover, raise the heat to medium, and cook 10 more minutes, stirring often, until the liquid has reduced to a thick masala that clings to the chicken and oil begins to separate at the edges. The thickest piece should read at least 74 °C / 165 °F next to the bone. Taste the masala: it should taste slightly over-salted and sharp, because the unsalted surface of the rice will draw from it during the dum. Add kosher salt by the pinch or lemon juice by the teaspoon if needed. Turn off the heat and level the chicken into an even layer.'
    time_min: 32
  - text: 'Prepare the saffron and dough. While the chicken cooks, crumble the 0.3 g saffron into the 60 ml / ¼ cup warm milk and leave it to steep to deep orange. In a small bowl, mix the 150 g / 1 cup plus 2 tbsp all-purpose flour with the 90 ml / 6 tbsp water and knead 1 minute into a soft dough; roll it into a rope long enough to circle the rim of the pot. Put the soaked rice in to drain.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Boil the rice to 70 percent. Bring the 3500 ml / 3½ quarts water to a rolling boil in a large pot with the 40 g / 4½ tbsp kosher salt, 2 g / 1 tsp shahi jeera, 5 cracked cardamom pods, 5 cloves, the 5 g / 2-inch cinnamon stick, 2 bay leaves, and 1 star anise. Taste the water: it should be noticeably salty, since the rice absorbs all its seasoning here. Add the 500 g drained basmati and stir once gently. Boil 5 minutes, then test a grain: pressed between your fingers, it should break into two or three pieces with a firm white core. That is 70 percent. If it smears, it is overcooked; if it will not break, give it 30 seconds more. Drain immediately in a large colander; leave the whole spices in the rice.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Layer. Working quickly while the rice is hot, scatter half of the drained rice over the chicken in an even layer without pressing it down. Scatter half of the remaining 90 g birista, half the 10 g / ⅓ cup mint, and half the 10 g / ⅓ cup cilantro for layering. Cover with the rest of the rice. Drizzle the 60 ml saffron milk over the top in streaks so some grains turn deep gold and others stay white, then drizzle over the 45 g / 3 tbsp melted ghee. Scatter the rest of the birista, mint, and cilantro on top.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Seal and dum. Press the 150 g / 1 cup plus 2 tbsp dough rope around the rim of the pot and press the lid down into it, so the seam is closed all the way round. Cook over medium heat 5 minutes, until you hear the bottom sizzle and see a wisp of steam at the seal. Then set a heavy flat griddle, a cast-iron skillet, or a heat diffuser under the pot, turn the burner to its lowest setting, and cook 20 minutes. The trapped steam finishes the rice and carries the masala, saffron, and onion aromas through every layer; the griddle underneath keeps the bottom from scorching. Do not open the pot.'
    technique: 'vapor-steaming'
    time_min: 25
  - text: 'Rest and serve. Turn off the heat and let the pot rest, still sealed, for 10 minutes; the grains firm up and separate. Break off the dough seal and discard it. Serve by sliding a wide spoon down the side of the pot to the bottom and lifting straight up, so each portion carries white rice, saffron rice, and masala-coated chicken from the bottom. Avoid stirring the pot, which breaks the grains and blurs the layers.'
    time_min: 12

notes: |
  Pakki or kacchi. Both are legitimate Hyderabadi methods. Kacchi biryani, with raw meat cooking under the rice, is the famous one, and when it works the meat and rice share flavor more completely. But it asks the cook to judge, without looking, whether a sealed pot on a home burner has delivered enough heat to cook a deep layer of raw chicken while not scorching the bottom or overcooking the rice above. Home burners and pots vary too much for that to be reliable on a first attempt. In the pakki method the chicken is checked with a thermometer before the rice goes on, and the dum becomes a forgiving 20-minute steam. The flavor gap is small, because the chicken still finishes under the rice and the masala still steams up through it. Once you know how your pot and burner behave, the kacchi modification is worth trying.

  Why the rice is boiled to 70 percent. The rice finishes in the steam that the masala and the saffron milk release under the sealed lid. Rice boiled fully before layering turns soft and broken in the dum; rice boiled too little stays chalky on top, where the steam is weakest. The finger test (breaks into two or three pieces, firm white core) is more reliable than a timer, because basmati varies in age and dryness.

  Why so much salt in the rice water. Basmati takes in its seasoning only while it boils, and it absorbs a small fraction of the water. The 40 g salt in 3.5 L is about 1.1 percent, which seasons the grains properly; the rice drains away the rest. Under-salted rice is the most common reason a home biryani tastes flat even when the masala is good.

  Birista and its oil. The fried onions carry much of the sweetness, color, and savory depth of a Hyderabadi biryani: they go into the marinade, between the layers, and on top. Slicing evenly matters more than anything else, since thin strands burn while thick ones are still pale. The oil left behind is onion-flavored and goes into the marinade and the pot, so nothing is wasted. Bagged fried onions work in a pinch but are often stale-tasting and too sweet.

  Umami and acid. The glutamate comes from the chicken on the bone, the yogurt, and the browned onions. The acid is the yogurt tang and the lemon in the marinade; Hyderabadi cooking leans sour on purpose to keep a rich, ghee-laced rice dish from feeling heavy. If the finished biryani tastes rich but dull, a squeeze of lemon at the table fixes it.

  Leftovers. Biryani is often better the next day. Refrigerate within an hour of serving and reheat covered, with a splash of water, until steaming throughout. Keeps 3 days refrigerated.
