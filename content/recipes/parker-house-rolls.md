before_you_start:
  - 'Plan on two days. Day one: cook the tangzhong, mix, bulk ferment, shape, and refrigerate the shaped rolls overnight, about 2.5 hours in total with 45 minutes hands-on. Day two: proof at room temperature 60 to 90 minutes, then bake 20 minutes.'
  - 'Cover the shaped pan for the overnight rest with a fitted lid, an inverted sheet pan, or a damp tea towel secured under the pan rim. Never use plastic wrap. The dough must not dry out on the surface, or it forms a skin that tears in the oven.'
  - 'On a holiday, bake the rolls in the morning, then hold them covered with a clean tea towel at room temperature. The oven is needed for the mains later. Rewarm the rolls at 175 °C / 350 °F for 5 minutes while the mains rest. The tangzhong crumb tolerates this rewarm without drying.'
  - 'Take the butter for the dough out 30 minutes ahead so it is soft and pliable. Cold butter will not knead evenly into the dough.'

steps:
  - text: 'Cook the tangzhong. In a small saucepan off the heat, whisk the 25 g / 3 tbsp bread flour into the 125 g / ½ cup whole milk until no lumps remain. Set over medium-low heat and whisk constantly. At about 65 °C the starch granules swell and gelatinize, and the liquid thickens suddenly into a paste that holds whisk trails, about 3 to 4 minutes. Scrape into a small bowl, press a piece of parchment onto the surface, and cool to below 30 °C, about 25 minutes. A hot paste would damage the yeast.'
    time_min: 30
  - text: 'Mix the dough. In a stand-mixer bowl, combine the 475 g / 3¾ cups bread flour and the 7 g / 2¼ tsp instant yeast on one side, and the 9 g / 1½ tsp fine sea salt on the other. Add the cooled tangzhong, the 40 g / 2 tbsp honey, the 200 g / ¾ cup room-temperature whole milk, and the 50 g / 1 egg. Mix on low with the dough hook until no dry flour remains, about 2 minutes, then knead on medium 6 minutes until the dough is smooth and pulls from the sides of the bowl.'
    technique: 'enriched-dough'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Knead in the butter. With the mixer on medium-low, add the 60 g / 4 tbsp softened butter one piece at a time, waiting until each disappears before adding the next. The dough will look broken and greasy for 2 to 3 minutes, then come back together. Knead 5 to 7 minutes more until the dough passes the windowpane test: a small piece stretches thin enough to see light through without tearing. Butter added after the gluten forms coats the strands and tenderizes the crumb without blocking gluten development.'
    technique: 'enriched-dough'
    time_min: 9
  - text: 'Bulk ferment. Shape the dough into a tight ball, set it seam-side down in a lightly buttered bowl, and cover with a damp tea towel. Let rise at 24 to 27 °C until doubled, 60 to 90 minutes. A floured finger pressed 1 cm into the dough should leave a dent that springs back slowly and only partway.'
    technique: 'bulk-fermentation'
    time_min: 75
  - text: 'Divide and round. Turn the dough onto an unfloured counter and press out the gas. Divide into 16 pieces of about 62 g each, using a scale so the rolls bake evenly. Cup each piece under your palm and roll it against the counter into a tight ball. Cover with the damp tea towel and rest 10 minutes so the gluten relaxes enough to roll out.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Shape the Parker House fold. Butter a 23 x 33 cm / 9 x 13-inch metal pan with 5 g / 1 tsp of the melted butter. Working one ball at a time, roll it into an oval about 10 x 7 cm and 6 mm thick. Brush the top lightly with some of the 45 g / 3 tbsp melted butter, using about 10 g / 2 tsp in total across all 16 rolls. Press a chopstick across the oval just off-center, then fold the larger half over the smaller so the top lip overhangs by about 5 mm. The butter keeps the two layers from fusing, so the baked roll opens along the fold.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Pan the rolls. Lay the rolls in the pan in 4 rows of 4, each one slightly shingled against the last with the folded edge facing the same way. Cover the pan with a fitted lid, an inverted sheet pan, or a damp tea towel tucked under the rim, and refrigerate 8 to 16 hours. The cold slows the yeast, develops flavor, and moves the bake to the morning.'
    time_min: 720
  - text: 'Final proof. Take the pan out of the fridge, keep it covered, and proof at room temperature until the rolls have risen by about half and fill the gaps between them, 60 to 90 minutes. A gently pressed roll should spring back slowly. Heat the oven to 190 °C / 375 °F with a rack in the center for the last 20 minutes of the proof.'
    technique: 'bulk-fermentation'
    time_min: 75
  - text: 'Bake. Bake 18 to 22 minutes, rotating the pan once at 10 minutes, until the tops are deep golden and an instant-read thermometer pushed into a center roll reads 88 to 93 °C / 190 to 200 °F. These enriched tops brown quickly; if the tops color too fast before the center reaches temperature, lay a sheet of foil loosely on top for the last 5 minutes.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Butter and salt. As the pan comes out, brush the tops with the remaining 30 g / 2 tbsp melted butter and sprinkle with the 3 g / ¾ tsp flaky salt. The hot crust absorbs the butter and softens. Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then lift the slab onto a wire rack. Serve warm, or hold covered with a clean tea towel up to 8 hours and rewarm at 175 °C / 350 °F for 5 minutes.'
    time_min: 10

notes: |
  Why tangzhong in a dinner roll. Holiday rolls are almost always baked hours before they are eaten. A conventional enriched roll stales noticeably within 6 hours, as the starch recrystallizes and pushes water out (retrogradation). Cooking 5% of the flour with milk to 65 °C gelatinizes that starch in advance, so it binds about five times its weight in liquid. That lets the dough carry more milk without turning sticky, and the bound water slows staling. The rolls rewarm the next day almost as soft as on the first day.

  Why the fold works. The Parker House shape is a rolled oval brushed with butter and folded off-center. The butter film keeps the two layers from fusing as they bake, so each roll has a soft seam that pulls open into two buttery leaves. Fold off-center, with the top half slightly larger, or the roll springs open in the oven and loses its shape.

  Reading doneness. Color is misleading here: honey and milk sugars brown the crust well before the center is set. Use a thermometer. At 88 to 93 °C the starch has set and the egg proteins have coagulated, so the crumb is cooked but still moist. Below 85 °C the centers are gummy, and above 95 °C they begin to dry.

  Pan choice. Metal conducts heat faster than glass or ceramic and gives evenly golden bottoms in 20 minutes. In a glass dish, reduce the oven to 175 °C / 350 °F and add 3 to 5 minutes.
