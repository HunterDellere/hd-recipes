steps:
  - text: 'Simmer the apricots. Put the 225 g / 8 oz quartered dried apricots, the 360 g / 1½ cups water, the 40 g / 3 tbsp packed light brown sugar, the 20 g / 4 tsp apple cider vinegar and the 1.5 g / ½ tsp kosher salt in a small heavy saucepan. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, stirring with a wooden spoon until the sugar dissolves. Cover, lower the heat and simmer gently 15 minutes, until the apricots are very soft and a piece crushes easily against the side of the pan. Drying concentrates the fruit sugars and acids, and simmering puts the water back so the fruit softens all the way through.'
    technique: 'simmering'
    time_min: 18
  - text: 'Reduce. Uncover and simmer 4 to 6 minutes more, stirring often, until the liquid has reduced to a thick syrup around the fruit. Watch the bottom of the pan in the last 2 minutes: the sugar can catch and scorch.'
    technique: 'reduction'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Mash and finish. Take the pan off the heat. Mash the apricots with a potato masher or a fork to a thick, slightly chunky sauce, or blend half of it for a smoother sauce. Stir in the 15 g / 1 tbsp butter and the 0.5 g / ¼ tsp black pepper until the butter melts and the sauce looks glossy.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Taste and store. It should taste sweet and fruity first, with a soft tang at the end. If it tastes only sweet, add apple cider vinegar 5 g / 1 tsp at a time. Cool it, transfer it to a glass jar and refrigerate up to 10 days. Before serving, stir in warm water 15 g / 1 tbsp at a time until it falls slowly from a spoon.'
    time_min: 4

notes: |
  Why dried apricots. Fresh apricots are out of season in November, and dried ones are the same all year. Drying also concentrates the flavor, so a short simmer gives a sauce that tastes more of apricot than most fresh fruit would.

  Why vinegar in a sweet sauce. A small amount of acid keeps the sauce from tasting like jam. Next to rich meat, sugar alone tastes heavy; sugar with a little acid tastes fresh. At 20 g in the batch, nobody tastes vinegar itself.
