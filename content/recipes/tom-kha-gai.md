steps:
  - text: 'Infuse the stock. In a 4-liter saucepan, combine the 400 g / 1⅔ cups chicken stock with the 40 g galangal coins, 60 g / 2 stalks bruised lemongrass, half of the 3 g / 8 torn lime leaves, and the 40 g smashed shallots. Bring to a simmer over medium-high heat and simmer 5 minutes. Steeping the aromatics in the thinner stock first, before the coconut fat goes in, extracts them faster, and the hard simmer is safe because there is no coconut milk yet.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Add the coconut milk. Pour in the 800 g / 2 cans coconut milk and heat over medium until small bubbles break steadily at the edges, about 90 °C. From here on, keep the soup at a gentle simmer and never let it roll; a hard boil breaks the coconut emulsion and the soup turns oily and grainy.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Poach the chicken. Add the 450 g sliced chicken thigh, 30 g / 1½ tbsp of the fish sauce, and the 8 g / 2 tsp palm sugar, and stir to separate the slices. Seasoning now lets the chicken absorb salt as it cooks. Hold at a gentle simmer 2 minutes, then add the 200 g torn oyster mushrooms and cook 3 to 4 minutes more, until the chicken is opaque through and reads 74 °C / 165 °F on an instant-read thermometer and the mushrooms are tender.'
    time_min: 6
  - text: 'Finish off the heat. Turn off the burner. Stir in the 45 g / 3 tbsp lime juice, the remaining lime leaves, and the 3 g / 4 to 6 smashed chilies. Lime juice boiled turns dull and faintly bitter, so it always goes in last, off the heat. Taste and adjust with the remaining 15 g / 1 tbsp fish sauce: the soup should taste salty and sour first, rich from the coconut, with sweetness in the background and chili heat building slowly. If it tastes flat, add fish sauce; if heavy, add 5 g / 1 tsp more lime juice.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Serve. Ladle into bowls, scatter with the 10 g / ½ cup cilantro, and, if using, drizzle about 7 g / 1½ tsp nam prik pao over each bowl. Serve with jasmine rice. The galangal, lemongrass, and lime leaves stay in the soup for aroma but are pushed aside, not eaten.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Galangal is the point. The name means boiled galangal, and 40 g is deliberately a lot. Its piney, peppery, citrusy aroma is what separates tom kha from a coconut chicken soup. Slice it thin so more surface area meets the liquid.

  Why the infusion starts in stock. The aromatic compounds in lemongrass, galangal, and lime leaf are only partly fat-soluble. A short hard simmer in stock pulls them out quickly. Coconut milk cannot take that boil, so it goes in second and is held gently.

  Keeping the coconut smooth. Coconut milk is an emulsion of fat in water held together by coconut proteins. A rolling boil splits it, and the soup turns greasy with grainy flecks. For curries, Thai cooks crack coconut cream on purpose to fry paste in the released oil; for tom kha the goal is the opposite, a creamy, unbroken broth.

  Balance. Tom kha is gentler than tom yum: less chili, less sour, with coconut richness in front. Fish sauce provides salt and glutamate, mushrooms add more, and lime is the finishing acid. Season fish sauce in two stages (with the chicken, then to taste at the end) because the chicken and mushrooms absorb salt as they cook.

  Seasonality. Galangal, lemongrass, and makrut lime leaves are sold fresh or frozen year-round at Thai and Southeast Asian grocers in US cities. Buy extra and freeze them; all three keep for months in the freezer.
