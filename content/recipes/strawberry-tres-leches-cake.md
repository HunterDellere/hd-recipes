steps:
  - text: 'Make the compote. Combine the 450 g / 16 oz frozen strawberries, 50 g / ¼ cup compote sugar and the 0.5 g pinch fine sea salt in a wide saucepan over medium heat. Cook, stirring with a wooden spoon and crushing the berries as they soften, until the mixture is thick and jammy and a spoon drawn across the bottom leaves a clear trail for 2 seconds, 15 to 20 minutes. It should reduce to about 300 g / 1 cup. Stir in the 15 g / 1 tbsp lemon juice, cool it, and refrigerate it covered. Frozen strawberries are picked ripe and frozen within hours, so cooking off their water concentrates more flavor than winter fresh berries have.'
    technique: 'reduction'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Prepare the pan. Heat the oven to 175 °C / 350 °F with a rack in the center. Rub a 23 x 33 cm / 9 x 13 inch glass or ceramic baking dish with the 10 g / 2 tsp softened butter and dust it with a spoonful of the 140 g flour, tapping out the excess. The cake is served from this dish, so do not use parchment.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Mix the dry ingredients. Whisk the 140 g / 1 cup plus 2 tbsp flour, 6 g / 1½ tsp baking powder and 2 g / ⅓ tsp fine sea salt together for 20 seconds.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Whip the yolks. In a large bowl, beat the 5 egg yolks (from the 250 g / 5 eggs) with 150 g / ¾ cup of the granulated sugar on medium-high until thick and pale, and a ribbon falling from the beaters sits on the surface for 2 seconds, about 4 minutes. Beat in the 80 g / ⅓ cup batter milk and the 8 g / 1½ tsp vanilla bean paste on low.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Whip the whites. In a clean, grease-free bowl with clean beaters, beat the 5 egg whites on medium until foamy, then add the remaining 50 g / ¼ cup granulated sugar slowly and beat on high to firm, glossy peaks that curl over at the tip, 3 to 4 minutes. Stop before they look dry and grainy.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Fold. Sift the flour mixture over the yolk mixture in two additions, folding with a large spatula just until no dry streaks remain. Stir in one third of the whites to loosen the batter, then fold in the rest in two additions until no white streaks remain. Spread the batter evenly into the dish.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Bake. Bake 25 to 30 minutes, until the top is golden, springs back when pressed, and a skewer in the center comes out clean. With no butter in the batter, the crumb is a dry, open network that absorbs the soak without collapsing. Cool the cake in the dish on a rack 30 minutes, until just warm.'
    time_min: 28
  - text: 'Soak. Whisk the 354 ml / 12 oz can evaporated milk, the 397 g / 14 oz can sweetened condensed milk, the 240 g / 1 cup soak whole milk and the 1 g pinch fine sea salt in a jug. Pierce the warm cake all over with a skewer every 1 to 2 cm, all the way to the bottom. Pour about one third of the soak slowly and evenly over the surface, wait 5 minutes, and repeat twice. It looks like too much liquid at first; the cake absorbs nearly all of it in the fridge.'
    time_min: 17
  - text: 'Chill. Cover the dish with a fitted lid or a sheet pan set on top and refrigerate at least 8 hours, ideally overnight, so the milk spreads evenly through the crumb.'
    time_min: 480
  - text: 'Add the compote (up to 6 hours before serving). Spread the cold 300 g / 1 cup compote evenly over the soaked cake with an offset spatula or the back of a spoon, right to the edges.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Whip the strawberry cream. Grind the 20 g / 1 cup freeze-dried strawberries to a fine powder in a spice grinder or blender, then sift out the seeds. In a chilled bowl, whip the 360 g / 1½ cups cold heavy cream, the 120 g / ½ cup cold crème fraîche, the 25 g / 2 tbsp topping sugar and all but 2 g / 1 tsp of the powder to soft, smooth peaks, about 2 minutes on medium-high. The dry powder flavors and colors the cream without adding water, and the acid in the crème fraîche helps the foam hold for the afternoon. Spread it gently over the compote and refrigerate until serving.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Serve. Just before serving, dust the last 2 g / 1 tsp strawberry powder over the top and arrange the 200 g / 10 halved fresh strawberries, if using. Cut into squares straight from the fridge and lift them out with a flat spatula. Taste a corner piece: milky and sweet, then sharp strawberry, then the tang of the cream.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  Why strawberries are not in the soak. Strawberries are acidic. Blended into the milk soak, the acid can curdle the milk proteins overnight, and the cake turns a grayish pink. A compote layer on top keeps the soak smooth and the color clean.

  Why compote and powder instead of fresh berries. Winter strawberries are bred to ship well and are picked underripe, so they have little sugar or aroma. Frozen berries are picked ripe; cooking off their water concentrates them. Freeze-dried berries have had nearly all their water removed, so they add flavor and color to the cream without thinning it.

  Why the compote goes on the cold cake on the day. A compote spread on the cake the night before would soak into the top and blur the layers. Cold compote on cold cake stays a distinct layer for several hours.
