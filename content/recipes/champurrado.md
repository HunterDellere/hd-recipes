steps:
  - text: 'In a heavy saucepan, combine the 500 ml / 2 cups water, the 360 ml / 1½ cups whole milk, the 5 g / one canela stick, the 90 g / about half a cone of piloncillo and the 1 g / pinch fine sea salt. Bring to a bare simmer over medium heat, stirring with a wooden spoon until the piloncillo dissolves, then lower the heat and let the canela steep for 10 minutes. Ceylon cinnamon gives up its flavor gently; a brief steep at a low simmer gets the floral, citrusy notes without the woody bitterness of a long boil.'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'While it steeps, whisk the 50 g / ⅓ cup plus 1 tbsp masa harina into the 240 ml / 1 cup cold water in a bowl until completely smooth, with no dry pockets. Masa starch must be dispersed in cold liquid first: dropped straight into hot liquid, the outside of each clump gelatinizes and seals dry flour inside, and you get lumps that never dissolve.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Remove the canela stick. Add the 90 g / 3.2 oz chopped Mexican chocolate and whisk until it melts, about 2 minutes.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Whisk the masa slurry again (the masa settles quickly), then pour it into the pot through a fine sieve while whisking constantly. Raise the heat to medium and cook, whisking steadily and scraping the bottom and corners of the pan, until it comes back to a simmer and thickens, then lower the heat and simmer gently for 8 to 10 minutes more. Whisk or spin a molinillo between your palms the whole time: the starch thickens fastest on the hot bottom of the pan and will scorch there if it sits. The starch gelatinizes at around 70 °C / 158 °F, but it needs the full simmer to lose its raw corn taste and reach full body.'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Check the texture: it should coat the back of a spoon like heavy cream and pour slowly from a ladle. If it is too thick, whisk in hot water about 30 ml / 2 tbsp at a time; if too thin, simmer a few minutes longer. Taste and adjust: it should taste of chocolate and toasted corn with a light cinnamon finish, and the pinch of salt should keep the sweetness from cloying. If you want it sweeter, dissolve in more grated piloncillo now, about 10 g / 1 tbsp at a time.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Just before serving, whisk hard or spin the molinillo for 30 seconds to raise a light foam on top, then ladle into mugs. Serve hot, with tamales, pan dulce or churros.'
    time_min: 1

notes: |
  What makes it champurrado and not hot chocolate. The body comes from nixtamalized corn: masa harina is corn that has been cooked in alkaline limewater and ground, and its starch thickens the liquid while adding a toasted, slightly mineral corn flavor. Cornstarch would thicken it but tastes of nothing, and the result is a chocolate pudding drink rather than an atole.

  Sweetness and salt. This is a sweet drink, so there is no glutamate or acid here; the balance comes from the bitterness of the cacao, the corn, and a pinch of salt. Mexican chocolate tablets vary a lot in sweetness (Abuelita and Ibarra are much sweeter than stone-ground Taza), which is why the piloncillo is adjusted at the end rather than all added up front.

  Reheating. Champurrado thickens as it cools and sets almost to a pudding in the refrigerator. Reheat it gently in a saucepan with a splash of water, whisking until smooth. It keeps for 2 days, covered, in a glass jar.
