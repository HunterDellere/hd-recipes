  note: 'The Maryland standard is defined by what it leaves out: no bell pepper, no onion, no panko crust, no heavy breading. The binder exists only to keep the lumps from falling apart, and a good crab cake should look like crab pressed together rather than a fritter with crab in it.'

before_you_start:
  - 'The formed cakes need 30 minutes in the refrigerator (up to 8 hours) before they hit the pan. The chill firms the mayonnaise fat and lets the saltines hydrate into a glue; an unchilled cake slumps and sheds lumps when flipped.'
  - 'Make the tartar sauce and the vinegar slaw first. Both improve after 30 minutes of rest, which is exactly how long the cakes chill.'

steps:
  - text: 'Pick the crab. Spread the 450 g / 1 lb crab meat in a single layer on a sheet pan. Run your fingertips lightly over it to find shell and cartilage; tilt the pan under a light and the shell shards catch the glare. Lift them out rather than stirring the meat; every stir breaks a lump, and the lumps are what you are paying for.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Build the binder. In a large bowl, whisk the 50 g / 1 egg, 45 g / 3 tbsp mayonnaise, 8 g / 1½ tsp dijon mustard, 5 g / 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce, 5 g / 1¼ tsp Old Bay seasoning, 5 g / 1 tsp lemon juice, and 5 g / 2 tbsp parsley until smooth. Stir in the 24 g / 8 crushed saltines and let it sit 2 minutes so the crackers start to hydrate. No added salt: the crab, the seasoning, the Worcestershire, and the crackers already bring about 1% salt to the mix, which is the target.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Fold. Tip the 450 g crab onto the binder. Fold with a flexible spatula, scooping from the bottom and turning the mass over itself, 6 to 8 strokes, until the binder coats the lumps and no dry crumbs remain. Stop there. The mixture will look barely held together; that is correct.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Form and chill. Divide into 4 portions of about 140 g. Cup each in your palms and press gently into a cake 7 cm across and 3 cm tall; pack the edges more firmly than the center, since the edges take the spatula on the flip. Set on a parchment-lined plate, cover, and refrigerate at least 30 minutes.'
    time_min: 33
  - text: 'Sear. Heat a 30 cm stainless or cast-iron skillet over medium heat for 2 minutes. Add the 15 g / 1 tbsp neutral oil and the 28 g / 2 tbsp butter. When the butter foam subsides and the milk solids just begin to tan, lay in the cold cakes with space between them. Cook undisturbed 4 minutes, until the underside is deep golden. The oil raises the smoke point so the butter can brown the crust without scorching across the full cook.'
    technique: 'shallow-frying'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Flip and finish. Slide a thin fish spatula fully under each cake and turn it in one motion, steadying the top with your other hand. Cook another 3 to 4 minutes, basting the tops once or twice with the browned fat. The center should read 71 °C / 160 °F on an instant-read thermometer, the point at which the binder sets. If the crust is done before the center is, move the pan to a 200 °C / 400 °F oven for 4 to 5 minutes.'
    technique: 'shallow-frying'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Rest and serve. Drain the cakes 1 minute on a rack (paper towels steam the bottom crust soft). Serve with the 4 lemon wedges, tartar sauce, and vinegar slaw. Squeeze the lemon at the table, not before; acid on a hot crust softens it within a minute.'
    time_min: 1

notes: |
  Crab cakes fail in one of two directions: too much filler, where the cake eats like a bread fritter, or too little chill, where it falls apart in the pan. This ratio (about 75 g of binder and crackers per 450 g of crab) sits at the low edge of what will hold, and the 30-minute chill is what makes that possible.

  Do not add onion, celery, or bell pepper. They are the most common drift from the Maryland standard; they release water during the sear, loosen the cake, and pull attention away from the crab.

  Pasteurized shelf-stable crab (the cans in the tuna aisle) is not a substitute. Pasteurization tightens and shreds the meat, and the cake loses its lumps.

  Recommended plate: one cake per person, tartar sauce, vinegar slaw, and sweet corn on the cob in season. Sliced ripe tomatoes with flaky salt work in late summer.
