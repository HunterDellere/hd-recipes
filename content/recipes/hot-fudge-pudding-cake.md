steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 350°F (177°C) with a rack centered. Melt the unsalted butter (3 tbsp / 45 g) in an ungreased 8-inch square baking dish in the oven as it heats, 3 to 4 minutes, then pour it into a medium bowl, leaving a film in the dish. Whisk in the whole milk (½ cup / 120 g) and vanilla bean paste (1 tsp / 5 g).'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'In a second bowl, whisk the all-purpose flour (1 cup / 125 g), granulated sugar (¾ cup / 150 g), Dutch-process cocoa (¼ cup / 22 g), baking powder (2 tsp / 8 g), and fine sea salt (¼ tsp / 1.5 g). Add the milk mixture and stir until no dry flour remains, then fold in the chopped dark chocolate (2 oz / 55 g). The batter is thick, more like brownie batter than cake batter; that density is what lets it rise up through the sauce instead of dissolving into it.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Spread the batter evenly in the buttered dish. In a small bowl, mix the dark brown sugar (scant 1 cup / 200 g), Dutch-process cocoa (¼ cup / 22 g), and fine sea salt (⅛ tsp / 1 g), breaking up lumps, and scatter it evenly over the batter.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Pour the hot water or hot brewed coffee (1½ cups / 360 g) slowly over the back of a spoon so it spreads across the whole surface. Do not stir. It will look wrong, like batter drowning in a puddle; that is correct. The hot liquid dissolves the sugar and blooms the cocoa into a dense syrup on top.'
    technique: 'blooming-cocoa'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Bake 32 to 38 minutes, until the top is set, cracked, and dry-looking, and dark sauce bubbles up at the edges and through the cracks. As the 2 tsp / 8 g baking powder aerates the batter it becomes lighter than the sugar syrup and rises through it; the syrup sinks to the bottom, picks up starch and cocoa from the underside of the cake, and thickens into fudge sauce. A tester will not come out clean because of the sauce; press the cake surface instead, which should spring back.'
    time_min: 35
  - text: 'Rest 15 minutes before serving; the sauce thickens as it cools from bubbling. Spoon into bowls, cake side up, with plenty of sauce from the bottom. A splash of cold whole milk (2 tbsp / 30 g per bowl) poured over, or a scoop of ice cream, is the classic finish.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'The Adjustment: if the sauce is thin and watery, it was underbaked or served too hot; bake 3 to 5 minutes longer next time and let it rest the full 15 minutes. If there is barely any sauce, it was overbaked and the cake absorbed it; pull it earlier. If it reads too sweet, use coffee rather than water and add a second ⅛ tsp / 1 g fine sea salt to the topping.'

notes: |
  How it sauces itself. The order matters: thick batter, dry sugar-cocoa layer, hot liquid on top. The water dissolves the sugar into a syrup denser than the batter will be once it starts to rise. In the oven, baking powder releases gas and the batter''s density drops below the syrup''s, so the cake floats up and the syrup trades places with it. At the bottom the syrup reduces and absorbs starch from the cake''s underside, which thickens it into a sauce. Stirring at any point breaks the layering and you get a soggy cake with no sauce.

  Why dark brown sugar on top. The topping becomes the sauce, and dark brown sugar''s molasses gives it a deeper, more caramel fudge flavor than granulated would. The pinch of salt in the topping keeps the sauce from tasting flat-sweet.

  Serve warm. The sauce is best warm and pourable. Leftovers reheat well in the microwave in 30-second bursts; the cake reabsorbs some sauce overnight, so add a spoonful of water before reheating.
