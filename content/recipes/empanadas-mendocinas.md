before_you_start:
  - 'Make the filling the day before (or at least 4 hours ahead) and refrigerate it. Cold filling with set fat is easy to spoon and does not soak through the dough; the fat melts in the oven and makes the inside juicy.'
  - 'Put the beef in the freezer for 30 minutes before dicing. Firm meat cuts into clean 5 mm cubes instead of smearing.'
  - 'The dough rests 30 minutes. Make it while the oven preheats on the day you bake, or a day ahead (it keeps 2 days refrigerated, wrapped in beeswax wrap or in a lidded glass container).'

steps:
  - text: 'Hard-boil the eggs. Lower the 3 eggs (about 150 g once peeled) into a pot of boiling water and boil 10 minutes, then cool them in a bowl of cold water, 5 minutes. Peel, chop into 1 cm pieces, and refrigerate in a lidded glass container.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Dice the beef. Take the 600 g beef chuck from the freezer after 30 minutes, when it is firm but not frozen through. Trim off silverskin and the thickest fat seams, then cut into 5 mm slices, 5 mm strips, and 5 mm dice. Hand-cut beef keeps its juice inside each cube; ground beef releases it into the pan and turns grainy.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Cook the onions. Melt the 40 g / 3 tbsp lard in a wide skillet over medium heat. Add the 600 g diced onions and 3 g / ¾ tsp of the kosher salt; the salt draws out water so they soften without browning. Cook, stirring with a wooden spoon, for 12 to 15 minutes until soft, sweet, and translucent with no color. The onion is what keeps the filling moist, so it should be fully soft before the beef goes in.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Cook the beef. Turn the heat to medium-high, push the onions to the edges, and add the 600 g diced beef with another 3 g / ¾ tsp kosher salt. Stir and cook only until the cubes are no longer pink on the outside and are releasing juice, 4 to 5 minutes. The beef will finish cooking in the oven; cooking it hard here dries it out.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Season off the heat. Take the pan off the burner and stir in the 8 g / 1 tbsp sweet paprika, 4 g / 2 tsp cumin, 2 g / 1 tsp red pepper flakes, 1 g / 1 tsp oregano, and 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper. The residual heat blooms the spices in the fat; paprika burns and turns bitter over direct heat. Taste and add up to the remaining 2 g / ½ tsp kosher salt. The filling should taste slightly too salty and too spiced while warm, because cold filling inside pastry reads milder.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Chill. Spread the filling in a glass dish, cool to room temperature, cover with a fitted lid or beeswax wrap, and refrigerate at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. It should be firm, with the fat set white around the meat.'
    time_min: 240
  - text: 'Make the dough. Whisk the 10 g / 1¾ tsp fine sea salt into the 230 ml hot water, then add the 100 g lard and stir until it melts. Pour this over the 500 g flour in a large bowl and mix with a wooden spoon, then knead on the counter for 5 minutes until smooth and soft, like an earlobe. Cover with a damp kitchen towel and rest 30 minutes so the gluten relaxes and the dough rolls thin without shrinking back.'
    time_min: 40
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 230 °C / 450 °F with a rack in the upper third. Line two sheet pans with parchment.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Roll and cut. Roll the dough on a lightly floured counter to 2.5 mm thick (about the thickness of a coin stack of two). Cut 13 cm / 5 inch circles with a small plate or bowl as a guide. Gather the scraps, rest them 10 minutes, and roll once more; you should get about 20 discs. Stack them with parchment between so they do not stick.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Fill. Stir the chilled filling to loosen it. Put about 40 g / a heaped tablespoon of filling on the center of each disc, leaving a 1.5 cm border. Add a few pieces of the 150 g chopped egg and one olive half (from the 80 g olives) on top. Do not overfill; a fat empanada will not close.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Seal. Dip a finger in the 30 ml / 2 tbsp cold water and moisten the border of a disc. Fold it in half over the filling, press the edges together from one end to the other, pushing out air as you go, so the two layers fuse into a single flat 1.5 cm rim.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Crimp with the repulgue. Hold the empanada in your left hand with the curved rim facing you (reverse hands if left-handed). Starting at the right corner, pinch the very edge of the rim between your right thumb and index finger, pull it out slightly, and fold it down and over onto the rim at an angle, pressing it flat with your thumb. Move 1 cm along and repeat: each new fold starts just under and overlaps the previous one, so the rim becomes a twisted rope. Work around to the other corner (about 12 to 14 folds) and press the last fold flat. The braid seals far better than a fork crimp, which keeps the juice inside while baking. If the repulgue is not working, fold the corners in and crimp with a fork; it bakes the same.'
    time_min: 25
  - text: 'Egg wash and bake. Beat the 1 egg yolk with 15 ml / 1 tbsp of the cold water and brush it over the tops (not the rims, where it glues the folds shut and burns). Set the empanadas 3 cm apart on the pans and bake one pan at a time for 14 to 16 minutes, until deep golden with small blisters. The filling must be bubbling hot; if you probe one, it should read above 74 °C / 165 °F.'
    time_min: 32
  - text: 'Rest and serve. Let them rest on the pan 5 minutes; the filling is molten. Eat by hand, bite-side up so the juice stays in, with chimichurri on the side if you like.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  Why so much onion. Mendoza empanadas use at least as much onion as beef. Cooked slowly until soft, the onion adds sweetness and holds water, so the filling stays juicy inside the pastry; it also stretches expensive beef. Undercooked onion stays crunchy and sharp, so give it the full time before the beef goes in.

  Why the filling is chilled. Warm filling melts the lard in the dough as you shape, so the dough tears and leaks. Cold filling with the fat set keeps the dough firm, and the set fat turns back into juice in the oven. This is the single most important step after the onion.

  Umami and acid. The browned beef, olives, and egg give the filling its savory depth; the tradition does not add stock cubes or sauces. There is no acid inside the empanada because a wet, sour filling would soak the dough. Acid comes from the side: chimichurri, a squeeze of lemon (common in the north of Argentina), or a glass of Malbec, which is what Mendoza drinks with them.

  On the dough. Hot water and melted lard make a tender, slightly flaky dough that is easy to fold and crimp. Bake hot and high in the oven so the pastry sets and blisters before the filling boils through.

  Olive pits. Traditional Mendoza cooks often add a whole olive, pit included. Pitted halves are used here; if you use whole olives with pits, tell your guests.
