steps:
  - text: 'Grind the whole spices. Put the 6 g / 1 tbsp celery seed, 1 g / 5 bay leaves (torn, ribs removed), 3 g / 1 tsp black peppercorns, 2 g / ½ tsp mustard seeds, 0.5 g / 4 allspice berries, 0.3 g / 3 cloves, and 0.3 g cardamom seeds in a blade spice grinder. Pulse to a fine powder, about 30 seconds, shaking the grinder between pulses. Do not toast them first: this style of blend tastes of raw, sharp celery seed and green bay, and toasting rounds them toward curry.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Sift out the bay. Tip the ground spices through a fine sieve into a bowl and regrind anything left behind (the leathery bay fragments are usually what stays). Fragments of bay are unpleasant to bite and do not soften in a crab cake.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Blend. Add the 18 g / 1 tbsp fine sea salt, 9 g / 1 tbsp + 1 tsp sweet paprika, 1 g / ½ tsp cayenne, 0.5 g / ¼ tsp ground ginger, 0.3 g / ⅛ tsp mace, and 0.2 g pinch of cinnamon. Whisk until uniform in color. Taste a pinch on a cracker: celery seed and bay first, then pepper heat, then a faint warm-spice finish. If cloves or cinnamon jump out, the blend is off balance; make the next batch with less.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Store. Transfer to a small airtight jar and keep away from light and heat.'
    time_min: 1

notes: |
  The blend is about 40% salt by weight, close to the commercial style, so recipes written for the store-bought tin work 1:1.

  Use: crab cakes, steamed crabs, shrimp boils, tartar sauce, fries, buttered corn, deviled eggs, and Bloody Marys.

  Shelf life: about 3 months at full strength. After that the celery seed and bay fade first and the paprika takes over; grind a new batch rather than adding more.
