steps:
  - text: 'Put the 200 g / about 10 chopped Medjool dates in a heatproof bowl, pour over the 250 ml / 1 cup boiling water, and stir in the 5 g / 1 tsp baking soda; it will foam briefly. Leave for 20 minutes. The alkaline water breaks down the pectin in the date skins and flesh so they melt into the batter, and it darkens the sponge. Then mash to a rough purée with a fork, leaving some small pieces.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'While the dates soak, heat the oven to 180 °C / 350 °F with a rack in the middle. Rub a 20 cm / 8-inch square metal or glass baking pan with a little of the softened butter and line the base with parchment.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Beat the 85 g / 6 tbsp softened butter with the 175 g / ¾ cup dark muscovado sugar using a hand mixer or a wooden spoon until lighter in color and fluffy, about 3 minutes with a mixer. Muscovado is damp and clumpy; press out any lumps against the side of the bowl as you go.'
    technique: 'creaming-butter-sugar'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Beat in the 100 g / 2 eggs one at a time, then the 5 ml / 1 tsp vanilla extract. If the mixture looks curdled, add a spoonful of the flour from the next step; it will come back together once all the flour is in.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Whisk the 175 g / 1⅓ cups all-purpose flour, the 8 g / 2 tsp baking powder and the 2 g / ⅓ tsp fine sea salt together in a separate bowl. Fold it into the butter mixture with a wooden spoon or metal spoon until just combined, then fold in the mashed dates and all their soaking liquid. The batter will be loose and wet, closer to a thick pancake batter than a cake batter; that is correct, and it is what keeps the pudding moist.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Scrape the batter into the pan and bake for 30 to 35 minutes, until the top springs back when pressed and a skewer in the center comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs.'
    time_min: 33
  - text: 'While the sponge bakes, make the toffee sauce. In a heavy saucepan over medium heat, melt the 115 g / 1 stick butter with the 175 g / ¾ cup dark muscovado sugar, stirring with a wooden spoon until the sugar dissolves, about 3 minutes. Pour in the 300 ml / 1¼ cups heavy cream (it will bubble up), bring to a boil and boil gently for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until the sauce is glossy and coats the back of the spoon. Stir in the 2 g / ½ tsp flaky salt off the heat and taste: it should taste of dark caramel with a clear salty edge, not just sweet. Keep warm.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'As soon as the pudding comes out of the oven, poke it all over with a skewer, about every 2 cm. Pour about one third of the hot toffee sauce, roughly 170 ml / ⅔ cup, evenly over the top and return the pan to the oven for 5 minutes, so the sauce bubbles and sinks into the sponge and the surface turns sticky.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Cut into 9 squares and serve warm, with the remaining toffee sauce, rewarmed if needed, spooned over each portion and the 240 ml / 1 cup cold heavy cream poured alongside. The contrast of hot sauce and cold cream is part of the dish.'
    time_min: 3

notes: |
  What the baking soda does. Soaking the dates in boiling water with baking soda raises the pH, which softens the fruit quickly so it melts into the batter, and speeds Maillard browning, which is why the sponge bakes up so dark. The soda also neutralizes some acidity in the dates and muscovado, and reacts in the oven to add lift alongside the baking powder. Do not increase it: more soda gives a soapy taste and a greenish cast.

  Why muscovado. Dark muscovado keeps most of its molasses, so it brings a deeper, slightly bitter, licorice-like flavor than American dark brown sugar, and that bitterness keeps a very sweet dessert from tasting flat. Salt does the same job in the sauce; taste it after the flaky salt goes in.

  Why the second bake. Pouring sauce over the hot sponge and giving it 5 more minutes in the oven lets the top absorb the sauce and set into a thin sticky layer. Pouring it all on at once instead drowns the sponge; most of the sauce belongs at the table.
