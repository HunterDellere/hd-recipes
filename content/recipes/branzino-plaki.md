source:
  name: 'Adapted from the Greek psari plaki tradition'
  note: 'Plaki is a lathera-family dish: the vegetables are the body of it, cooked in generous olive oil, and the fish is laid on top to bake briefly. Traditional versions use whole fish or thick cod steaks; fillets keep it to weeknight length. The divergence from many home versions is cooking the sauce down first on the stove. Raw tomato poured into a baking dish releases its water around the fish, which then poaches in a thin, pink liquid; a reduced sauce stays thick and lets the fish skin stay above it.'

steps:
  - text: 'Salt the fish. Season the 700 g / 4 fillets on both sides with the 7 g / 2 tsp kosher salt for the fish and refrigerate, skin-side up and uncovered, while the sauce cooks. Heat the oven to 200 C / 400 F.'
    technique: 'dry-brining'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Sweat the onions. In a 30 cm / 12 in oven-safe skillet or braiser, warm the 80 g / ⅓ cup olive oil over medium. Add the 300 g / 2 sliced onions with the 6 g / 2 tsp kosher salt for the sauce. Cook, stirring occasionally, 12 to 15 minutes until soft, translucent, and just turning gold at the edges. The salt draws water out so the onions soften rather than fry; this sweetness is the backbone of plaki.'
    technique: 'sweating'
    time_min: 14
  - text: 'Bloom the garlic and paste. Add the 20 g / 5 sliced garlic cloves and cook 1 minute. Push the onions aside, add the 30 g / 2 tbsp tomato paste to the bare oil, and fry 2 minutes until it darkens from red to brick. Frying the paste caramelizes its sugars and deepens its glutamate.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Deglaze and reduce. Pour in the 120 ml / ½ cup white wine, scraping the pan, and boil 2 minutes until the alcohol smell fades. Add the 400 g / 1 can crushed tomatoes, 2 g / 2 tsp rigani, 1 bay leaf, and 1 g / ½ tsp pepper flakes. Simmer 10 to 12 minutes until thick enough that a spoon dragged through leaves a clear trail. Taste: if sharp, stir in the 2 g / ½ tsp sugar. The sauce should be fully seasoned now; the fish will not add salt to it.'
    technique: 'reduction'
    time_min: 13
  - text: 'Bake the fish. Pat the fillets dry. Nestle them into the sauce skin-side up, pressing so the flesh sits in the sauce but the skin stays above it. Bake uncovered 10 to 12 minutes, until the flesh is opaque and reads 55 C / 131 F at the thickest point. The fillets cook from below in the simmering sauce and from above in dry heat, so the skin firms instead of going slack.'
    time_min: 11
  - text: 'Finish. Squeeze the juice of half the 100 g lemon over the pan, drizzle the 14 g / 1 tbsp finishing olive oil, and scatter the 15 g / ½ cup parsley. Discard the bay leaf. Taste the sauce with a flake of fish; add lemon if it reads heavy. Serve with the remaining lemon wedges and bread for the sauce.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Why reduce the sauce first. Fish fillets cook in 10 to 12 minutes; tomatoes need 20 or more to lose their raw edge and concentrate. If you build the dish raw in the baking dish, you either overcook the fish waiting for the sauce or serve a thin, acidic sauce. Cooking the sauce down to a spoon-trail consistency first decouples the two timelines.

  Plaki as lathera. The olive oil quantity is not a mistake. Lathera dishes are defined by generous oil that emulsifies into the tomato as it reduces, carrying the fat-soluble lycopene and the oregano aroma. Cutting the oil gives you a lean, sharp tomato sauce, which is a different dish.

  Umami and acid. Fried tomato paste and reduced tomato carry the glutamate; the wine and fresh lemon carry the acid. The lemon goes on at the end for the same reason as in any Greek fish dish: cooked lemon goes flat.
