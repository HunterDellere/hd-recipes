steps:
  - text: 'Brine-soak. Dissolve the 15 g / 1½ tbsp kosher salt for the soak in the 2000 ml / 8 cups cold soaking water in a large bowl. Add the 500 g rinsed kidney beans, cover with a plate, and leave at room temperature 8 to 24 hours. Sodium ions in the brine replace some of the calcium and magnesium that hold the pectin in the bean skins together, so the skins soften and stay whole instead of splitting while the inside cooks.'
    time_min: 480
  - text: 'Drain and rinse. Drain the beans, discard the soaking water, and rinse them well. They should have roughly doubled in size with no wrinkles; a few that stay small and hard are old beans and can be picked out.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Boil hard for 10 minutes. Put the beans in a heavy 5 L pot with the 2000 ml / 8 cups cold cooking water, the 6 g / 1½ tsp kosher salt for cooking, the 200 g halved onion, the 12 g / 3 smashed garlic cloves, the 2 bay leaves, and the 5 g kombu. Bring to a full rolling boil over high heat and keep it there for 10 minutes, skimming off the foam. This step is not optional: it is what destroys the phytohaemagglutinin in kidney beans, and a gentle simmer does not reach it reliably.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Simmer gently. Lower the heat until only a few small bubbles break the surface, partly cover with the lid ajar, and cook 45 to 90 minutes, stirring gently every 20 minutes and topping up with hot water so the beans stay covered by at least 2 cm. A hard boil past the first 10 minutes tumbles the beans and splits their skins. Fresh beans finish in about 45 minutes; beans that have sat in the pantry for a year or more can take twice as long.'
    time_min: 75
  - text: 'Check for doneness. Taste five beans from different parts of the pot. All five should be creamy all the way through with no chalky center, and their skins should be tender. If even one is firm, keep simmering and check again in 10 minutes. Taste the liquid too; it should be pleasantly seasoned, like a light broth.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Cool in the liquid. Take the pot off the heat and discard the onion, the 2 bay leaves, and the kombu. Let the beans cool in their liquid for at least 30 minutes; they keep absorbing seasoning and firm up slightly, which helps them hold together in a stew. Refrigerate within 2 hours, still in their liquid.'
    time_min: 30

notes: |
  The toxin, and why the boil matters. Red kidney beans contain phytohaemagglutinin, a lectin that binds to the lining of the gut and causes severe nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea within one to three hours. As few as four or five raw or undercooked beans can cause symptoms. Boiling at 100 °C for 10 minutes destroys it. Heating to only about 80 °C, as a slow cooker on low can do, does not, and has been reported to increase its activity. White kidney beans (cannellini) contain it too, at lower levels. Pinto and black beans contain much less.

  Salt in the soak. The idea that salt keeps beans hard is a myth at these amounts. Brining softens the skins (sodium replaces calcium and magnesium in the pectin of the skin), so the beans cook more evenly and fewer burst. What does keep beans firm is acid and sugar: tomatoes, vinegar, and molasses slow softening, which is why they go in after the beans are cooked.

  If the beans will not soften. Very old beans and hard water are the usual causes. Add 0.5 g / ⅛ tsp baking soda per liter of cooking water; the alkaline water speeds the breakdown of pectin. Use no more than that, or the beans turn mushy and taste soapy.

  Using and storing. Keep the beans in their liquid: it stops them drying out and the liquid itself is a seasoned stock for chili, soup, or rice. They keep 5 days refrigerated, or 6 months frozen in portions of 250 g / about 1½ cups of beans with enough liquid to cover. About 220 g dried beans gives 500 g cooked, the amount in a pot of chili.
