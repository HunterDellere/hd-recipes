steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 220 C / 425 F with a rack in the center. Choose a metal roasting pan or rimmed sheet pan large enough to hold the wedges in one layer with a little space (about 33 x 23 cm / 13 x 9 in minimum). Glass and ceramic insulate the bottom and the frying phase never happens.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Season the potatoes. In the pan, toss the 1200 g potato wedges with the 12 g / 4 tsp kosher salt, the 1 g / ½ tsp pepper, and the 3 g / 1 tbsp rigani. Arrange them cut-side down, skin-backs up, in a single layer.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Pour in the bath. Whisk the 240 ml / 1 cup chicken stock, 80 g / ⅓ cup olive oil, 45 g / 3 tbsp lemon juice for the bath, and 20 g / 5 grated garlic cloves, and pour it around (not over) the potatoes. The liquid should come about a third of the way up the wedges. Measured against 1.2 kg of potatoes, this volume evaporates in about 40 minutes, leaving only oil.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Roast, phase one: absorb. Roast 35 to 40 minutes without touching. The stock simmers, the starch at the potato surface gelatinizes in seasoned liquid, and the wedges drink the seasoned lemon-garlic bath to the center. You are done with this phase when the liquid has disappeared and the oil is audibly sizzling.'
    time_min: 38
  - text: 'Roast, phase two: fry. Flip each wedge with a thin metal spatula, scraping up the stuck crust (it is the best part), so the second cut face lies in the oil. Roast another 15 to 20 minutes until both faces are deep gold and the edges crisp. If the liquid had not fully evaporated, roast until it has before flipping; a wet flip steams instead of frying.'
    time_min: 18
  - text: 'Finish raw. Out of the oven, zest the 100 g / 1 finishing lemon directly over the pan, squeeze over its juice, and scatter the 8 g / ¼ cup parsley. Toss gently. Taste a wedge from the center: it should be lemony through, savory from the stock, and crisp-edged. Add the 1 g pinch of flaky salt only if it needs it.'
    time_min: 3

notes: |
  How the two-phase roast works. Greek lemon potatoes are a braise that turns into a roast. While the stock is present, the pan cannot exceed 100 C, so the potatoes poach and absorb. Salt and citric acid diffuse into the outer layer; the surface starch gelatinizes and later dries into a crisp shell (the same principle as parboiling roast potatoes, done in one pan). Once the water is gone, the oil heats past 150 C and the cut faces brown by Maillard reaction against the metal.

  Why measure the liquid. Most failed lemon potatoes are soggy because the cook added liquid by eye. The ratio here, about 200 ml liquid per kilo of potatoes plus the oil, evaporates in 35 to 40 minutes in a standard pan. A smaller pan holds deeper liquid and takes longer; give it the extra time rather than flipping wet.

  Why split the lemon. Citric acid survives the oven; the aromatic compounds (limonene, citral) do not. The lemon in the bath seasons the potato with acid. The raw zest and juice at the end supply the perfume. Both are needed.

  Acid and starch. Acid in the bath slightly firms the potato surface by reinforcing pectin, which is why these wedges hold their shape through a long wet roast instead of collapsing.
