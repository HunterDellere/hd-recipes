steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven and pans. Set racks in the upper and lower thirds of the oven, put two rimmed sheet pans on them and heat the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Season the carrots. In a large bowl, toss the 1200 g / 2½ lb carrots with the 30 g / 2 tbsp olive oil, the 8 g / 2¾ tsp kosher salt and the 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper. The salt draws a little surface moisture that evaporates in the oven, so the surface sugars concentrate and caramelize.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Roast. Spread the carrots cut side down in a single layer across the two hot pans, with space between the pieces. Roast 12 minutes, then scatter the 4 g / 6 thyme sprigs over them, swap the pans between racks, and roast 10 to 15 minutes more, until the undersides are deeply browned and a knife meets slight resistance at the thickest point. Thyme added halfway perfumes the oil without burning. Crowded carrots steam and taste boiled.'
    technique: 'high-heat-dry-roasting'
    time_min: 25
  - text: 'Make the garlic oil while the carrots roast. In a small saucepan over low heat, warm the 40 g / 3 tbsp olive oil and the 12 g / 3 cloves minced garlic, stirring, until the garlic is soft, fragrant and only just turning pale gold, 3 to 4 minutes. Take it off the heat and whisk in the 10 g / 2 tsp white miso and the lemon zest. Garlic cooked gently in oil turns sweet and mellow; browned garlic turns bitter.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Toss hot. Strip the roasted thyme leaves off their stems onto the carrots and discard the stems. Transfer the carrots to a platter and toss them with the garlic oil. They can sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Finish at serving time. Squeeze the lemon juice (about 20 g / 4 tsp) over the carrots and scatter the 15 g / ½ cup chopped parsley and the 1 g / ¼ tsp flaky salt. Taste a carrot: it should taste sweet from the roasting, then garlicky, salty and bright. If it tastes flat, add a little more lemon juice before more salt.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Why savory carrots on a holiday table. Roasted carrots are already sweet: high heat caramelizes their sugars. A holiday plate usually has several sweet things on it, such as cranberry sauce and fruit in the salad, so garlic, lemon and salt balance the plate where a glaze would add more sweetness.

  Why olive oil, not butter. These carrots sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Butter sets as it cools and leaves a waxy coating; olive oil stays fluid and glossy.

  Gluten note. Most white (shiro) miso is made from rice and soybeans and is gluten-free, but some brands use barley. Check the label if a guest is celiac.
