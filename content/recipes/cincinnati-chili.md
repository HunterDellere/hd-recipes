steps:
  - text: 'Bloom the spices. Heat the 15 ml / 1 tbsp neutral oil in a large Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the 85 g / 5 tbsp tomato paste and cook, stirring, 2 minutes until it darkens to brick red. Add the 16 g / 2 tbsp ancho chile powder, 4 g / 2 tsp ground cumin, 3 g / 1 tsp ground cinnamon, 2 g / ¾ tsp ground allspice, 0.5 g / ¼ tsp ground cloves, 1 g / ½ tsp cayenne, and 8 g / 1 tbsp cocoa powder and stir 45 seconds, until fragrant and paste-like. Ground spices scorch in about a minute in a dry, hot pot, so move straight to the next step.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Break the beef into cold water. Pour in the 1000 ml / 4 cups cold water and take the pot off the heat; the water stops the spices cooking and cools the pot quickly. Add the 900 g raw ground beef and break it up with a whisk or potato masher until there are no lumps and the mixture looks like a loose, pink slurry. Doing this before the meat heats keeps the proteins from setting into crumbles, which is what gives Cincinnati chili its fine, saucy texture.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Add everything else. Stir in the 250 g grated onion, 15 g grated garlic, 400 g blended tomatoes, 15 ml / 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, 30 ml / 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar, 4 g / 1 tsp dark brown sugar, 10 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt, 2 g / 1 tsp black pepper, and the 2 bay leaves. The salt goes in early so it seasons the meat during the long simmer; it is deliberately on the low side because the sauce reduces by about a third.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Simmer. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, whisking for the first 5 minutes as it heats so the beef stays fine. Lower the heat to keep a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 2½ to 3 hours, stirring every 20 minutes and scraping the bottom, until it reduces to a thick, spoonable sauce that mounds slightly on a spoon. Add 60 ml / ¼ cup water if it thickens before the time is up; the long cook is what blends the spices, not just reduction.'
    time_min: 165
  - text: 'Chill and skim. Remove the 2 bay leaves. Cool, cover, and refrigerate overnight. Lift off the solid orange fat cap with a spoon and discard most of it. If cooking the same day, skim the liquid fat from the surface with a ladle instead.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Reheat and season. Warm the chili over medium-low heat with 60 ml / ¼ cup water, stirring, until it simmers. Taste and add up to 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt if needed. The vinegar should sharpen the spice without tasting sour.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Cook the spaghetti. Bring 3 L of water to a boil with the 30 g / 3 tbsp kosher salt for the pasta water. Cook the 450 g spaghetti until just past al dente, about 1 minute longer than the package says; Cincinnati spaghetti is soft enough to cut with the side of a fork. Drain without rinsing.'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Assemble a three-way. Put a bed of spaghetti on each oval plate, ladle chili over it, and top with a tall mound of the 340 g finely shredded cheddar; the parlor standard is more cheese than chili by volume. Serve with the 120 g oyster crackers and hot sauce at the table.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  A meat sauce, not a stew. Cincinnati chili is thin by Texas standards and is built to be ladled over spaghetti or hot dogs. Its warm spices (cinnamon, allspice, clove) come from the eastern Mediterranean meat sauces the founders knew. Cumin and chile are present but secondary.

  Why the beef is never browned. Raw ground beef dropped into a hot pan sets into crumbles within seconds as its proteins contract. Dispersed in cold water first, the strands separate before they set, so they cook into fine particles suspended in the sauce. The trade is less Maillard flavor, which the bloomed tomato paste, cocoa, and long reduction make up for.

  Glutamate and acid. Tomato paste, tomatoes, Worcestershire sauce, and the beef supply the savory base, and the cheddar adds more at the table. Cider vinegar is the acid, cooked in from the start so it rounds the spices rather than standing out.

  The cheese. Shred it finely and pile it loose. Pre-shredded cheese is coated in starch and melts into a greasy layer instead of a fluffy mound that slowly softens on the hot chili.
