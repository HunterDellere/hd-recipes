  - 'Have a clean glass jar or squeeze bottle ready. The sauce keeps about 6 weeks refrigerated because of its vinegar, sugar, and salt load, but only if it goes into a clean container.'

steps:
  - text: 'Combine the 220 g Fresno chilies, the 30 g Thai chilies, the 45 g / 10 to 12 garlic cloves, and the 180 g / ¾ cup water in a small heavy saucepan. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, cover, and cook 8 minutes, until the chilies are fully soft and the garlic yields to a spoon. This short cook tames raw allicin bite and softens chili skins so they purée smooth, but stops well short of browning: the target flavor is fresh chili, not roasted.'
    technique: 'simmering'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Add the 70 g / ⅓ cup granulated sugar, the 9 g / 1½ tsp fine sea salt, and the 1.5 g / ¼ tsp MSG; stir until dissolved, about 30 seconds. Seasoning the hot liquid before blending dissolves everything fully and lets the salt pull pigment and capsaicin out of the chili flesh.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Transfer to a blender with the 60 g / ¼ cup rice vinegar. Vent the lid, cover with a towel, and blend on high 60 to 90 seconds until completely smooth. Adding the vinegar off heat preserves its volatile sharpness; boiled vinegar reads flat.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Press the purée through a fine-mesh sieve back into the rinsed saucepan, working it with a ladle or spatula. Discard the seeds and skin left behind (about 2 tbsp). Sieving is what separates Chin-su-style tuong ot from rustic chili paste: the sauce should be satin-smooth with no visible flecks.'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Whisk the 8 g / 1 tbsp tapioca starch into the 30 g / 2 tbsp cold water. Bring the sieved purée back to a bare simmer over medium-low, whisk in the slurry, and cook 1 to 2 minutes, whisking, until the sauce turns glossy and coats a spoon in an even, opaque layer. Tapioca gelatinizes clear and cuts cleanly from a squeeze-bottle tip; cornstarch sets duller and goes slightly gummy as it cools.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Taste hot, knowing heat exaggerates sweetness. The balance should read sweet first, then garlic, then a building chili heat, with vinegar as a clean back note. If flat, add 1 g / a pinch of fine sea salt; if harsh, 5 g / 1 tsp granulated sugar; if cloying, 5 g / 1 tsp rice vinegar. Cool, transfer to a clean jar or squeeze bottle, and refrigerate at least 12 hours before using.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  What defines the Chin-su style: smooth, glossy, bright red-orange, sweet before hot, and loaded with garlic. It is a table sauce meant to be squeezed into soup, onto banh mi, and beside fried food, so it has to hold its heat and color in hot broth without separating. The starch set is what lets it do that; an unthickened chili purée bleeds into a pho bowl as an orange slick.

  Why no fermentation. Thai Si Racha and American sriracha start from a salted chili mash that sits for days or weeks, which builds lactic sourness and a funky depth. Vietnamese bottled tuong ot is cooked fresh and acidified with vinegar instead, so it tastes cleaner and fruitier. If you want the fermented profile, that is a different sauce.

  Color holding. Capsanthin and the other red carotenoids in the chilies are fat-soluble and heat-stable, but long boiling dulls them to brick. The 8-minute covered simmer is enough to soften the fruit without cooking the color out; blending and sieving afterward does the rest of the work mechanically.

  Storage. Refrigerated in a clean jar, the sauce holds its color and flavor for about 6 weeks. Failure looks like a dull brown top layer (oxidation, harmless but flat-tasting) or any fuzzy spotting or fizzing on opening, which means discard. A thin watery layer that separates after a few weeks is starch retrogradation; shake and use.
