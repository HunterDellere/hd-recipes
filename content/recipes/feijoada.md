before_you_start:
  - 'Start desalting the carne seca 24 hours before you cook. It sits in cold water in the refrigerator and needs the water changed every 4 hours while you are awake (at least 4 changes). Carne seca that skips this step makes the whole pot inedibly salty, and there is no fixing it afterward.'
  - 'Soak the black beans the night before (12 hours) in a separate container in the refrigerator.'
  - 'On the day, the beans need 2 to 2½ hours of simmering. The rice, farofa, and pepper sauce are made during the last hour; the collards are cooked last, in 3 minutes, right before serving, because they go drab if they sit.'
  - 'Feijoada is better the next day. If you can, cook it a day ahead, refrigerate, lift off the solid fat, and reheat gently while you make the sides.'
  - 'Shopping: carne seca, linguiça calabresa, smoked pork ribs (costelinha defumada), and farinha de mandioca are all stocked at Brazilian grocers and many Latin markets; see the substitutions for supermarket fallbacks.'

steps:
  - text: 'Desalt the carne seca, 24 hours ahead. Put the 400 g carne seca, cut into 5 cm pieces, in a glass or stainless container and cover with 2 L cold water. Refrigerate. Pour off and replace the water every 4 hours while you are awake, at least 4 changes in total. The salt diffuses out of the meat into the water until the two concentrations meet; fresh water restarts the gradient, which is why changes matter more than total soaking time.'
    time_min: 1440
  - text: 'Soak the beans, 12 hours ahead. Pick over the 500 g dried black beans, rinse, and cover with 2 L cold water in a separate container in the refrigerator. Soaking hydrates the beans evenly so they cook in about 2 hours and burst less.'
    time_min: 720
  - text: 'Blanch the cured meats. Drain the 400 g carne seca. Put it and the 600 g smoked ribs in a 6 L Dutch oven, cover with fresh cold water (about 2 L), bring to a boil, and boil 10 minutes. Drain and discard the water. This pulls out a last round of salt and the gray proteins that would otherwise cloud the broth. Tear a fiber off a piece of carne seca and taste it: it should be well seasoned, like ham, not harsh. If it is still harsh, repeat the blanch once.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Start the beans. Drain the 500 g soaked beans and add them to the Dutch oven with the blanched meats, the 4 bay leaves, and the 3000 ml / 12½ cups water. Bring to a boil over high heat, skim the foam, then lower to a gentle simmer with the lid ajar. Cook 60 minutes, stirring the bottom every 15 minutes. Do not salt yet: the cured meats are still releasing salt and you cannot judge the pot until the end.'
    time_min: 65
  - text: 'Render the bacon and brown the sausage. While the beans simmer, put the 150 g bacon lardons in a wide skillet over medium heat and cook, stirring, until the fat has rendered and the lardons are golden, about 10 minutes. Lift them out with a slotted spoon. In the same fat, brown the 400 g sliced linguiça for about 4 minutes, turning once, and lift it out. Leave about 45 g / 3 tbsp fat in the skillet; pour any excess into a cup (use 30 g of it for the collards later if you like).'
    technique: 'lardon-rendering'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Make the refogado. In the same skillet over medium heat, cook the 300 g chopped onions in the pork fat, stirring with a wooden spoon, until soft and lightly golden, about 8 minutes. Add the 30 g minced garlic and the 2 g / 1 tsp black pepper and cook 1 minute until fragrant, without letting the garlic brown. Take the skillet off the heat.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Combine and thicken. After the first 60 minutes, add the 400 g browned linguiça and the 150 g of bacon lardons to the pot. Ladle about 400 ml / 1⅔ cups of beans with their broth into the skillet with the refogado, set it over medium heat, and mash with the back of the wooden spoon to a coarse paste. Fry the paste 2 minutes, then scrape every bit back into the pot. The mashed beans thicken the broth, and frying them in the seasoned fat carries the onion and garlic into the whole pot.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Finish the simmer. Continue simmering, lid off, for 60 to 75 minutes, scraping the bottom every 10 minutes because the thickening broth scorches easily. Add hot water 120 ml / ½ cup at a time if it gets thicker than heavy cream. It is done when the beans are creamy all the way through, the carne seca pulls into shreds, the rib meat slides off the bone, and the broth is dark and coats a spoon.'
    time_min: 70
  - text: 'Season and rest. Remove the 4 bay leaves. Taste the broth with a bean or two. Only now add kosher salt, a pinch at a time, up to the 4 g / 1 tsp listed; with well-desalted meats you will likely need half of it, and you may need none. Take the pot off the heat, rest 20 minutes, and spoon off the excess fat that rises.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Cook the rice (start during the final simmer). In a heavy 3 L saucepan over medium heat, warm the 20 g / 1½ tbsp neutral oil and cook the 50 g chopped onion and 8 g minced garlic for 2 minutes without browning. Add the 400 g rinsed, drained rice and stir 2 minutes until the grains turn opaque at the edges; this toasting coats the starch so the grains stay separate. Pour in the 880 ml / 3⅔ cups boiling water and the 8 g / 2 tsp kosher salt, stir once, and boil uncovered until the water drops below the surface of the rice and small craters appear, about 6 minutes. Cover, turn the heat to the lowest setting, and cook 12 minutes. Rest off the heat, covered, 10 minutes, then fluff with a fork.'
    time_min: 30
  - text: 'Make the farofa. In a wide skillet over medium heat, melt the 60 g / 4 tbsp butter and cook the 80 g chopped onion until golden at the edges, about 5 minutes. Add the 200 g farinha de mandioca and the 3 g / ¾ tsp kosher salt and stir constantly with a wooden spoon for 5 to 7 minutes, until the flour smells toasty, turns a shade darker, and feels sandy and dry. Spread it on a plate to stop the cooking; it holds at room temperature for hours.'
    technique: 'dry-toasting'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Make the molho apimentado. Once the beans are seasoned, ladle 240 ml / 1 cup of their hot broth into a small bowl. Stir in the 45 g / 3 tbsp lime juice, the 60 g minced white onion, the 5 g minced chilies, the 8 g chopped parsley, and the 1 g / ¼ tsp kosher salt. Taste: it should be sour first, then hot, then savory. Each diner spoons it over their beans; it is the main acid on the plate.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Cut the collards. Stack 6 or so stemmed leaves from the 450 g collard greens, roll them tightly into a cigar, and slice across with a sharp knife into ribbons as thin as you can manage, 2 mm or less. Thin ribbons cook in 2 minutes and stay bright and slightly crisp; thick ones need longer and turn army green.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Cook the collards, at the last minute. Heat a wide skillet over high heat with the 30 g / 2 tbsp olive oil (or bacon fat). Add the 10 g sliced garlic and stir 20 seconds until it just starts to color, then add the collards and the 3 g / ¾ tsp kosher salt. Toss constantly with tongs for 1½ to 2 minutes, only until the ribbons wilt and turn vivid green. Move them to a dish straight away, since the residual heat in the pan keeps cooking them.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Serve. Bring the feijoada to the table in its pot (or lift the meats onto a platter and the beans into a deep bowl), with the rice, collards, farofa, the molho apimentado, and the 600 g / 3 oranges sliced into rounds. The usual order on the plate: rice, beans and meats over it, collards beside, farofa scattered on top, a spoon of molho, and orange slices eaten between bites to reset the palate against the pork fat.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  Salt is the one thing that ruins feijoada, and it comes almost entirely from the cured meats. That is why the recipe desalts the carne seca for a full day, blanches the cured meats before they meet the beans, and holds all added salt until the very end. Taste the broth before adding any. If the pot does end up too salty, add 500 ml unsalted water and another 150 g of dried beans cooked separately, which dilutes rather than masks.

  The flavor comes in layers. The cured beef and smoked ribs supply glutamate and smoke over the long simmer; the bacon and sausage are browned separately so they add roasted notes the simmer alone cannot make; the refogado, fried with mashed beans, carries onion and garlic sweetness through the pot and thickens the broth at the same time. The acid is deliberately kept off the pot and put on the plate, as lime in the molho apimentado and as orange slices, so each bite can be as bright as the eater wants.

  The accompaniments are part of the dish, not garnish. Rice is the neutral base; the collards bring bitterness and crunch; farofa soaks up broth and adds a toasty crunch; the orange cuts the fat. Plan the sides so they finish together: rice and farofa during the last 45 minutes of the beans, the molho once the beans are seasoned, the collards in the final 3 minutes.

  On the black-beans-from-dried recipe on this site: it is a stand-alone pot of beans cooked with onion and kombu. Feijoada cooks the dried beans directly with the meats, which is where their flavor comes from, so it is not used here.

  Leftovers keep 4 days refrigerated and freeze well for 3 months (beans and meats only; make the sides fresh). Reheat gently with a splash of water, since the broth sets firm when cold. Leftover feijoada mashed and fried with garlic is tutu de feijão, a dish in its own right.
