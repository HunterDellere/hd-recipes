safety_notes:
  - for: 'The egg yolk is only warmed by the hot cheese and a minute in the oven, and stays liquid'
    ref: 'safety/egg-doneness#conditionally-safe-runny-yolk-set-white'
    why: 'The yolk does not reach the 60 °C / 140 °F hold that would pasteurize it; it is stirred into cheese that is well above that, which warms it but does not reliably cook it through. For healthy adults the risk from a fresh egg is low. For pregnancy, young children, older or immunocompromised eaters, use a pasteurized in-shell egg, or crack the yolk in for the last 3 minutes of baking so it sets.'

before_you_start:
  - 'The dough needs 1 to 1½ hours to double at warm room temperature, or it can rise slowly in the refrigerator overnight (8 to 18 hours) in a covered glass bowl; let cold dough sit 30 minutes before shaping.'
  - 'Preheat the oven with a baking steel or stone on the lower-middle rack at 260 °C / 500 °F for a full 45 minutes before baking. A cold or briefly heated oven gives a pale, bready base under a cheese filling that has already split into oil.'
  - 'Take the cheese out of the refrigerator when you shape, not earlier: cold cheese melts more evenly and is easier to grate.'

steps:
  - text: 'Mix the dough. In a large bowl whisk the 300 g / 2⅓ cups bread flour, 4 g / 1¼ tsp instant yeast, 6 g / 1½ tsp granulated sugar, and 6 g / 1 tsp fine sea salt. Add the 140 g / ½ cup plus 1 tbsp warm milk, the 60 g / ¼ cup warm water, and the 20 g / 1½ tbsp melted butter, and stir with a wooden spoon until no dry flour remains. The dough will be soft and slightly tacky.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Knead on a lightly floured counter for 8 to 10 minutes (or 6 minutes in a stand mixer with the dough hook on medium-low) until smooth and elastic: a small piece should stretch thin enough to let light through before it tears. The milk fat and butter tenderize the crumb, so the rims bake soft and pull apart rather than chewing like pizza crust. Return it to the bowl, cover with a damp kitchen towel, and let it rise at warm room temperature until doubled, 1 to 1½ hours.'
    technique: 'enriched-dough'
    time_min: 75
  - text: 'Divide and rest. Turn the dough out, cut it in half (about 260 g each), and shape each piece into a tight ball. Cover with the towel and rest 15 minutes so the gluten relaxes enough to roll without springing back. Check that the oven and steel have had their 45 minutes at 260 °C / 500 °F.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Make the filling. In a bowl, combine the 250 g / 2½ cups grated mozzarella and the 150 g / 1 cup crumbled feta with 1 of the egg whites (about 30 g) and the 30 g / 2 tbsp milk, and mash with a fork into a thick, cohesive paste. The white and milk bind the cheese so it melts into a creamy mass instead of separating into strings and grease. Taste a crumb: it should be clearly salty and a little sour. If your feta is mild, add a pinch of fine sea salt (about 0.5 g / ⅛ tsp).'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Shape the boats. On a sheet of parchment, roll one ball into an oval about 30 x 20 cm (12 x 8 inches) and 5 mm thick. Lay a 2 cm-wide line of filling, about 25 g / 3 tbsp, along each long edge. Roll each long edge inward over its line of cheese, two full turns, so you have two thick cheese-stuffed rims with an open center about 8 cm / 3 inches wide. Pinch the rims together at both ends and twist each end once to make the pointed bow and stern of the boat. Spoon about 150 g / 1 heaping cup of the remaining filling into the center and spread it level with the rims. Repeat with the second ball.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Brush the dough rims, not the cheese, with the second egg white (about 30 g), beaten with a fork. The white browns and glosses the crust without the extra color a yolk wash would add at this oven temperature.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Bake. Slide the boats on their parchment onto the hot steel and bake 12 to 14 minutes, until the rims are deep golden with darker blisters and the cheese is bubbling with a few brown spots. If your oven runs unevenly, rotate the parchment at the 8-minute mark.'
    time_min: 13
  - text: 'Add the yolks. Pull the boats out, and with the back of a spoon press a shallow well into the middle of each cheese bed. Slip 1 egg yolk into each well and return the boats to the oven for 60 to 90 seconds, just until the yolk is warmed and glossy but still fully liquid.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Serve. Set one 20 g / 1½ tbsp slab of cold butter beside each yolk and bring the boats to the table at once. Each eater stirs the yolk and butter vigorously into the hot cheese with a fork until it turns into a smooth, glossy, almost fondue-like sauce, then tears off pieces of the rim, starting at the pointed ends, to scoop it up.'
    time_min: 1

modifications:
  - for: 'the cheese blend'
    to: 'closer to Georgian sulguni and imeruli'
    how: 'If you can find sulguni (sold in Georgian, Russian, and Eastern European groceries, often vacuum-packed), use 250 g grated sulguni in place of the mozzarella. For imeruli, use 150 g farmer''s cheese or dry-curd cottage cheese with 3 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt mixed in, in place of the feta. The result is milder, more elastic, and more sour than the supermarket version.'
    kind: 'regional'
  - for: 'egg yolk'
    to: 'whole egg, set white'
    how: 'Crack a whole egg into each well and bake 3 to 4 minutes instead of 60 to 90 seconds, until the white is just set and the yolk is still soft. This is how many bakeries in Batumi serve it, and it avoids the raw-yolk question.'

notes: |
  Why two cheeses. Sulguni and imeruli each do half the job. Sulguni is a stretched-curd cheese, like mozzarella, that melts into an elastic, cohesive mass; imeruli is a young curd cheese that brings salt and lactic sourness but does not flow. Either alone is wrong: all mozzarella is bland and rubbery, all feta goes dry and grainy. Mixed roughly 60/40, the stretchy cheese carries the crumbly one into a molten, salty, slightly tangy bed.

  Why the egg white and milk in the filling. Low-moisture mozzarella heated hard tends to break: its fat pools on top and the protein tightens into strings. A little egg white and milk add water and protein that bind the melted cheese into a creamy, stable mass, which is closer to how fresh Georgian cheeses behave and gives the yolk and butter something smooth to stir into.

  Why the yolk goes in at the end. Added at the start, the yolk overcooks to chalk in a 260 °C oven. Added in the last minute, it stays liquid, and stirring it into the hot cheese with cold butter at the table makes a quick enriched cheese sauce. The cold butter matters: it cools the cheese a few degrees and keeps the mixture glossy rather than greasy.

  Umami and acid. The feta (or imeruli) supplies both: aged and brined cheese carries free glutamate, and its lactic sourness is the dish's acid. Georgian cooks do not add lemon or vinegar here, and the bread does not need it. A sharp side, such as a cucumber and tomato salad with walnuts or vinegar-dressed greens, does the balancing on the plate.

  Storage. Khachapuri is best within 10 minutes of leaving the oven. Leftovers (without the yolk) reheat in a 200 °C / 400 °F oven for 6 to 8 minutes. The shaped, unfilled dough balls can be refrigerated, covered, for up to 18 hours.
