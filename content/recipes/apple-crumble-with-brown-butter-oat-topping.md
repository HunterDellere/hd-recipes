before_you_start:
  - 'Make the topping first and chill it at least 30 minutes, up to 3 days ahead in a covered jar or a bowl under a plate. Warm brown butter makes a paste that bakes flat; cold topping breaks into clusters that stay crisp over the juicy fruit.'
  - 'The apples can be peeled, sliced, and tossed with the lemon juice the morning of (refrigerated, covered with a plate). Add the sugar, cornstarch, and spice only at assembly; sugar draws juice out of the slices within 30 minutes and the filling turns soupy.'
  - 'On a holiday schedule, assemble while the mains rest and bake at 190 °C / 375 °F as everyone sits down. It comes out warm for dessert and needs 15 minutes of rest anyway for the juices to set.'

steps:
  - text: 'Brown the butter. Melt the 170 g / 12 tbsp butter in a light-colored saucepan over medium heat. It foams as the water boils off (about 3 minutes), then the foam thins and the milk solids sink and begin to toast. Swirl constantly. Pull it off the heat when the solids are hazelnut-brown and the butter smells of toffee and toasted nuts, 5 to 7 minutes total. Scrape it, solids and all, into a medium bowl; the solids carry most of the flavor. Cool 10 minutes, until warm rather than hot.'
    technique: 'brown-butter'
    time_min: 17
  - text: 'Mix and chill the topping. Add the 125 g / 1 cup flour, 90 g / 1 cup rolled oats, 110 g / ½ cup packed light brown sugar, 70 g / ¾ cup toasted chopped pecans (if using), and 3 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt to the warm brown butter. Stir with a fork until no dry flour remains, then squeeze handfuls together so it forms damp clumps from pea to walnut size. Spread in a shallow dish, cover with a plate, and refrigerate at least 30 minutes. Cold fat sets the clumps; they hold their shape in the oven and crisp at the edges instead of melting into one sandy layer.'
    time_min: 35
  - text: 'Heat the oven and prepare the apples. Heat the oven to 190 °C / 375 °F with a rack in the middle. Peel, core, and cut the 800 g Granny Smith and 800 g Honeycrisp (or Pink Lady or Braeburn) apples into 1 cm / ⅜ inch wedges. Toss them immediately in a large bowl with the 30 g / 2 tbsp lemon juice and 2 g / 1 tsp zest. The acid slows polyphenol oxidase browning and sets the bright edge the sugar will balance. Two varieties do two jobs: Granny Smith holds its shape and brings malic acid; the sweeter apple softens partway and thickens the syrup.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Season the filling. In a small bowl, whisk the 80 g / ⅓ cup packed light brown sugar, 16 g / 2 tbsp cornstarch, 3 g / 1¼ tsp cinnamon, 0.5 g / ¼ tsp nutmeg or cardamom, and 1.5 g / ¼ tsp fine sea salt. Whisking the cornstarch into the sugar first separates the granules so they do not clump when they meet the wet apples. Add it to the apples with the 8 g / 1½ tsp vanilla bean paste and toss until every slice is coated. Taste a slice: it should read tart-sweet with a clear spice note. If your apples are very sweet, add 5 g / 1 tsp more lemon juice.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Assemble. Turn the apples and any syrup into an ungreased 23 x 33 cm / 9 x 13 inch baking dish and level them. Scatter the cold topping evenly over the fruit, leaving the clumps intact; do not press it down. Small gaps let steam vent, which keeps the underside of the topping from turning gummy. Sprinkle the 1 g / scant ¼ tsp flaky salt over the top. Set the dish on a sheet pan to catch drips.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Bake. Bake at 190 °C / 375 °F for 45 to 55 minutes. It is done when the topping is deep golden-brown and the juices bubble thickly at the edges and through the gaps. Thick, slow bubbles mean the starch has passed its gelatinization point (about 95 °C) and the juice will set; thin, fast bubbles mean it needs 5 to 10 more minutes. A paring knife should slide through the apples with slight resistance. If the topping browns before the juices thicken, lay a sheet of foil loosely over the top.'
    time_min: 50
  - text: 'Rest. Cool on a rack for at least 15 minutes, up to 1 hour. The starch gel firms as it cools from bubbling toward 60 °C; served straight from the oven, the juices run thin across the plate.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Whip the crème fraîche cream. While the crumble rests, whisk the 240 g / 1 cup cold heavy cream, 120 g / ½ cup cold crème fraîche, and 15 g / 1 tbsp granulated sugar in a chilled bowl to soft, drooping peaks, 2 to 3 minutes by hand. The crème fraîche adds lactic tang that matches the Granny Smith acid and stabilizes the foam, so it holds for an hour in the fridge. Serve the crumble warm with a spoonful of cream on each portion.'
    time_min: 3

notes: |
  Why two apples. A single variety forces a compromise. All Granny Smith reads sharp and stays in firm slices with a thin syrup. All Honeycrisp turns sweet and soft with little acid. Half and half gives distinct slices in a glossy, tart-sweet sauce, which is what makes a crumble taste like apple rather than like sugar.

  Why cornstarch, and why so little. Apples release about 15 to 20% of their weight as juice in the oven. With 16 g of cornstarch, that juice sets to a loose, spoonable syrup once cooled to about 60 °C. Flour would need twice the amount and leaves a cloudy, pasty finish. More cornstarch turns the filling gluey, which is the common failure of pie-style apple fillings.

  Why brown butter in the topping. Browning toasts the milk proteins and lactose into nutty, toffee-like compounds that echo the brown sugar. It also removes the butter's water, so the topping has less steam to soften it from the inside and bakes crisper than one made with cold cubed butter. The chill afterwards restores the firmness that cold-butter rubbing would otherwise provide.

  Holiday timing. Assemble while the mains rest and bake at 190 °C / 375 °F once the oven is free. A 50 minute bake plus a 15 minute rest lands neatly at the end of a long dinner.
