steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 230 C / 450 F with a rack in the upper third. Give it a full 20 minutes; crisp skin depends on the oven being at temperature when the fish goes in.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Dry-brine the fish. Season the 1100 g / 2 scored branzino inside the cavity, outside, and into the slashes with the 11 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt. Set on a rack, uncovered, for 20 minutes. The salt dissolves into surface moisture, diffuses into the outer flesh, and the skin dries as it sits; pat dry again with paper towels just before rubbing.'
    technique: 'dry-brining'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Build the bed while the fish brines. On a rimmed sheet pan toss the 200 g / 1 sliced red onion and 300 g / 2 cups cherry tomatoes with the 28 g / 2 tbsp olive oil for the bed and the 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt. Spread into two fish-length strips. Roast alone for 8 minutes so the onion starts to soften and the tomato skins split before the fish joins them; the fish cooks faster than raw onion.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Rub the fish. Mix the 2 g / 1 tsp ground coriander, 2 g / 1 tsp sweet paprika, 2 g / 2 tsp crumbled rigani, and 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper. Rub each fish all over with the 28 g / 2 tbsp olive oil for the fish, then the spice mix, working it into the slashes. Coriander and paprika are fat-soluble; the oil carries them into the skin, where they bloom in the oven rather than scorching as dry powder.'
    technique: 'fish-skin-scoring'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Stuff the cavities. Divide the 100 g / 1 sliced lemon, 15 g / 4 sliced garlic cloves, 10 g parsley sprigs, and 5 g dill sprigs between the two fish. Lay them in loosely; a packed cavity insulates the spine and slows the cook.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Roast. Lay each fish on its strip of onion and tomato and roast 14 to 18 minutes, until the skin is blistered and the flesh at the thickest part behind the head reads 55 C / 131 F on an instant-read thermometer, or flakes when nudged at the spine. Branzino is lean (2 to 4% fat) and dries past 58 C; pull at the low end. The tomatoes collapse into the fish drippings below and concentrate into the savory base of the plate.'
    technique: 'high-heat-dry-roasting'
    time_min: 16
  - text: 'Whisk the ladolemono while the fish roasts. In a small bowl whisk the 55 g / ¼ cup olive oil for the ladolemono with the 30 g / 2 tbsp lemon juice until cloudy and loosely emulsified, then stir in the 10 g / 1 tbsp chopped capers and the 1 g / 1 tsp dried oregano for the ladolemono. Make it within 15 minutes of serving; raw lemon loses its top notes as it sits.'
    technique: 'vinaigrette-emulsion'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Rest and serve. Rest the fish on the pan 3 minutes so carryover finishes the spine. Lift the fillets off the bone, spoon the blistered tomatoes and onion alongside, and whisk the ladolemono again before spooning it over everything. Taste a bite of flesh and tomato together; if it reads flat, add the 1 g pinch of flaky salt and another squeeze of lemon. Scatter the reserved chopped parsley from the 10 g bunch.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  What changed from the reference. The source recipe is sound: whole fish, warm spices, lemon and herbs, hot oven. The refinements address the three ways a home-roasted branzino disappoints. Underseasoned interior: salt applied right before roasting sits on the skin; a 20-minute dry brine moves it into the outer flesh and dries the skin. Flat lemon: lemon slices roasted in the cavity perfume the flesh but their juice turns cooked and dull, so the brightness has to come from raw lemon at the table, which is exactly what Greek ladolemono is. Missing savory depth: lean white fish has little free glutamate. Cherry tomatoes roasted under the fish blister, collapse into the drippings, and concentrate their glutamate; capers in the dressing add a briny counterpoint.

  Why coriander and paprika. They are not classically Greek on fish (Greek tavernas use salt, oil, rigani, lemon), but they are the reference's signature and they earn their place: ground coriander has a citrusy linalool note that bridges to the lemon, and paprika browns on the skin for color. Keep the quantities light; the fish should taste of fish and lemon, not of a spice rub. For the purist taverna version, drop both and double the rigani.

  Scoring. Three slashes per side let heat reach the shoulder at the same rate as the thin tail and belly, and give the brine and rub a path into the flesh. Cut to the bone but not through it.
