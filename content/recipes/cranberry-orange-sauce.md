steps:
  - text: 'Make the syrup. In a medium saucepan, stir the 140 g / ⅔ cup granulated sugar, about 40 g / 3 tbsp of the orange juice, the 60 g / ¼ cup water and the 1 g / ¼ tsp kosher salt over medium heat with a wooden spoon until the sugar dissolves and the syrup simmers.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'First stage. Add about 225 g / 2 cups (two thirds) of the 340 g cranberries and simmer, stirring now and then, until nearly all of them have burst and the liquid turns glossy and thick, 6 to 8 minutes. Bursting releases the pectin in the skins and flesh, and the sugar and acid let that pectin form a gel as it cools.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Second stage. Add the remaining 115 g / 1 cup cranberries and the orange zest (about 3 g / 1 tbsp) and cook 2 minutes more, only until the new berries turn bright and the first few split. Most of them stay whole, which gives the sauce its texture.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Finish off the heat. Stir in the remaining orange juice (about 40 g / 3 tbsp). Fresh juice added cold keeps the orange aroma that boiling drives off. The sauce will look loose; it sets as it cools.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Taste and store. It should taste tart first, then sweet, with orange at the end. If it tastes only sweet, add fresh orange or lemon juice 5 g / 1 tsp at a time. Cool it 30 minutes, then transfer it to a glass jar, lid it and refrigerate at least 4 hours, up to 5 days.'
    time_min: 240

notes: |
  Why two stages. Berries cooked until they burst give pectin and body but no texture. Berries added at the end give texture but no body. Splitting them gives a sauce that sets and still has whole berries in it.

  Why there is so little water. Cranberries are about 87% water and release most of it as they burst. More water would make a thin sauce that needs a long reduction, and a long boil dulls the color and the flavor.

  Why salt in a sweet sauce. A small amount of salt reduces how bitter and flat the cranberry skins taste, so the fruit tastes brighter. At 1 g per batch, nobody tastes the salt itself.
