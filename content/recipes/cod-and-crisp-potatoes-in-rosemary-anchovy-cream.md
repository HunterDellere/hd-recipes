safety_notes:
  - for: 'cod at 54 to 57°C / 130 to 135°F internal, carrying to about 60°C / 140°F (below the USDA published-safe 145°F for fish)'
    ref: 'safety/seafood-doneness#cooking-targets-for-fin-fish'
    why: 'Cod is about 0.7% fat and turns dry and chalky well before 63°C, so restaurant and home practice pulls lean white fish at 54 to 57°C and lets carryover in the hot skillet finish it near 60°C. The published 145°F is the conservative line for the immunocompromised. Cod from a reputable counter (frozen-at-sea is excellent), dry-brined and baked in a bubbling 230°C sauce that holds the exterior well above pasteurizing temperature, follows established practice at the lower number. Cooks who want the USDA-published-safe figure should bake to 145°F / 63°C internal, accepting firmer, drier flake.'

before_you_start:
  - 'Dry-brine the cod first. It needs 30 to 60 minutes uncovered in the refrigerator, and the potatoes and sauce fill that window. Do not go past 2 hours.'
  - 'Run the potatoes and the sauce in parallel: once the potatoes go into the oven, fry the capers and toast the panko, then build the sauce in the same skillet. Both should be ready within a few minutes of each other.'
  - 'Use a 30 cm / 12 in oven-safe skillet (stainless or cast iron). Everything ends up in it, and a smaller pan crowds the cod and drowns the potatoes.'

steps:
  - text: 'Dry-brine the cod. Pat the 680 g / 1½ lb cod portions dry and season all sides with the 7 g / 2½ tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt, about 1% of the fish weight. Set them on a rack over a plate and refrigerate uncovered 30 to 60 minutes. The salt dissolves into the surface moisture and partially solubilizes the myosin, so the flesh cooks up firm and seasoned through instead of watery. Past 2 hours the texture turns ham-like.'
    technique: 'dry-brining'
    time_min: 45
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 230C / 450F with a rimmed sheet pan on the center rack. Bring the 1.9 L / 2 qt water, 16 g / 2 tbsp kosher salt, and 3 g / ½ tsp baking soda to a boil. Add the 680 g / 1½ lb Yukon Gold chunks and boil 8 to 10 minutes, until a paring knife meets slight resistance at the core. The alkaline water breaks down surface pectin, and that softened exterior becomes the starchy paste that crisps in the oven.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Drain the potatoes and let them steam dry 1 minute. Return them to the pot with the 27 g / 2 tbsp olive oil and 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper and shake hard until the edges look fuzzy and coated in paste. Spread them in a single layer on the preheated pan and roast 25 to 30 minutes, until deep golden on at least two faces. Flip at 15 minutes and toss in the 3 g / 1 tbsp minced rosemary then; added earlier, it scorches.'
    technique: 'high-heat-dry-roasting'
    time_min: 30
  - text: 'While the potatoes roast, heat the 40 g / 3 tbsp olive oil in the 30 cm / 12 in oven-safe skillet over medium heat. Add the 27 g / 3 tbsp dried capers and fry 3 to 4 minutes, until they open and the sputtering stops, which means their water has cooked off. Lift them onto paper towel. Pour all but 1 tbsp of the oil into a cup and reserve it for the sauce.'
    technique: 'shallow-frying'
    time_min: 4
  - text: 'Toast the 30 g / ½ cup panko in the oil left in the skillet, stirring, 2 to 3 minutes, until amber. Transfer it to a bowl, season with the 0.5 g pinch of kosher salt, and wipe out the skillet.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Return the skillet to medium heat with the reserved caper oil and the 28 g / 2 tbsp butter. When the butter foams, add the 16 g / 4 anchovy fillets and mash them with a wooden spoon until they dissolve into the fat, about 1 minute. Add the 90 g / 2 minced shallots with the 1 g / ¼ tsp kosher salt; the salt draws out moisture so they sweat translucent without browning, 3 to 4 minutes. Add the 15 g / 4 cloves sliced garlic and 0.5 g / ¼ tsp red pepper flakes and cook 45 seconds, until fragrant.'
    time_min: 6
  - text: 'Deglaze with the 120 ml / ½ cup dry white wine, scraping up the fond, and reduce to a syrupy glaze, about 3 minutes. This drives off the raw ethanol edge and concentrates the tartaric and malic acids. Add the 120 ml / ½ cup fish stock and reduce by half, about 2 minutes.'
    technique: 'deglazing'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Add the 240 g / 1 cup heavy cream, the 4 g / 2 rosemary sprigs, and the 4 g / 2 tsp lemon zest. Simmer at a lively bubble 4 to 5 minutes, until the sauce lightly coats a spoon but still runs; it tightens further in the oven and you need it fluid enough to come partway up the potatoes. Pull the rosemary sprigs at 5 minutes at the latest: camphor and cineole extract into fat quickly and turn medicinal with a long steep. Heavy cream carries so little casein relative to its fat that it will not curdle at a simmer, even with the wine acid present.'
    technique: 'reduction'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Off the heat, whisk in the 15 g / 1 tbsp dijon mustard. Added late, its isothiocyanate heat survives, and its seed mucilage helps hold the emulsion through the bake. Taste: the anchovy carries most of the salt, so add more only if the sauce needs it, remembering the cod is already brined and the capers go on top.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Set the roasted potatoes into the sauce in a single layer, pressing them in so the sauce comes about a third of the way up and the crisp tops stay above it. Pat the cod dry, nestle the portions between the potatoes, and spoon a little sauce over each one. Bake at 230C / 450F for 8 to 12 minutes, until the thickest portion reads 54 to 57C / 130 to 135F. Carryover in the hot skillet brings it to about 60C / 140F, where the flakes separate under a fork but the center stays moist.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Taste the sauce, then squeeze the 22 g / 1½ tbsp lemon juice into the cream around the fish rather than over the crust, and taste again: the sauce should read cleaner and the rosemary sharper. Toss the toasted panko with the fried capers and the 10 g / 3 tbsp minced parsley and scatter it over the top. Finish the potatoes with the 1 g / ½ tsp flaky sea salt and serve from the skillet.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'The Adjustment: if the sauce tastes flat, mash in ¼ tsp anchovy paste or add 1 tsp caper brine before reaching for salt; glutamate is usually what is missing, not sodium. If it reads heavy, add lemon in ½ tsp increments rather than more mustard. If oil beads on the surface, the sauce was over-reduced: whisk in 1 tbsp cold cream off the heat to re-emulsify. If the rosemary reads piney or medicinal, push lemon and parsley harder now and pull the sprigs sooner next time.'

notes: |
  Why it tastes deeper than the original. The gain is mostly umami synergy. Cured anchovy is dense in free glutamate, and cod muscle accumulates inosinate (IMP) as ATP breaks down after the catch. Glutamate activates the T1R1/T1R3 umami receptor and IMP binds a separate site on the same receptor that locks it in its active state, so together they read several times more savory than either alone. Dissolving the anchovy into the base of the sauce pairs glutamate with the inosinate in every bite of fish, so the dish tastes savory without tasting of anchovy.

  How much sauce, and why it stays loose. One cup of cream reduced with the wine and stock gives roughly 250 ml of sauce, which in a 30 cm skillet with the potatoes and cod in it sits only about 1 cm deep. That is the right amount: the potatoes are meant to be partly submerged so their tops stay crisp, but it leaves no margin for an over-reduced sauce. Stop the stovetop reduction while the sauce still runs; the 10 minutes at 230C and the juices the cod sheds settle it to a coating consistency.

  The doneness rule. Cod is about 0.7% fat and has no buffer for overcooking. Pull it at 54 to 57C / 130 to 135F in the thickest part and let the hot skillet finish it. The USDA 63C / 145F target leaves cod chalky. If the cod flakes dry, you overshot; pull at 54C next time.

  Salt map. The cod is brined at 1%, the potatoes are seasoned by their parboil water, the anchovy and fried capers carry most of the sauce salt, and the flaky salt goes only on the potato tops for crunch. Taste the sauce before you add any salt to it.
