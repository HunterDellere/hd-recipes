steps:
  - text: 'Heat the pan. Put a 25 cm (10 inch) cast-iron skillet on the middle rack, heat the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F, and leave the skillet in for 20 minutes after the oven reaches temperature.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Blend the batter while the pan heats. Put the 150 g / 3 room-temperature eggs, 160 ml / ⅔ cup room-temperature milk, 85 g / ⅔ cup flour, 12 g / 1 tbsp granulated sugar, 1.5 g / ¼ tsp fine sea salt, 3 g / ½ tsp vanilla bean paste, and 2 g / 1 tsp lemon zest in a blender. Blend on high for 20 seconds, scrape down the sides, and blend 10 seconds more until completely smooth and frothy. Let it rest in the jar for at least 10 minutes while the pan finishes heating; the flour hydrates, the bubbles settle, and the pancake rises more evenly.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Butter the pan. Pull out the oven rack, add the 40 g / 3 tbsp butter to the skillet (use a thick, dry oven mitt; the handle is at oven temperature), and swirl until it melts and foams, about 30 seconds. Work quickly: the butter should be foaming and just starting to smell nutty, not brown, when the batter goes in.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Pour and bake. Give the batter a 2-second pulse to recombine, pour it all into the center of the skillet, and slide the rack back in. Close the door and do not open it for 15 minutes; a gust of cool air before the egg sets will collapse the puff. Bake 18 minutes total, until the edges have climbed high up the sides and are deep golden brown and crisp, and the center is set with no wet sheen. If the center is still wobbly, give it 2 more minutes.'
    time_min: 18
  - text: 'Finish. Take the skillet out and set it on a trivet. The pancake will start to sink within a minute, which is normal; the center settles into a soft custard while the rim stays crisp. Squeeze the 30 ml / 2 tbsp lemon juice evenly over the surface and dust with the 15 g / 2 tbsp powdered sugar through a small sieve. Cut into wedges in the pan and serve immediately.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  How it rises. A Dutch baby has no baking powder or yeast. The batter is mostly egg and milk, so it is about 70% water. When it hits a pan at well over 200 °C, the water at the bottom and sides flashes to steam and pushes the batter up the walls, and the egg proteins set into a crisp shell before the steam escapes. That is why the pan temperature matters more than anything else, and why the oven door stays shut for the first 15 minutes.

  Why room temperature and a blender. Cold eggs and milk drop the pan temperature the moment they go in, and the edges rise less. A blender mixes the thin batter in seconds without lumps; overmixing is not a concern here, because you want some gluten development to help the walls hold their height.

  Why lemon and powdered sugar. The batter is only lightly sweet, so the finish carries the flavor. Fresh lemon juice soaks into the custardy center and cuts the richness of the eggs and butter, and the powdered sugar dissolves into it to make a thin, sharp-sweet glaze. Bottled lemon juice tastes flat; use a fresh lemon.

  Why it falls. The puff is held up by steam, so as it cools the pancake sinks. Serve it right away; it will taste just as good once it settles, but the drama is in the first minute.
