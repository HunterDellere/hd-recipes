steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 350°F (177°C) with a rack centered. In a 2-cup measuring cup or small bowl, whisk the hot brewed coffee or hot water (1 cup / 240 g) with the Dutch-process cocoa (⅓ cup / 30 g) until smooth. Rest 2 minutes. Hot liquid blooms the cocoa, dissolving its starch and releasing the aromatics a cold batter leaves locked up; coffee''s roast compounds overlap with cocoa''s and deepen it.'

    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Whisk the neutral oil (⅓ cup / 75 g), vinegar (1 tbsp / 15 g), and vanilla bean paste (1 tsp / 5 g) into the cocoa mixture. The oil also cools it down so the leaveners do not fire too early.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Directly in an ungreased 8-inch square metal pan, whisk the all-purpose flour (1½ cups / 190 g), granulated sugar (¾ cup / 150 g), light brown sugar (¼ cup / 55 g), baking soda (½ tsp / 3 g), baking powder (1 tsp / 4 g), and fine sea salt (½ tsp / 3 g) with a fork, breaking up any brown sugar lumps.'

  - text: 'Pour the cocoa mixture into the pan and whisk with the fork until smooth, about 1 minute, working into the corners and along the bottom edges so no flour hides there. Wipe the rim clean.     time_min: 2
  - text: 'Bake 30 to 35 minutes, until the center springs back and a tester comes out with a few moist crumbs, or the center reads 205°F (96°C).'
    time_min: 32
  - text: 'Cool in the pan on a rack at least 30 minutes, then cut and serve from the pan. Dust with powdered sugar if you like; it needs nothing else.'

  - text: 'The Adjustment: if the cake tastes soapy or metallic, the soda was measured heavy or the vinegar short; level both exactly. notes: |
  Why it works with no eggs, butter, or milk. Eggs normally give a cake structure and lift; here the lift comes entirely from chemical leavening (vinegar reacting with baking soda, plus baking powder), and the structure comes from flour starch setting around those bubbles. Oil replaces butter and stays liquid at room temperature, which is why this cake is softer on day three than most butter cakes are on day one.

  Dutch cocoa and the leavening split. The original uses natural cocoa, which is acidic and helps the vinegar neutralize a full teaspoon of soda. Dutch-process cocoa has been alkalized, so that acid is gone. Keeping a full teaspoon of soda would leave the cake soapy and over-darkened. Half the soda plus a teaspoon of baking powder, which carries its own acid, gives the same rise with a rounder, deeper chocolate flavor.

  Speed matters. The soda starts reacting the moment the vinegar hits it. Mix quickly and bake immediately; a pan left sitting on the counter for 10 minutes loses lift and sinks.

  Storage. Covered at room temperature 4 days. It does not need the fridge, which would firm the crumb.
