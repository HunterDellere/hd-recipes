steps:
  - text: 'Soften the onion. Melt the 60 g / 4 tbsp butter in a 3 L saucepan over medium heat. Add the 100 g / ½ medium minced onion and cook, stirring with a wooden spoon, until soft and light golden, 5 to 6 minutes.'
    technique: 'sweating'
    time_min: 6
  - text: 'Cook a brown roux. Sprinkle the 40 g / ⅓ cup flour over the onion and stir until no dry flour shows. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly and scraping the corners, until the roux turns the color of peanut butter and smells nutty, 5 to 7 minutes. Browning the flour gives the gravy its color and toasted flavor. A darker roux thickens less, because heat breaks down some of the starch, which is why this gravy uses a little more flour than a pale one would.'
    time_min: 7
  - text: 'Add the stock. Whisk in the 720 g / 3 cups chicken stock in three additions, whisking smooth before each, so no lumps form. Add the 15 g / 1 tbsp soy sauce and the 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper. Bring to a simmer and cook 8 to 10 minutes, whisking now and then, until it coats a spoon and a line drawn through it on the spoon holds.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Season. Taste, then add the 2 g / ¾ tsp kosher salt a little at a time, only as much as it needs. For a smooth gravy, pour it through a sieve and press on the onion; for a home-style gravy, leave the onion in. If it is too thick, whisk in water 30 g / 2 tbsp at a time.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Using juices from the meat. Juices from a roast or from a container of bought smoked meat add a lot of flavor. Let them stand 5 minutes, spoon off the fat, and whisk up to 120 g / ½ cup into the gravy in place of the same amount of stock. They are salty, so season only after they go in.

  Why soy sauce. Soy adds the brown color that roast drippings normally give, and its glutamate makes the gravy taste meatier. At 15 g in 3 cups, it does not taste of soy.
