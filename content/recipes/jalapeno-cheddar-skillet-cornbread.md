steps:
  - text: 'Soak the cornmeal. Stir the 240 g / 1½ cups plus 2 tbsp cornmeal into the 420 g / 1¾ cups buttermilk in a large bowl and let it stand 15 minutes. The coarse cornmeal particles absorb water slowly; soaking softens them before they bake.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Heat the skillet. Put a 25 cm / 10 inch cast iron skillet on the middle or top rack and heat the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Brown the butter. In a small saucepan over medium heat, melt 70 g / 5 tbsp of the 90 g butter and cook it, swirling, until the milk solids turn amber and smell of toasted hazelnut, 3 to 4 minutes. Pour it into a cup so it stops cooking and let it cool 5 minutes.'
    technique: 'brown-butter'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Mix the dry ingredients. Whisk the 90 g / ¾ cup flour, 8 g / 2 tsp baking powder, 3 g / ½ tsp baking soda and 6 g / 1 tsp fine sea salt together in a small bowl.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Mix the batter. Whisk the 100 g / 2 eggs and the 40 g / 2 tbsp honey into the soaked cornmeal, then whisk in the brown butter. Fold in the flour mixture with a spatula until only a few dry streaks remain, then fold in 90 g / 1 cup of the cheddar, the 60 g / 2 minced jalapeños and the 150 g / 1 cup corn, just until no dry flour shows. The buttermilk acid reacts with the baking soda at once, so work without stopping from here.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Bake. Pull out the hot skillet, add the remaining 20 g / 1½ tbsp butter and swirl it to coat the bottom and sides. Pour in the batter; it should sizzle at the edges. Scatter the remaining 30 g / ⅓ cup cheddar over the top. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until the top is golden, the edges pull away from the pan and a skewer in the center comes out clean.'
    time_min: 23
  - text: 'Rest and serve. Sprinkle the 1 g / ¼ tsp flaky salt over the top and rest the cornbread in the skillet 10 minutes, so the crumb sets. Cut it into 8 wedges and serve it from the skillet. Taste a wedge from the center: it should taste of corn and butter first, then cheese and a little heat, and be only faintly sweet.'
    time_min: 10

notes: |
  Why a preheated skillet. Cast iron holds a lot of heat. Batter poured into a skillet at 220 °C fries in the butter on contact, which gives a crisp, browned bottom and edges. In a cold pan the batter sets slowly and the outside stays pale and soft.

  Why the batter is mixed only briefly. Flour develops gluten when it is wet and stirred. A cornbread mixed like a cake turns tough and full of tunnels, so the flour goes in last and is folded only until it disappears.

  Why buttermilk and baking soda. Buttermilk is acidic. The baking soda reacts with the acid to make carbon dioxide, which lifts the bread, and it neutralizes some of the acid, so the crumb browns better and tastes less sour. The baking powder adds lift that keeps working in the oven.
