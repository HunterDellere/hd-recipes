steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 350°F (177°C) with a rack centered. Butter two 4-inch round pans with 1 tsp / 5 g of the butter, line the bottoms with parchment, and dust the walls with a little Dutch-process cocoa instead of flour so the sides bake without a pale seam.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, warm the flat stout (6 tbsp / 90 g) with the remaining butter (5½ tbsp / 85 g) until the butter melts and the liquid steams, about 3 minutes; do not boil, which drives off the stout''s volatile hop and roast aromatics. Off heat, whisk in the Dutch-process cocoa (¼ cup + 1 tbsp / 25 g), granulated sugar (½ cup / 100 g), and dark brown sugar (3 tbsp / 35 g) until smooth and glossy. The hot liquid blooms the cocoa, and the stout''s roasted-barley compounds stack onto the cocoa''s so the bitterness registers as chocolate depth rather than beer. Cool 5 minutes, until warm but not hot to the touch.'
    technique: 'blooming-cocoa'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'In a small bowl, whisk the sour cream (3½ tbsp / 50 g), the egg (1 large / 50 g), and vanilla bean paste (¾ tsp / 4 g) until smooth, then whisk into the cocoa mixture. The sour cream brings lactic acid for the soda and milk fat for a close, damp crumb.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Whisk the all-purpose flour (¾ cup / 90 g), baking soda (½ tsp / 3 g), and fine sea salt (¼ tsp / 1.5 g) together, then add to the wet and whisk only until no dry streaks remain. The batter is loose and shiny. Overmixing develops gluten and tunnels the crumb.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Divide evenly between the pans, about 255 g each, and tap each once to release large bubbles. Bake 24 to 28 minutes, until the centers spring back and a tester pulls out with a few moist crumbs, or the center reads 205 to 208°F (96 to 98°C). The stout''s sugars and the dark brown sugar brown fast, so a dark top is normal; judge by the center, not the color.'
    time_min: 26
  - text: 'Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and peel off the parchment. Cool completely, at least 1 hour.'
    time_min: 70
  - text: 'While the layers cool, make the frosting. Beat the room-temperature cream cheese (4 oz / 115 g) with a hand mixer until smooth and lump-free, about 30 seconds. Beat in the sifted powdered sugar (scant ½ cup / 50 g), a pinch of fine sea salt (0.5 g), and the vanilla bean paste (½ tsp / 2 g). Then, on medium speed, stream in the cold heavy cream (¼ cup / 60 g) and beat until it thickens and holds a soft peak, 1 to 2 minutes. The whipped cream lightens the cheese into a foam that looks and eats like the head on a pint; stop at soft peaks, since overbeaten cream-cheese frosting turns grainy and weeps.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Level any dome off the layers with a serrated knife. Set the first layer cut-side up and spread about a third of the frosting to the edge. Set the second layer on top, cut-side down, and pile the remaining frosting on top, swooping it just over the rim so it drips slightly. Leave the sides bare: the dark crumb under a pale cap is the point.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Chill 20 minutes to firm the frosting before slicing. Cut with a hot, dry knife.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'The Adjustment: if the layers domed and cracked, the oven runs hot or the pans were overfilled; drop to 325°F (163°C) and bake a few minutes longer. If the crumb tastes soapy or metallic, the soda was measured heavy; level the ½ tsp exactly. If the beer reads too bitter, the stout was a hoppy American style rather than a dry Irish one. If the frosting is runny, the cream cheese was tub-style or too warm; chill 15 minutes and rebeat briefly.'

notes: |
  What the stout is doing. Dry Irish stout is brewed with roasted unmalted barley, which carries the same pyrazine and furan roast compounds as coffee and cocoa. Used as the liquid, it plays the role coffee plays in most chocolate cakes: it amplifies the chocolate without announcing itself. The alcohol mostly bakes off; what stays is roast, a faint malt sweetness, and a mild acidity that helps the soda. Warm it gently with the butter rather than boiling, so the aromatics survive into the batter.

  Why Dutch cocoa and soda together. The cocoa is alkalized, so the soda reacts against the stout, sour cream, and dark brown sugar instead. Half a teaspoon is enough for 90 g of flour. The original deep 9-inch cake is soda-heavy by design, which is fine in a tall pan but domes and cracks small layers; pulling it back keeps the 4-inch layers flat enough to stack without much trimming.

  Split sugar. Dark brown sugar''s molasses holds water in the crumb and echoes the stout''s caramel-malt notes. All-granulated reads lighter and drier; all-brown turns the crumb heavy.

  The frosting is the pint head. Cream cheese alone is dense and sweet; whipping cold heavy cream into it at the end aerates it into a light, tangy foam that contrasts the dense, bitter crumb. Sugar is kept low on purpose, because the lactic tang is what cuts the chocolate. Bare sides make the cake read as a pint of stout: dark body, pale cap.

  Storage. Refrigerate covered up to 3 days because of the cream cheese; let slices sit 20 minutes before eating so the butter crumb softens.
