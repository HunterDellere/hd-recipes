steps:
  - text: 'Break the 40 g / 3 tbsp tamarind pulp into a bowl and pour over the 150 g / ⅔ cup hot water. Soak 5 minutes, then squeeze and mash the pulp with your fingers until the fibers come clean. Press through a sieve into a small saucepan, scraping the underside; discard the fibers and any seeds. You should have about 160 g of thick brown tamarind water.'
    technique: 'tamarind-souring'
    time_min: 7
  - text: 'Add the 45 g / 3 tbsp palm sugar and bring to a simmer over medium heat. Reduce, stirring, 4 to 5 minutes, until it loses about a third of its volume and coats a spoon like thin syrup. Reduction concentrates the tartaric acid and fruit esters together with the sugar, so the sourness reads round rather than sharp.'
    technique: 'reduction'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Meanwhile, warm the 15 g / 1 tbsp neutral oil in a small skillet over medium-low and add the 12 g / 3 cloves minced garlic. Fry, stirring, 1½ to 2 minutes, until pale gold and fragrant. Pull it the moment it colors evenly; carryover heat finishes it, and dark garlic turns bitter in the sauce.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Take the syrup off the heat. Stir in the 30 g / 2 tbsp fish sauce, the fried garlic and its oil, and the 6 g / 2 to 3 sliced Thai chilies. Adding fish sauce off heat keeps its aroma from cooking off into a flat, stale saltiness.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Cool to warm and taste on a piece of shellfish. It should be sweet-sour first, then salty, with garlic and chili behind. If too thick, loosen with 10 g / 2 tsp warm water; if too sweet, 5 g / 1 tsp more fish sauce. Serve warm or at room temperature.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Why tamarind for shellfish. Crab, snail, and shrimp are sweet and mineral. Tartaric acid in tamarind is gentler and fruitier than the citric acid in lime, and the reduced sugar gives the sauce enough body to cling to shells. A lime-based sauce reads sharper and runs off.

  Garlic. Frying the garlic is what separates nuoc mam me from a simple tamarind dip. The browned garlic adds a toasty, savory note that bridges the fruit and the fish sauce.

  Storage. Refrigerated in a clean jar, keeps 1 week. It sets thick when cold; warm briefly or loosen with a splash of warm water. Off smells (yeasty, alcoholic) mean it has fermented: discard.
