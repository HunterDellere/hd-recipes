before_you_start:
  - 'Soak the rice sticks 60 minutes ahead in room-temperature water. Warm or boiling water softens the outside before the core hydrates, and those noodles turn gummy and break in the wok. Soaked and drained noodles hold at room temperature for up to an hour.'
  - 'Cook one portion at a time. A home burner cannot keep a wok hot enough for two portions of noodles; doubled batches steam, clump, and go soft. Lay out two identical sets of mise (tofu, aromatics, shrimp, egg, sprouts, chives, sauce) before lighting the burner.'
  - 'Have both plates, the peanuts, and the lime wedges at the stove. Each batch takes about 5 minutes and is best eaten within a few minutes of leaving the wok.'

steps:
  - text: 'Soak the noodles. Cover the 160 g rice sticks with plenty of room-temperature water and soak 60 minutes, until limp and bendable but still opaque white with a firm, slightly chalky core when bitten. Drain well. They will finish cooking in the wok on sauce and steam; noodles that are fully soft now will be overcooked by the end.'
    time_min: 60
  - text: 'Make the sauce. Pour the 60 ml / ¼ cup hot water over the 30 g tamarind pulp, soak 10 minutes, then mash and push through a fine-mesh sieve, scraping the underside, to get about 60 g of thick brown purée. Discard the seeds and fiber. Combine the purée with the 40 g / 3 tbsp shaved palm sugar and 36 g / 2 tbsp fish sauce in a small saucepan and simmer 2 minutes, stirring, until the sugar dissolves and the sauce coats a spoon. Taste it: sour, sweet, and salty should all be loud and roughly equal, because the noodles and egg will mute it. Adjust with 5 g / 1 tsp fish sauce or palm sugar if one side is missing. You will have about 130 g; each batch gets half.'
    technique: 'tamarind-souring'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Set up two batches. For each batch, portion into small bowls: 60 g tofu, 125 g shrimp, 15 g shallot with 8 g garlic, 15 g radish with 8 g dried shrimp, 1 egg, 60 g sprouts with 25 g garlic chives, and 65 g sauce. Pat the shrimp dry; wet shrimp spatter and stew rather than sear.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Batch one, tofu and shrimp. Heat a wok over the highest heat until a drop of water vanishes on contact. Add 30 g / 2 tbsp oil, then the 60 g tofu, and fry 2 minutes, turning, until golden on most sides. Add the 125 g shrimp in one layer and sear 30 seconds per side until barely opaque. Lift the shrimp out onto a plate (they finish later); leave the tofu in.'
    technique: 'maillard-sear'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Aromatics. Lower the heat to medium-high. Add the 15 g shallot and 8 g garlic with the 15 g preserved radish and 8 g dried shrimp and stir-fry 30 seconds, until the garlic is pale gold and the radish smells caramelized. Do not let the garlic brown past gold; it turns bitter fast in this little oil.'
    technique: 'blooming-aromatics'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Noodles and sauce. Return the heat to high. Add half the drained noodles (about 80 g dry weight) and 30 ml / 2 tbsp water and toss with a metal spatula and tongs for 1 minute, lifting and turning, until the noodles soften and turn translucent at the edges. Add the 65 g sauce and keep tossing until it is absorbed and the noodles are chewy-tender with no hard core, about 1 to 2 minutes. If they are still firm but the pan is dry, add another 15 ml / 1 tbsp water. The noodles should look glossy and tan-brown, never wet.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Egg. Push the noodles to one side. Add a few drops of oil to the empty space if it looks dry, crack in 1 egg, and break the yolk. Let it set 20 seconds, scramble it loosely, then fold the noodles over it and toss so the egg breaks into ribbons that cling to the noodles.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Finish. Return the 125 g seared shrimp, add the 60 g bean sprouts and 25 g garlic chives, and toss 30 seconds, only until the chives wilt and the sprouts warm through but still crunch. Taste a strand: if it reads flat, a splash of 5 g / 1 tsp fish sauce; if too sour, a pinch of palm sugar. Plate immediately.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Serve. Top the plate with 15 g / 2 tbsp crushed peanuts, 40 g raw bean sprouts, half the lime wedges, and a pinch of the 2 g / 1 tsp chili flakes if wanted. Squeeze the lime over at the table; it supplies the bright top note that the deeper tamarind acid does not. Wipe the wok, then repeat steps 4 to 9 for the second batch.'
    time_min: 6

notes: |
  Why the color is brown. Real pad thai sauce is tamarind, palm sugar, and fish sauce, which reduce to a glossy tan-brown glaze. The orange-red color of many restaurant versions comes from ketchup or paprika, which adds tomato sweetness and masks the tamarind. If the noodles look red, the sauce has drifted.

  Why soak and not boil. Rice sticks are made from rice starch with no gluten to hold them together. Boiling gelatinizes the outside fast while the core is still dry, so by the time the center is tender the surface is slick and the strands tear. A room-temperature soak hydrates the noodle evenly; the wok then finishes the gelatinization with sauce and a little water, so the noodle absorbs flavor instead of plain water.

  Why one portion at a time. A home wok on a residential burner loses heat quickly when 250 g of wet noodles hit it. Two portions at once drop the pan below the temperature that drives off moisture, so the noodles steam, clump, and the sauce pools. One portion keeps the pan hot enough to toss the noodles dry and glossy in about 5 minutes.

  Balancing the sauce. Taste the sauce before it meets the noodles and make it a little more intense than you want the dish, because starch, egg, and sprouts dilute it. The finished plate should taste sour-sweet first, then salty and savory from the fish sauce and dried shrimp, with lime and chili added by each eater. Fish sauce and dried shrimp carry the glutamate; tamarind is the main acid, with lime as the bright finish.

  Shrimp doneness. Shrimp are seared briefly, removed, and returned at the end so they finish at just-opaque (about 63 °C / 145 °F inside). Left in the wok through the noodle stage, they turn rubbery.
