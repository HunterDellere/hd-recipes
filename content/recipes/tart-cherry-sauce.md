steps:
  - text: 'Cook the cherries. Put the 450 g / 16 oz frozen tart cherries, the 100 g / ½ cup granulated sugar, the 60 g / ¼ cup water and the 1 g / ¼ tsp kosher salt in a medium heavy saucepan over medium heat. Stir with a wooden spoon as the cherries thaw and the sugar dissolves. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes, stirring every minute or two, until the cherries are soft and slumped and sit in plenty of red juice. Press a few against the side of the pan with the spoon to break them, so the sauce has some body.'
    technique: 'simmering'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Thicken. Stir the 8 g / 1 tbsp cornstarch into the 30 g / 2 tbsp cold water in a small cup until smooth. Pour it into the simmering cherries, stirring, and let the sauce boil gently for 1 full minute, until it turns from cloudy to clear and glossy. Cornstarch thickens only once it nears boiling, and a full minute at a boil removes its raw, starchy taste. Cherries have little pectin, so without the starch the juice stays thin.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Finish. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the 15 g / 1 tbsp butter and the 2 g / ½ tsp vanilla bean paste until the butter melts. The butter softens the edge of the acid and makes the sauce shine; the vanilla rounds out the cherry flavor without tasting of dessert.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Taste and store. It should taste sweet first, then gently tart, like a good cherry pie filling but looser. If it tastes too sharp, stir in granulated sugar 5 g / 1 tsp at a time while it is still warm. Cool it, transfer it to a glass jar and refrigerate up to 5 days. Before serving, stir in warm water 15 g / 1 tbsp at a time if it has set too firm.'
    time_min: 3

notes: |
  Why tart cherries and not sweet ones. Tart cherries have about twice the acid of dark sweet cherries, so once sweetened they taste of cherry rather than only sugar. That acid is also what lets the sauce cut through rich meat.

  Why so little water. Frozen cherries release a lot of juice as they thaw and cook. The 60 g of water only stops the sugar scorching before the juice comes out.
