safety_notes:
  - for: 'Pulling the beef at 48 °C / 118 °F and resting to about 54 °C / 130 °F (below the USDA published-safe 145 °F for whole-muscle beef)'
    ref: 'safety/meat-doneness#beef-and-lamb-whole-muscle'
    why: 'The USDA published-safe target for whole-muscle beef is 145 °F (63 °C) followed by a 3-minute rest. A tenderloin is an intact whole muscle, and the contamination that matters sits on its surface, which is seared hard before wrapping; the interior is served medium-rare in the classic dish. For pregnancy, immunocompromise, the very young or the very old, bake to 57 °C / 135 °F and rest to about 63 °C / 145 °F; the pastry will be darker, so cover it loosely with foil once it is golden.'

before_you_start:
  - 'Spread the work over two days. Day 1: dry-brine and sear the beef, make the duxelles, wrap the beef in prosciutto and duxelles, and make the jus base. Day 2: wrap in pastry, chill 30 minutes, bake 35 to 45 minutes, and rest 10.'
  - 'Order a center-cut tenderloin (chateaubriand) of even thickness, about 9 cm / 3½ inches across, trimmed of silverskin. An even cylinder cooks evenly; a tapered piece overcooks at the thin end.'
  - 'Keep everything cold. Warm duxelles or a warm wrapped beef melts the butter in the pastry before it reaches the oven, and the pastry will not puff.'
  - 'An instant-read thermometer is not optional here. The pastry hides every visual cue for doneness.'

steps:
  - text: 'Dry-brine the beef (day 1, morning, or the night before). Pat the 1400 g / 3 lb tenderloin dry and season it on all sides with the 12 g / 4¼ tsp kosher salt. Set it on a rack over a sheet pan and refrigerate uncovered at least 8 hours and up to 24. The salt seasons the meat through and dries the surface, so the sear browns fast without cooking the outer layer gray.'
    technique: 'dry-brining'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Make the duxelles. Pulse the 700 g / 1½ lb cremini in a food processor in two batches until they are chopped to the size of rice, not a paste. Melt the 30 g / 2 tbsp duxelles butter in a 30 cm / 12 inch skillet over medium heat, add the 60 g / 2 minced shallots, and sweat 2 minutes. Add the mushrooms and the 2 g / ¾ tsp duxelles salt and cook, stirring with a wooden spoon, until all the released water has evaporated and the mushrooms start to stick and brown, 25 to 30 minutes. Add the 8 g / 2 cloves garlic and 3 g / 1 tbsp thyme for 1 minute, then the 45 g / 3 tbsp dry sherry, and cook until the pan is dry again, about 2 minutes.'
    technique: 'sweating'
    time_min: 30
  - text: 'Cool the duxelles completely. Spread it on a plate and refrigerate it until cold, at least 30 minutes. It should hold together when pressed and leave no wet mark on the plate. Any water left in it turns to steam inside the pastry and makes the bottom soggy, so if it looks wet, cook it longer.'
    time_min: 30
  - text: 'Sear the beef. Pat the tenderloin dry and season it with the 3 g / 1½ tsp black pepper. Heat a 36 cm / 14 inch cast-iron skillet or a griddle over high heat for 4 minutes (in a 30 cm pan, curve the beef slightly and turn it end for end halfway through each side), add the 15 g / 1 tbsp neutral oil, and sear the beef 1 to 1½ minutes per side, including the ends, until deep brown all over, about 8 minutes in total. Move it to a rack, let it cool 10 minutes, cut off the string, and brush it all over with the 30 g / 2 tbsp dijon mustard. Refrigerate it uncovered until cold, at least 30 minutes.'
    technique: 'maillard-sear'
    time_min: 50
  - text: 'Wrap in prosciutto and duxelles. Lay a 60 cm / 24 inch sheet of parchment on the counter. Shingle the 150 g / 10 to 12 slices prosciutto on it, overlapping by 1 cm, into a rectangle as long as the beef and wide enough to wrap around it. Spread all of the cold duxelles over the prosciutto in an even 2 to 3 mm layer, leaving a 2 cm border. Set the beef along the near edge and roll it up tightly using the parchment to lift and push, peeling the paper away as you go. Wrap the log in the parchment, then roll it tightly in a clean tea towel and tie the towel with kitchen twine at 3 points to compress it into an even cylinder. Refrigerate at least 1 hour, or overnight. The prosciutto is the moisture barrier between the duxelles and the pastry.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Make the jus base (day 1). In a 3 L saucepan over medium heat, sweat the 60 g / 2 sliced shallots in 10 g of the 40 g / 3 tbsp jus butter until soft, 4 minutes. Stir in the 15 g / 1 tbsp tomato paste and cook 2 minutes, until it darkens to brick red. Add the 375 g / 1½ cups red wine and the 3 g / 4 thyme sprigs and boil until reduced by two thirds, about 12 minutes. Add the 500 g / 2 cups chicken stock and simmer until reduced to about 250 g / 1 cup, 25 to 35 minutes. Strain, cool, and refrigerate.'
    technique: 'reduction'
    time_min: 45
  - text: 'Wrap in pastry (day 2). Dust the counter with some of the 15 g / 2 tbsp flour. Overlap the two sheets of the 450 g / 1 lb cold puff pastry by 2 cm along a long edge, press the seam, and roll it to a rectangle about 3 mm thick, 35 cm / 14 inches in the direction that wraps around the beef and 40 cm / 16 inches along it. Unwrap the beef, set it on the near edge, and roll it up in the pastry so the seam ends underneath, trimming excess so the overlap is no more than 3 cm (a thick double layer stays raw). Fold the ends under and trim them. Set it seam-side down on a parchment-lined sheet and refrigerate 30 minutes, until the pastry is firm.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Heat the oven and the baking sheet. Put a second rimmed sheet pan in the oven on the middle rack and heat it to 220 °C / 425 °F for 30 minutes. Landing on a hot pan sets the bottom pastry before the juices can soak into it.'
    time_min: 30
  - text: 'Egg wash and score. Beat the 36 g / 2 egg yolks with the 15 g / 1 tbsp cream. Brush the pastry all over, chill 5 minutes, and brush again for a deeper color. Score a shallow crosshatch with the back of a paring knife without cutting through, and sprinkle with the 2 g / ½ tsp flaky salt.'
    time_min: 8
  - text: 'Bake. Lift the Wellington by the edges of its parchment onto the hot sheet pan. Bake 35 to 50 minutes, until the pastry is deep golden-brown and an instant-read thermometer pushed into the center of the beef through the end reads 48 °C / 118 °F for medium-rare. Start checking at 30 minutes. If the pastry browns before the beef is ready, lay foil loosely over the top.'
    time_min: 40
  - text: 'Finish the jus while it bakes. The cold jus base will have set to a jelly. Bring it to a simmer to melt it. Off the heat, whisk in the remaining 30 g / 2 tbsp cold jus butter a piece at a time until glossy, then the 5 g / 1 tsp sherry vinegar. Taste and add up to 1 g / ¼ tsp kosher salt. Keep warm, not boiling, or the butter separates.'
    technique: 'pan-emulsion'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Rest and slice. Rest the Wellington on a rack 10 minutes; the center climbs to about 54 °C / 130 °F. Slice it with a serrated knife into 3 cm / 1¼ inch slices, using a gentle sawing motion so the pastry does not crush. Serve with the jus.'
    time_min: 12

notes: |
  Why 48 °C: the pastry traps heat, so carryover during the 10-minute rest is larger than for a bare roast, about 6 °C. Pulling at 48 °C lands at medium-rare; pulling at 52 °C, the usual bare-roast number, ends near medium.

  Why the duxelles must be dry: about 90% of a mushroom is water. Cooked until it sticks to the pan, the duxelles adds flavor; cooked only until soft, it adds steam that soaks the pastry from the inside.
