steps:
  - text: 'Soften the shallots. Warm the 15 g / 1 tbsp olive oil in a medium heavy saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the 100 g / 2 large sliced shallots and the 2 g / ¾ tsp kosher salt and cook, stirring every minute or two with a wooden spoon, until they are completely soft and golden at the edges, 10 to 12 minutes. Slow heat converts their sharp sulfur compounds into sweet, savory ones; this is the savory base that separates the agrodolce from a plain cranberry sauce. If they brown before they soften, lower the heat and add 15 g / 1 tbsp water.'
    technique: 'sweating'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Build the syrup. Add the 1 rosemary sprig and stir for 30 seconds, until it smells piney. Add the 120 g / ½ cup packed light brown sugar, the 90 g / 6 tbsp red wine vinegar, the 90 g / 6 tbsp water and the 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper, raise the heat to medium and stir until the sugar dissolves and the liquid simmers. Lean over the pan briefly: the vinegar fumes should soften after a minute of simmering.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Cook the berries. Add the 340 g / 12 oz cranberries and the orange zest (about 3 g / 1 tbsp). Simmer over medium heat, stirring every minute, until about half the berries have burst and the liquid thickens to a loose syrup that coats the spoon, 8 to 10 minutes. Cranberries are rich in pectin, and the sugar and acid let it set as the sauce cools, so stop while it still looks a little loose.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Finish off the heat. Lift out the rosemary sprig and stir in the orange juice (about 60 g / ¼ cup). Cold juice added at the end keeps the orange aroma that simmering drives off.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Taste and store. It should taste sweet first, then cleanly sour, with shallot and rosemary at the end. If it tastes sharp, stir in light brown sugar 5 g / 1 tsp at a time; if it tastes flat or only sweet, add red wine vinegar 5 g / 1 tsp at a time. Cool it 30 minutes, then transfer it to a glass jar, lid it and refrigerate at least 4 hours, up to 7 days.'
    time_min: 240

notes: |
  Why agrodolce works with rich meat. Fat coats the palate, and acid and sugar together cut through it without the heat of mustard or chile. The shallots add savory depth, so the relish reads as part of the main course rather than as a dessert on the plate.

  Why the vinegar is cut with water. Straight vinegar at this volume would make the relish taste harsh on its own. Equal parts vinegar and water, plus the natural acid of the cranberries, give a sour edge that a guest notices without wincing.

  Why the rosemary comes out. Rosemary left in for a week turns bitter and medicinal. A sprig in the hot syrup gives its aroma in a few minutes, and lifting it out keeps the flavor in the background.
