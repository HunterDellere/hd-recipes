before_you_start:
  - 'Start the broth first. It needs 45 to 60 minutes of simmering, and everything else (marinating the thighs, cutting and drying the tortilla strips, frying) fits inside that window.'
  - 'The chipotle thighs need at least 30 minutes in the marinade. Overnight in the fridge is better: the salt diffuses through the whole thigh instead of seasoning only the outer few millimetres.'
  - 'Fresh tortillas need 20 minutes spread on a rack to surface-dry before frying. Cut them as soon as the broth is simmering.'

steps:
  - text: 'Char the aromatics. Set a 6 to 7 L Dutch oven over medium-high heat for 2 minutes. Lay 3 quarters of the 250 g white onion and both halves of the 50 g garlic head cut side down in the dry pot and leave them for 4 to 5 minutes, until the cut faces are blackened in patches. Set the fourth onion quarter aside raw for the purée. The char adds roasted, faintly bitter pyrazine notes that a plain simmer cannot produce, and the browned residue on the pot floor dissolves into the broth.'
    technique: 'comal-charring'
    time_min: 7
  - text: 'Simmer the broth. Add the 1100 g bone-in chicken thighs, 2400 ml / 10 cups water, and 10 g / 2½ tsp kosher salt. Bring to a bare simmer over medium-high, then lower the heat so only a few bubbles break the surface. Skim the grey foam that rises during the first 10 minutes. Simmer partially covered for 45 to 60 minutes, until the meat pulls easily from the bone. Keep it below a boil: rolling agitation emulsifies the rendered fat into the liquid and the broth turns cloudy and greasy. Salting now seasons the meat as it cooks rather than only the liquid around it.'
    time_min: 60
  - text: 'Marinate the chipotle chicken. While the broth simmers, stir together the 40 g minced chipotle in adobo, 10 g grated garlic, 1 g / 1 tsp crumbled Mexican oregano, 7 g / 1¾ tsp kosher salt, and 15 g / 1 tbsp neutral oil in a bowl. Add the 700 g boneless thighs and turn to coat every surface. Leave at room temperature for 30 minutes, or cover and refrigerate up to 24 hours (pull it out 30 minutes before searing).'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Cut and dry the tortillas. Set 2 of the 10 fresh corn tortillas aside whole. Stack the other 8 tortillas, halve the stack, and cut it into 6 mm / ¼ inch strips. Spread the strips and the 2 whole tortillas in a single layer on a wire rack and leave them uncovered for 20 minutes, until the surfaces feel dry and slightly leathery. Fresh tortillas are close to half water; frying them straight from the package makes the oil boil violently, the strips take longer to crisp, and they soak up more oil. In a hurry, dry them on a sheet pan in a 120°C / 250°F oven for 8 minutes instead.'
    time_min: 20
  - text: 'Fry the tortillas. Heat the 240 ml / 1 cup neutral oil in a 25 cm / 10 inch cast-iron or heavy skillet to 175°C / 350°F. Fry the 2 whole tortillas one at a time, 45 to 60 seconds per side, until rigid and golden; drain on a rack. Fry the strips in 3 batches, stirring with a spider, for 90 seconds to 2 minutes each. The cue is the sound and the bubbles: the vigorous fizz of escaping water quiets to small lazy bubbles when the strips are done. Lift them onto the rack and sprinkle with the 2 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt while still hot, so it sticks. Pour off all but 30 g / 2 tbsp of the oil into a heatproof jar and set the skillet aside unwashed for the chicken.'
    technique: 'deep-fry'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Shred the chicken and strain the broth. Lift the bone-in thighs onto a plate and let them cool 10 minutes. Strain the broth through a fine-mesh sieve into a large bowl and discard the onion and garlic. You should have about 2 L. Pull the meat into bite-size shreds, discarding the skin and bones.'
    time_min: 12
  - text: 'Blend the soup base. In a blender, combine the reserved raw onion quarter from the 250 g white onion, the 30 g chipotle in adobo, the 10 g / ¼ cup cilantro, the 2 fried whole tortillas broken into pieces, and 500 ml / 2 cups of the hot broth. Remove the centre cap of the blender lid and cover the hole with a folded towel so steam can escape, then blend on high for 60 seconds until completely smooth. The fried tortillas carry nixtamalized corn starch that gelatinizes in the hot liquid, which gives the soup body and a toasted-corn flavor without any flour or cream.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Fry the purée. Wipe out the Dutch oven and heat the reserved 30 g / 2 tbsp frying oil over medium-high until it shimmers. Pour in the purée (it will spatter) and cook, stirring constantly, for 4 to 5 minutes, until it darkens a shade, thickens to a paste, and smells toasted rather than raw. Frying drives off water and browns the chile and corn solids, which deepens the flavor in a way simmering cannot. Whisk in the rest of the broth, bring to a simmer, and cook 10 minutes. Stir in the shredded chicken.'
    time_min: 16
  - text: 'Sear the chipotle chicken. While the soup simmers, set the oiled skillet over medium-high heat for 2 minutes. Lay the 700 g marinated thighs in it smooth side down and leave them undisturbed for 5 to 6 minutes, until the edges char. The sugars and tomato in the adobo burn faster than bare chicken, so turn the heat to medium if the surface goes black before 4 minutes. Flip and cook 4 to 5 minutes more, to 80°C / 175°F at the thickest point; thigh meat keeps getting more tender up to that temperature as its connective tissue softens. Rest 5 minutes, then cut into 2 cm pieces. Pour a ladle of the soup into the hot skillet, scrape up the browned bits, and pour it back into the pot.'
    technique: 'maillard-sear'
    time_min: 16
  - text: 'Season and finish. Taste the soup and stir in the 4 g / 1 tsp kosher salt, a little at a time, until the broth tastes fully seasoned on its own; the toppings are lightly salted and will not fix a flat base. Turn off the heat and stir in the 20 g / 1 lime of lime juice. Acid goes in off the heat so its fresh aroma does not cook away.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Assemble the bowls. Divide the 170 g pulled Oaxaca cheese and the 300 g cubed avocado among 6 wide bowls. Ladle the hot soup over them; the heat melts the cheese into strands. Top each bowl with the chopped chipotle chicken, a large handful of the tortilla strips, the 80 g sliced radishes, a spoonful of the 120 g Mexican crema, and the 15 g chopped cilantro. Serve the 2 limes of lime wedges at the table. Add the strips last and keep the extras in a bowl on the side: they soften within a few minutes in the broth, and each person adds more as they eat.'
    time_min: 5

notes: |
  Two kinds of chicken, two jobs. The poached bone-in thighs exist to make the broth: their collagen turns to gelatin over the long simmer, and the shredded meat goes into the pot soft and broth-soaked. The seared chipotle thighs exist for the bowl: they bring charred, smoky, concentrated flavor that poached meat cannot have. Simmering the seared pieces in the soup would wash the crust off, which is why they go on top at the end.

  Why the tortillas go in twice. Blended fried tortillas thicken the soup and give it a toasted-corn base; the strips on top are texture. Both come from the same oil, and the 2 tbsp of tortilla-flavored oil left in the skillet is what the purée fries in.

  Fresh tortillas versus stale. Traditional cooks fry day-old tortillas because they are drier. Fresh tortillas taste better but carry more water, which is the whole reason for the 20-minute rack dry: it removes enough surface moisture that the strips crisp in under 2 minutes instead of turning chewy and greasy.

  Storage. The soup (without toppings) keeps 4 days in the fridge and freezes for 3 months. Store the chipotle chicken separately and reheat it in a hot skillet, not in the soup. Tortilla strips keep 2 days at room temperature in a sealed glass jar; re-crisp them for 3 minutes in a 175°C / 350°F oven if they soften.
