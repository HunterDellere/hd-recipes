steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven and pans. Set racks in the upper and lower thirds of the oven, put two rimmed sheet pans on them and heat the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F.'
    time_min: 15
  - text: 'Toast the cumin. Toast the 6 g / 2 tsp cumin seeds in a dry skillet over medium heat, shaking the pan, until a shade darker and fragrant, about 90 seconds. Crush them coarsely in a mortar. Toasting drives off moisture and develops the roasted aroma compounds that raw cumin lacks.'
    technique: 'dry-toasting'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Season the carrots. In a large bowl, toss the 1200 g / 2½ lb carrots with the 30 g / 2 tbsp olive oil, the crushed cumin, the 8 g / 2¾ tsp kosher salt and the 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper. The salt draws a little surface moisture that evaporates in the oven, so the surface sugars concentrate and caramelize.'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Roast. Spread the carrots cut side down in a single layer across the two hot pans, with space between the pieces. Roast 22 to 28 minutes, swapping the pans halfway, until the undersides are deeply browned and a knife meets slight resistance at the thickest point. Crowded carrots steam and taste boiled.'
    technique: 'high-heat-dry-roasting'
    time_min: 25
  - text: 'Make the salsa verde while the carrots roast. Chop the 30 g / 1 cup parsley and 8 g / ⅓ cup mint finely with a sharp knife. Chop the 20 g / 2 tbsp capers, then mash the 8 g / 2 anchovy fillets and 4 g / 1 garlic clove to a paste on the board with the side of the knife. Stir everything together in a bowl with the lemon zest, the 10 g / 2 tsp red wine vinegar, the 80 g / ⅓ cup olive oil and the 1 g / ½ tsp red pepper flakes, if using. A knife bruises the herbs less than a blender, so they stay green and fresh-tasting.'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Dress warm. Transfer the hot carrots to a platter and spoon about half the salsa verde over them. Warm carrots absorb the oil and aromatics as they cool. They can sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Finish at serving time. Stir the lemon juice (about 30 g / 2 tbsp) into the remaining salsa verde, spoon it over the carrots and scatter the 1 g / ¼ tsp flaky salt. Taste a carrot: it should taste sweet from the roasting, then salty, herbal and sharp. If it tastes flat, add lemon juice before salt.'
    time_min: 2

notes: |
  Why savory carrots on a holiday table. Roasted carrots are already sweet: high heat caramelizes their sugars. A holiday plate usually has several sweet things on it, such as cranberry sauce and fruit in the salad. A savory, acidic carrot side balances the plate where a glazed one would add more sweetness.

  Why anchovy. Anchovy supplies glutamate and salt, which make the herbs and carrots taste deeper and more savory. Mashed to a paste with garlic it dissolves into the oil, and the fish flavor disappears.

  Why the lemon juice goes in last. Acid breaks down chlorophyll in cut herbs and turns them olive-brown. The vinegar is a small amount and is enough to season the sauce; the lemon juice goes in just before serving so the sauce stays bright green.
