steps:
  - text: 'Soften the onions. Melt the 30 g / 2 tbsp butter with the 15 g / 1 tbsp olive oil in a wide heavy pan over medium heat. Add the 1000 g / 4 large sliced onions and the 4 g / 1½ tsp kosher salt, and turn them with a wooden spoon to coat. Cover and cook 10 minutes, stirring twice, until they have collapsed and released their water. The salt draws water out of the onion cells, and the lid traps the steam, so the onions soften evenly before any browning starts.'
    technique: 'sweating'
    time_min: 10
  - text: 'Brown slowly. Uncover, lower the heat to medium-low and cook 45 to 55 minutes, stirring every 5 minutes, until the onions are soft, deep amber and reduced to about a quarter of their raw volume. When a brown film builds on the bottom of the pan, add a splash of the 120 g / ½ cup water (about 15 g / 1 tbsp) and scrape it up with the spoon. That film is concentrated onion sugar and browned flavor, and scraping it back in is what colors the onions evenly. If the edges darken before the centers soften, turn the heat down.'
    technique: 'deglazing'
    time_min: 50
  - text: 'Make the jam. Add the 50 g / ¼ cup packed light brown sugar and stir 3 minutes, until it melts and coats the onions. Add the 30 g / 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar, the 0.5 g / ¼ tsp black pepper and any water left from the 120 g, and simmer 4 to 6 minutes, stirring, until the liquid has cooked down to a thick glaze and a spoon drawn across the pan leaves a clear trail.'
    time_min: 9
  - text: 'Taste and store. It should taste sweet and savory first, with a soft tang at the end. If it tastes only sweet, add apple cider vinegar 5 g / 1 tsp at a time; if it tastes flat, add a pinch of kosher salt. Cool it, transfer it to a glass jar and refrigerate up to 7 days.'
    time_min: 3

notes: |
  Why it takes an hour. Onions brown through two slow processes: their sugars break down and darken (caramelization), and their sugars react with their amino acids (the Maillard reaction). Both need the water gone first and moderate heat after. High heat browns the outside of each slice while the inside stays crunchy and sharp.

  Why vinegar at the end. Without acid, an hour of concentrated onion sugar tastes heavy and one-note. A small amount of vinegar makes it taste like a condiment instead of a side dish, and it helps the jam keep for a week.
