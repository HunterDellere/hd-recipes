steps:
  - text: 'Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C) with a rack centered. Grease an 8-inch round cake pan with the butter for the pan (1 tsp / 5 g), line the bottom with a parchment circle, butter the parchment, and dust the pan with the cocoa for the pan (1 tsp / 3 g), tapping out the excess.'
    time_min: 5
  - text: 'Melt the chopped dark chocolate (6 oz / 170 g) and cubed unsalted butter (½ cup / 115 g) together in a large microwave-safe bowl in 30-second bursts, stirring between each, until just smooth, about 90 seconds total. Or melt over a pan of barely simmering water. Stop while a few small pieces remain and stir them out; above about 120°F (49°C) the chocolate risks scorching and turning grainy.'
    technique: 'chocolate-butter-emulsion'
    time_min: 3
  - text: 'Whisk the granulated sugar (⅔ cup / 135 g), fine sea salt (¼ tsp / 1.5 g), and vanilla bean paste (1 tsp / 5 g) into the warm chocolate. The sugar also cools the mixture slightly before the eggs go in.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Whisk in the eggs (3 large / 150 g) one at a time, whisking each until fully absorbed and glossy before adding the next. Whisk steadily but do not whip; you want a dense, silky batter, not an airy one. The mixture will thicken and turn shiny as the egg emulsifies the fat.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'Sift the Dutch-process cocoa (⅓ cup / 30 g) over the batter and whisk until fully combined. The cocoa replaces flour: its starch and fiber absorb liquid and give the cake enough body to slice.'
    time_min: 1
  - text: 'Scrape into the prepared pan and smooth the top. Bake 22 to 26 minutes, until the edges are set and slightly puffed, the top has formed a thin, dull crust, and the center jiggles only slightly when nudged. An instant-read thermometer in the center should read 165 to 175°F (74 to 79°C). Pull at the low end for a softer, more truffle-like center; the egg proteins have set enough by then and carryover finishes the job.'
    time_min: 24
  - text: 'Cool in the pan on a rack for 15 minutes, then run a thin knife around the edge and invert onto a plate. Peel off the parchment and invert again onto a serving plate so the crust is up. Cool completely, at least 1 hour, or chill overnight. The center sinks a little as it cools; that is normal.'
    time_min: 75
  - text: 'Sprinkle with flaky sea salt (1 pinch / 0.5 g) and slice with a knife dipped in hot water and wiped dry between cuts. Serve at room temperature; cold from the fridge, it is dense and firm, at room temperature it turns fudgy.'
    time_min: 2
  - text: 'The Adjustment: if the cake is dry and crumbly, it overbaked; pull it 3 to 4 minutes sooner and trust the jiggle. If it is wet and collapses when cut, it was underbaked or cut warm; chill it and it will firm up. If the batter looked grainy, the eggs were cold or the chocolate overheated; it will still bake fine but less smooth. If it reads too rich, slice thinner and serve with something tart, like berries.'

notes: |
  Where the structure comes from. With no flour, three things hold the cake together: egg proteins, which coagulate between about 145 and 170°F (63 and 77°C); cocoa butter, which solidifies as the cake cools; and the starch and fiber in the cocoa powder, which absorb water the way flour would. That is why the cake is soft and wobbly hot from the oven and firm after chilling, and why cutting it warm makes a mess.

  Why the eggs are not whipped. Whipped-egg flourless cakes rise like a soufflé and fall into a light, mousse-like texture. Stirring the eggs in one at a time instead keeps the cake dense and fudgy, needs no mixer, and is much more forgiving.

  Why it is better the next day. Overnight, the cocoa butter crystallizes fully and moisture evens out between the edge and the center, so the texture becomes uniform and the flavor rounder. It keeps covered in the fridge 5 days; bring slices to room temperature for 20 minutes before eating.
