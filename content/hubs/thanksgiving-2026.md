plan:
  - when: 'Decide by Tuesday'
    items:
      - 'Second dessert: the tres leches is baked on Wednesday. If you skip it, the crème fraîche is only needed for the crumble.'
      - 'Second sauce: choose the tart cherry sauce, the caramelized onion jam or the brown gravy from the Backups, or none. Make it Tuesday or Wednesday.'
  - when: 'Shopping notes'
    items:
      - 'Order the pork today or Monday: 1.4 kg / 3 lb of pulled smoked pork shoulder, unsauced, with the bark mixed in and the juices in the container. Barbecue restaurants take holiday orders days ahead and many sell out. Pick it up on Wednesday.'
      - 'Buy ground turkey from thigh meat or an 85/15 or 93/7 blend. Avoid 99% lean breast, which stays dry even with the gelatin.'
      - 'Buy the cream for the crème fraîche as pasteurized, not ultra-pasteurized, if your store has it, and check that the buttermilk says "live cultures".'
      - 'For the tres leches, buy a cinnamon stick for the soak, Ceylon (canela) if your store has it. '
  - when: 'For 6 adults and a child'
    items:
      - 'No recipe needs scaling. Every dish is written for 8, and with two mains on the table that leaves a margin of about a fifth for seconds and leftovers. 1.4 kg of pulled pork is about 200 g per adult beside the meatballs, so expect leftovers.'
      - 'For the child: the meatballs, gratin, carrots, pork and bread are the easy plate. None of the sauces has any heat. The pork vinegar sauce has only ½ tsp pepper flakes and can be made without them. The Wellington jus, the coq au vin and the beef braise are made with wine or beer, and cooking removes most but not all of the alcohol; the beef recipe has an alcohol-free version.'
  - when: 'If you cannot find it'
    items:
      - 'Smoked pork shoulder: if no restaurant can take the order, buy smoked pulled pork in tubs from a supermarket deli; if it is sauced, serve the vinegar sauce on the side. If there is none, make the braised beef chuck from the Backups.'
      - 'Ground turkey: ground chicken thigh, same weight and method.'
      - 'Fresh sage: dried rubbed sage, 3 g / 1¼ tbsp in the meatballs. Skip the fried-leaf garnish.'
      - 'Fresh cranberries: frozen cranberries work without thawing in both sauces. If there are none at all, make the tart cherry sauce instead.'
      - 'Frozen tart cherries: dried tart cherries, as the cherry sauce page says.'
      - 'Cinnamon stick: a cassia stick works; steep it only 15 minutes, as the tres leches page says.'
      - 'Pomegranate: 80 g / ½ cup dried cranberries or dried tart cherries in the salad.'
      - 'Ripe Bosc pears: a crisp, sweet apple such as Honeycrisp, or a Fuyu persimmon, sliced thin.'
      - 'Baby arugula: watercress, or a mix of frisée and baby spinach.'
      - 'Gruyère: Comté, Emmental or an aged white cheddar.'
      - 'Goat cheese: crumbled feta or a mild blue.'
      - 'Rainbow carrots: orange carrots, or half carrots and half parsnips.'
      - 'Medium-grind cornmeal: medium-grind polenta, not instant.'
      - 'Crème fraîche, if the home batch fails or there is no time: a store-bought tub, or sour cream for the same tang.'
      - 'Honeycrisp apples: Pink Lady, Braeburn, Jonagold or Gala. Keep the Granny Smith half.'
  - when: 'Using a backup'
    items:
      - 'Cider-glazed ribs (replace the pork): the oven method fits the menu best. Salt the racks Tuesday evening, bake them Wednesday at 135 °C / 275 °F after the cake, and refrigerate them in their foil. On Thursday, put the sealed packets on the middle rack of the 220 °C oven at 3:45 pm in place of the pork, and glaze them from about 4:25. Make the glaze on Tuesday.'
      - 'Braised beef chuck (replaces the pork, or adds a main): salt the beef Tuesday evening. Braise it Wednesday at 160 °C / 325 °F for 3 to 3½ hours, after the cake. Cool it in a shallow dish and refrigerate within 2 hours. On Thursday, brown the mushrooms at about 3:00 pm, then from 3:45 reheat the braise on its own burner over medium-low heat, 45 to 60 minutes, until the largest piece reads 74 °C / 165 °F. Hold it covered on low. At 4:45, stir in the mushrooms and vinegar and scatter the parsley.'
      - 'Coq au vin (adds a main, or replaces one that fails): decide by Tuesday evening. Marinate the chicken in the wine Tuesday evening, 12 to 24 hours before cooking, then braise it Wednesday, before or after the tres leches. Cool it in shallow dishes within 2 hours and refrigerate. On Thursday at about 3:45 pm, reheat it on its own burner over medium-low heat, 45 to 60 minutes, stirring from the bottom every 10 minutes, until the chicken reads 74 °C / 165 °F, then hold it covered on low. Add the garnish just before serving, as the recipe says.'
      - 'Turkey breast (replaces the pork): salt it Wednesday between 3:30 and 9:15 pm and refrigerate it uncovered on a rack (18 to 24 hours). On Thursday, start the gratin at 1:00 pm instead of 12:45. Take the turkey out at 2:40 pm, and at 3:00 pm make the herb butter and work it under the skin. When the carrots come out at about 3:30, roast the turkey 20 minutes at 220 °C / 425 °F, then at 165 °C / 325 °F to 65 °C inside, mixing the glaze meanwhile. Glaze and roast to 71 °C, about 4:35. Rest it 20 minutes. As it comes out, raise the oven to 220 °C and re-crisp the gratin on the top rack. Bake the cornbread before the turkey goes in, or choose the rolls. Dinner is at about 5:05.'
      - 'Beef Wellington (replaces the pork): salt the beef by 8 am Wednesday (or Tuesday night). Wednesday evening, sear it, make the duxelles, wrap it in prosciutto and duxelles, and make the jus base. On Thursday, wrap it in pastry by 3:00 pm and chill it. When the carrots come out at about 3:30, put a sheet pan in the 220 °C / 425 °F oven to heat. At 3:45 egg wash it, chill 5 minutes, wash again and score. Bake from 3:55 pm on the middle rack to 48 °C inside, checking from 4:25. Put the gratin on the top rack at 4:25, leaving room for the 14 cm tall Wellington. At 4:35 finish the jus. Rest the Wellington 10 minutes, then follow the main timeline from 4:45. With the Wellington, choose the rolls: the cornbread needs the top rack at the same time.'
      - 'Snapper (adds a pescatarian main): order two fish of 1.1 to 1.4 kg the week before, no longer than about 45 cm so both fit on a half sheet pan, and pick them up Wednesday. Cube the sourdough Wednesday and leave it out to stale. Make the stuffing Thursday morning and refrigerate it. At 3:15 pm salt the fish and refrigerate them on a rack. At 3:45 pm, once the carrots are out, rewarm the stuffing covered in its dish in the 220 °C oven to at least 60 °C. At 4:05 pm stuff the fish with the hot stuffing and roast them on the lower rack. The fish is done at 57 °C, and the stuffing in the cavity must reach 74 °C / 165 °F. With three dishes in the oven, everything takes about 5 minutes longer, so go by the thermometer and serve at about 5:10 pm.'
      - 'Salsa verde carrots, maple-cumin carrots or delicata squash (replace the garlic and thyme carrots): roast them in the carrots'' 2:50 to 3:30 pm slot. For the squash, make the hot honey and toast the pepitas on Tuesday.'
      - 'Sweet potato rolls (replace the bread): roast the sweet potato on Tuesday, then follow the Parker House schedule on Wednesday evening and Thursday morning. Take the butter for the honey butter out of the fridge 30 minutes before you beat it.'
      - 'Strawberry tres leches or pumpkin Basque cheesecake (replace the tres leches): bake the strawberry version on Wednesday on the same schedule, making its compote first, or the cheesecake on Tuesday or Wednesday; it keeps 4 days. Take the cheesecake out of the fridge 1 hour before dessert.'

    items:
      - 'Order the smoked pork shoulder for Wednesday pickup.'
      - 'Start the crème fraîche. It cultures 18 to 36 hours on the counter, then needs at least 12 hours in the fridge.'
  - when: 'Tuesday 24 November'
    items:
      - 'Move the crème fraîche to the fridge once it is thick.'
      - 'Make the cranberry-orange sauce, the pork vinegar sauce and the second sauce if you choose one, and refrigerate them in jars. The cranberry sauce needs at least 4 hours in the fridge to set.'
      - 'Make the salad vinaigrette and toast the almonds.'
      - 'Toast the pecans, then make the crumble topping and keep it in the fridge.'
  - when: 'Wednesday 25 November'
    items:
      - 'Wednesday is the busiest day, so start early: meatballs in the morning, the tres leches around midday, then the arrival dinner. Breadstick dough at about 3:30 pm for a 6 pm dinner.'
      - 'Make the meatballs and the gravy. Cool them in shallow dishes and refrigerate them within 2 hours of cooking.'
      - 'Steep the cinnamon milk for the tres leches, then bake the sponge, soak it, and refrigerate it overnight. Oven order: the meatballs first (they roast hot at 230 °C), then the cake at 175 °C.'
      - 'Pick up the pork and refrigerate it within 2 hours.'
      - 'Arrival dinner: mix the breadstick dough about 2½ hours before you eat. Start the chicken gnocchi soup 1 hour before, at 1½ times the recipe, in a pot of at least 6 L / 6 quarts. Bake the breadsticks at 200 °C / 400 °F in the last 15 minutes. Cool leftover soup in shallow containers and refrigerate it within 2 hours.'
      - 'Between 5:30 pm and midnight, make the roll dough, shape the rolls, and refrigerate them overnight under a fitted lid or an inverted sheet pan.'
  - when: 'Thanksgiving morning (dinner at 5 pm)'
    items:
      - 'At about 8:30, take the rolls out of the fridge to proof 60 to 90 minutes. Bake them and cool them on a rack, then hold them under a clean tea towel. Finish by 11:30 so the oven is free.'
      - 'Scrub and cut the carrots. Peel and slice the apples, toss them with the lemon juice, and refrigerate them covered with a plate.'
      - 'Seed the pomegranate, and grate the gruyère, and the parmigiano.'
  - when: '12:15 to 2:40 pm'
    items:
      - 'Infuse the cream for the gratin.'
      - 'At 12:45, slice and assemble the gratin. Bake it at 190 °C / 375 °F, 40 minutes covered and 35 to 45 minutes uncovered, then rest it uncovered on the counter. It can wait up to 2 hours before it re-crisps.'
      - 'While the gratin bakes, from about 1:00 pm, whip the crème fraîche cream for the tres leches, spread it on, and return the cake to the fridge. The topping holds up to 6 hours, which covers dessert.'
  - when: '2:40 to 3:45 pm'
    items:
      - 'Raise the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F with racks in the top and middle positions and the sheet pans inside. At about 2:50, roast the carrots 22 to 28 minutes, take them out at about 3:20 and toss them with the garlic oil. Hold them at room temperature.'
      - 'At 3:15, take the pork out of the fridge.'
      - 'At 3:30, start reheating the meatballs: add the held-back stock, cover, and warm over medium-low heat 45 to 60 minutes, stirring from the bottom every 10 minutes, until the largest reads 74 °C / 165 °F. Then hold them covered on low heat.'
      - 'At 3:45, take the sauces out of the fridge.'
  - when: '3:45 to 4:45 pm'
    items:
      - 'At 3:45, put the pork, covered with foil with its stock and 60 g / ¼ cup of the vinegar sauce, on the middle rack for 35 to 45 minutes, until the center reads 74 °C / 165 °F.'
      - 'At about 4:25, put the gratin on the top rack to re-crisp for 15 to 20 minutes. Uncover the pork, toss it, and return it uncovered 8 to 10 minutes to crisp the top, then hold it covered on the counter.'
      - 'At 4:30, take the salad dressing out of the fridge.'
  - when: '4:45 to 5:00 pm'
    items:
      - 'Take out the gratin and turn the oven down to 190 °C / 375 °F. Rewarm the rolls loosely covered with foil for 4 to 5 minutes.'
      - 'Slice the pears and assemble the salad undressed.'
      - 'Toss the apples with the sugar, cornstarch and spice and top them with the crumble.'
      - 'Finish the carrots with the lemon juice, parsley and flaky salt. Scatter parsley and the fried sage over the meatballs. Put the sauces and the jug of vinegar sauce on the table. Dress the salad at the table.'
  - when: 'At dinner'
    items:
      - 'As everyone sits down, put the crumble into the 190 °C oven for 45 to 55 minutes, then rest it at least 15 minutes, so it is warm for dessert.'
      - 'While the crumble rests, whip its crème fraîche cream. Serve the tres leches straight from the fridge, dusted with the ground cinnamon.'
