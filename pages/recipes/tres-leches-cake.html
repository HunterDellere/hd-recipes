Tres leches cake

A butterless, separated-egg sponge baked in a 9 x 13 pan, pierced all over and soaked overnight with evaporated milk, sweetened condensed milk and cinnamon-steeped whole milk. The open, dry crumb drinks more than its own weight in milk and stays cake rather than turning to pudding. A crème fraîche whipped cream on top cuts the sweetness.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Bake and soak the cake the day before. It needs at least 8 hours in the fridge for the milk to spread evenly to the center; overnight is better.
  • Steep the cinnamon stick in the whole milk first and let it cool while the cake bakes. Hot soak poured on a hot cake makes the bottom soggy and the top dry.
  • Separate the eggs while they are cold (yolks break less), then let the whites warm 20 minutes; warm whites whip to a larger volume.
  • If you use homemade crème fraîche for the topping, start it 2 to 3 days ahead.

Method

  1. 1
    Steep the cinnamon milk. Bring the 240 g / 1 cup soak whole milk and the 1 cinnamon stick to a bare simmer in a small saucepan over medium-low heat. Take it off the heat, cover, and steep 30 minutes. Remove the stick and let the milk cool to room temperature. Cinnamon aroma compounds dissolve in the milk fat, so a steeped stick gives a rounder flavor than ground cinnamon stirred into cold milk.
  2. 2
    Prepare the pan. Heat the oven to 175 °C / 350 °F with a rack in the center. Rub a 23 x 33 cm / 9 x 13 inch glass or ceramic baking dish with the 10 g / 2 tsp softened butter and dust it with a spoonful of the 140 g flour, tapping out the excess. The cake is served from this dish, so do not use parchment.
  3. 3
    Mix the dry ingredients. Whisk the 140 g / 1 cup plus 2 tbsp flour, 6 g / 1½ tsp baking powder and 2 g / ⅓ tsp fine sea salt together in a bowl for 20 seconds.
  4. 4
    Whip the yolks. In a large bowl, beat the 5 egg yolks (from the 250 g / 5 eggs) with 150 g / ¾ cup of the granulated sugar on medium-high until thick and pale, and a ribbon falling from the beaters sits on the surface for 2 seconds, about 4 minutes. Beat in the 80 g / ⅓ cup batter milk and the 8 g / 1½ tsp vanilla bean paste on low.
  5. 5
    Whip the whites. In a clean, grease-free bowl with clean beaters, beat the 5 egg whites on medium until foamy, then add the remaining 50 g / ¼ cup granulated sugar slowly and beat on high to firm, glossy peaks that curl over at the tip, 3 to 4 minutes. The sugar stabilises the foam, so the air cells survive folding. Stop before the whites look dry and grainy; overbeaten whites break into lumps when folded.
  6. 6
    Fold. Sift the flour mixture over the yolk mixture in two additions, folding with a large spatula just until no dry streaks remain. Stir in one third of the whites to loosen the batter, then fold in the rest in two additions, cutting down through the center and turning the bowl, until no white streaks remain. Spread the batter evenly into the dish.
  7. 7
    Bake. Bake 25 to 30 minutes, until the top is golden, springs back when pressed, and a skewer in the center comes out clean. With no butter in the batter, the crumb is a dry, open network of egg protein and starch; that structure is what absorbs the soak without collapsing. Cool the cake in the dish on a rack 30 minutes, until just warm.
  8. 8
    Mix the soak. Whisk the 354 ml / 12 oz can evaporated milk, the 397 g / 14 oz can sweetened condensed milk, the cooled cinnamon milk and the 1 g pinch fine sea salt in a jug until smooth. The salt does not make it salty; it keeps the condensed milk from tasting flat and cloying.
  9. 9
    Soak. Pierce the warm cake all over with a skewer or fork every 1 to 2 cm, all the way to the bottom, especially at the edges and corners. Pour about one third of the soak slowly and evenly over the surface. Wait 5 minutes for it to sink in, then repeat twice. It will look like too much liquid at first; the cake absorbs nearly all of it in the fridge.
  10. 10
    Chill. Cover the dish with a fitted lid or a sheet pan set on top and refrigerate at least 8 hours, ideally overnight. During this time the milk moves evenly through the crumb by capillary action, so the center and the edges end up equally moist.8 h
  11. 11
    Top (up to 6 hours before serving). In a chilled bowl, whip the 360 g / 1½ cups cold heavy cream, the 120 g / ½ cup cold crème fraîche and the 25 g / 2 tbsp topping sugar to soft, smooth peaks, about 2 minutes on medium-high. The acid in the crème fraîche helps the foam hold, so the topping keeps its shape in the fridge for the afternoon. Spread it over the cold cake and refrigerate until serving.
  12. 12
    Serve. Dust with the 1 g / ½ tsp ground cinnamon just before serving. Cut into squares straight from the fridge and lift them out with a flat spatula. Spoon any milk left in the dish over each piece.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion soak→rum tres leches

    Stir 30 g / 2 tbsp dark rum into the soak. The alcohol does not cook off, so leave it out if children or non-drinkers are at the table.

  • Regional topping→meringue topping

    Replace the whipped cream with a Swiss meringue of 3 egg whites and 150 g / ¾ cup granulated sugar, whisked over simmering water to 70 °C, then whipped to stiff peaks. Spread it over the cake and brown it with a kitchen torch. Sweeter, but lighter and very stable.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • crème fraîche, cold → more heavy cream, or sour cream Use 120 g / ½ cup extra heavy cream for a classic plain topping, or 120 g / ½ cup sour cream for the same tang. Sour cream makes a softer topping; whip it no more than a day ahead.
  • cinnamon stick, Ceylon (canela) if possible → cassia cinnamon stick Steep only 15 minutes. Cassia is sharper and hotter than Ceylon cinnamon.
  • vanilla bean paste → vanilla extract 1:1 by volume.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → crème fraîche, cold make it yourself Two ingredients and two days on the counter; homemade is thicker and more sour than most tubs, which is what this topping needs.

Notes

Why a sponge, not a butter cake: butter coats the flour and fills the crumb with fat, so a butter cake repels the milk and turns heavy and wet only at the bottom. A separated-egg sponge has no added fat and an open, dry crumb that absorbs the soak evenly.

On sweetness: this is a very sweet dessert by design. The crème fraîche topping and the salt in the soak are there so the last bite is not as sweet as the first.
