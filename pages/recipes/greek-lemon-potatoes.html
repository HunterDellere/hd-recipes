Recipe

Greek lemon potatoes

Patates lemonates: Yukon Gold wedges roasted in a shallow bath of chicken stock, lemon juice, garlic, olive oil, and rigani. For the first half of the roast the potatoes simmer and absorb the seasoned liquid, so they are lemony to the center; once it evaporates, the oil left behind fries the cut faces gold. Finished with raw lemon zest and juice so the citrus reads bright, not cooked.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

This is a 60-minute oven side; start it before the main. If the main roasts hotter (the roasted branzino wants 230 C / 450 F), raise the oven for the last 10 minutes of the potatoes, which only helps their crust, then pull them to rest while the fish roasts. They hold well for 20 minutes and reheat in a hot oven.

Step 1 · Potatoes

  1. 1200 g Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled, cut lengthwise into 6 to 8 wedges each, about 2.5 cm thick at the back
  2. 12 g kosher salt
  3. 1 g black pepper, freshly ground
  4. 3 g dried Greek oregano (rigani), crumbled

Step 2 · Braising liquid

  1. 240 ml chicken stock
  2. 80 g extra-virgin olive oil
  3. 45 g lemon juice, for the bath, freshly squeezed
  4. 20 g garlic cloves, grated on a microplane — (5 cloves)

Step 3 · Finish

  1. 100 g lemon, for the finish, zested, then juiced
  2. 8 g flat-leaf parsley, leaves chopped
  3. 1 g flaky salt — (a pinch, to taste)

Method

  1. 1
    Heat the oven to 220 C / 425 F with a rack in the center. Choose a metal roasting pan or rimmed sheet pan large enough to hold the wedges in one layer with a little space (about 33 x 23 cm / 13 x 9 in minimum). Glass and ceramic insulate the bottom and the frying phase never happens.
  2. 2
    Season the potatoes. In the pan, toss the 1200 g potato wedges with the 12 g / 4 tsp kosher salt, the 1 g / ½ tsp pepper, and the 3 g / 1 tbsp rigani. Arrange them cut-side down, skin-backs up, in a single layer.
  3. 3
    Pour in the bath. Whisk the 240 ml / 1 cup chicken stock, 80 g / ⅓ cup olive oil, 45 g / 3 tbsp lemon juice for the bath, and 20 g / 5 grated garlic cloves, and pour it around (not over) the potatoes. The liquid should come about a third of the way up the wedges. Measured against 1.2 kg of potatoes, this volume evaporates in about 40 minutes, leaving only oil.
  4. 4
    Roast, phase one: absorb. Roast 35 to 40 minutes without touching. The stock simmers, the starch at the potato surface gelatinizes in seasoned liquid, and the wedges drink the seasoned lemon-garlic bath to the center. You are done with this phase when the liquid has disappeared and the oil is audibly sizzling.
  5. 5
    Roast, phase two: fry. Flip each wedge with a thin metal spatula, scraping up the stuck crust (it is the best part), so the second cut face lies in the oil. Roast another 15 to 20 minutes until both faces are deep gold and the edges crisp. If the liquid had not fully evaporated, roast until it has before flipping; a wet flip steams instead of frying.
  6. 6
    Finish raw. Out of the oven, zest the 100 g / 1 finishing lemon directly over the pan, squeeze over its juice, and scatter the 8 g / ¼ cup parsley. Toss gently. Taste a wedge from the center: it should be lemony through, savory from the stock, and crisp-edged. Add the 1 g pinch of flaky salt only if it needs it.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion dairy→feta and oregano finish

    Crumble 80 g / ½ cup sheep's-milk feta over the hot potatoes with the finishing lemon. The feta softens without melting and adds a lactic salt that makes the dish a light meal on its own.

  • Regional side for lamb→patates fournou alongside a roast

    Arrange the wedges and bath around a leg or shoulder of lamb for its last hour; the lamb drippings join the stock. Omit the finishing parsley and use fresh rosemary in the bath.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • chicken stock → vegetable stock or water plus 1 tsp white miso Vegetable stock makes the dish vegan. Plain water loses the savory body; a teaspoon of white miso whisked in restores glutamate.
  • Yukon Gold potatoes → russet potatoes Fluffier interior, more fragile edges. Cut wedges slightly thicker and flip with care; they absorb the bath faster.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → chicken stock make it yourself The potatoes absorb nearly all of it, so a gelatin-rich homemade stock shows up as a glossier crust and deeper savoriness.

Notes

How the two-phase roast works. Greek lemon potatoes are a braise that turns into a roast. While the stock is present, the pan cannot exceed 100 C, so the potatoes poach and absorb. Salt and citric acid diffuse into the outer layer; the surface starch gelatinizes and later dries into a crisp shell (the same principle as parboiling roast potatoes, done in one pan). Once the water is gone, the oil heats past 150 C and the cut faces brown by Maillard reaction against the metal.

Why measure the liquid. Most failed lemon potatoes are soggy because the cook added liquid by eye. The ratio here, about 200 ml liquid per kilo of potatoes plus the oil, evaporates in 35 to 40 minutes in a standard pan. A smaller pan holds deeper liquid and takes longer; give it the extra time rather than flipping wet.

Why split the lemon. Citric acid survives the oven; the aromatic compounds (limonene, citral) do not. The lemon in the bath seasons the potato with acid. The raw zest and juice at the end supply the perfume. Both are needed.

Acid and starch. Acid in the bath slightly firms the potato surface by reinforcing pectin, which is why these wedges hold their shape through a long wet roast instead of collapsing.
