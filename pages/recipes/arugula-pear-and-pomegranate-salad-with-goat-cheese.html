Arugula, pear, and pomegranate salad with goat cheese

Baby arugula, paper-thin pear, pomegranate arils, salted toasted almonds, and crumbled fresh goat cheese, dressed at the table with a shallot, cider, and dijon vinaigrette that carries a spoonful of white miso. Built as the sharp, raw, crisp counterweight to a rich holiday table: pepper from the arugula, acid from the cider and pomegranate, crunch from the almonds, and lactic tang from the cheese.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Make the vinaigrette and toast the almonds up to 3 days ahead. Keep the dressing in a lidded glass jar in the fridge and the almonds in a sealed tin at room temperature. Take the dressing out 30 minutes before serving so the olive oil liquefies, then shake hard.
  • Slice the pears last, within 15 minutes of serving. Cut pear browns in about 20 minutes as polyphenol oxidase meets air; a quick toss in a spoonful of the dressing slows it.
  • Dress at the table, not in the kitchen. Arugula leaves have a thin cuticle and wilt within 5 to 10 minutes of contact with salt and acid, so the bowl should be tossed as it goes out, not while the mains rest.

  8. 1 g black pepper, freshly ground

Step 2 · Toasted almonds

  1. 80 g sliced almonds, skin-on
  2. 5 g extra-virgin olive oil, for the almonds
  3. 2 g flaky salt

Step 3 · Salad

  1. 225 g baby arugula, washed and spun completely dry
  2. 400 g pears, ripe but firm (Bosc or Bartlett), quartered, cored, sliced 3 mm thin lengthwise, skin on — (2 medium pears)
  3. 120 g pomegranate arils
  4. 115 g fresh goat cheese (chèvre), cold, crumbled into 1 cm pieces straight from the fridge

Method

  1. 1
    Macerate the shallot. In a 500 ml glass jar, combine the 30 g / 3 tbsp minced shallot, the 45 g / 3 tbsp apple cider vinegar, and the 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt. Let stand 10 minutes. The acid and salt draw water out of the shallot cells and convert the harsh thiosulfinates into a milder, sweeter allium note, so the shallot reads as seasoning rather than raw onion bite.
  2. 2
    Build the vinaigrette. Add the 10 g / 2 tsp dijon mustard, the 8 g / 1½ tsp white miso, the 15 g / 2 tsp maple syrup, and the 1 g / ½ tsp black pepper to the jar and stir with a fork until the miso fully dissolves into the vinegar with no lumps. Pour in the 90 g / 6 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, close the lid, and shake hard for 20 seconds until the dressing turns opaque and slightly thickened. The mustard mucilage and miso proteins hold the emulsion for several minutes at a time; it will separate in the fridge and reshake cleanly. Technique: Vinaigrette emulsion
  3. 3
    Taste and correct the dressing on a leaf. Dip an arugula leaf in it rather than tasting from a spoon; the dressing should taste slightly too sharp and too salty on its own, because the pear, cheese, and greens will dilute it. If it tastes flat, add 1 g / ¼ tsp kosher salt; if it tastes harsh, add 5 g / 1 tsp maple syrup. Refrigerate up to 3 days.
  4. 4
    Toast the almonds. Heat the oven to 175 °C / 350 °F. Toss the 80 g / ¾ cup sliced almonds with the 5 g / 1 tsp olive oil on a small sheet pan and toast 6 to 8 minutes, shaking the pan once, until golden at the edges and fragrant. Sprinkle at once with the 2 g / ½ tsp flaky salt so it sticks to the oiled surface. Cool completely on the pan; they crisp as they cool. Store in a sealed tin up to 3 days. Technique: Dry-toasting (grains, spices, seeds, dried chilies)
  5. 5
    Slice the pears last. Within 15 minutes of serving, quarter, core, and slice the 400 g / 2 pears 3 mm thin. Toss the slices in a small bowl with 15 g / 1 tbsp of the shaken vinaigrette; the acid coats the cut surfaces and slows browning.
  6. 6
    Assemble undressed. Pile the 225 g / 8 oz dry arugula loosely in a wide serving bowl. Tuck the dressed pear slices through the greens, then scatter the 120 g / ¾ cup pomegranate arils, the 115 g / 4 oz crumbled goat cheese, and the toasted almonds over the top. Keep the cheese cold until this point; warm chèvre smears into the leaves instead of holding as crumbs.
  7. 7
    Dress at the table. Shake the remaining vinaigrette (about 185 g / ¾ cup), pour about half of it (90 g / ⅓ cup) around the edge of the bowl, and toss with two large spoons, lifting from the bottom, 6 or 7 turns. Taste a leaf with pear and cheese: it should be bright, peppery, and salted. Add the rest of the dressing only if the leaves look dry. Serve within 10 minutes.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Texture greens→arugula and radicchio, or arugula and frisée

    Replace a third of the arugula (75 g / 3 oz) with torn radicchio or frisée. Radicchio adds bitterness that sharpens the pear sweetness and holds up an extra 15 minutes after dressing; frisée adds volume and a crisp frilled texture. Increase the maple syrup in the dressing to 20 g / 1 tbsp to balance the extra bitterness of radicchio.

  • Occasion cheese→shaved aged cheese instead of goat cheese

    Shave 60 g / 2 oz parmigiano reggiano or aged manchego over the top with a vegetable peeler instead of crumbling goat cheese. The salad turns nuttier and saltier and loses the creamy lactic note; reduce the kosher salt in the dressing to 2 g / ½ tsp.

  • Regional season→persimmon or crisp apple instead of pear

    Fuyu persimmon sliced into thin wedges, or a Honeycrisp apple sliced 2 mm thin, replaces the pear one for one by weight. Fuyu does not brown, so it can be cut 30 minutes ahead; apple browns faster than pear and needs the vinaigrette toss immediately.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • white miso (shiro) → shaved parmigiano reggiano Omit the miso from the dressing and shave 30 g / 1 oz parmigiano over the finished salad. Same glutamate load, but the umami sits on top rather than inside the dressing.
  • apple cider vinegar → sherry vinegar Sherry vinegar is sharper and nuttier; use 40 g / 2½ tbsp and increase the maple syrup to 20 g / 1 tbsp.
  • sliced almonds, skin-on → toasted pecans or pistachios Pecans add a buttery note; pistachios add color. Toast pecans 8 to 10 minutes at the same temperature, pistachios 5 to 6.
  • pomegranate arils → dried cranberries or dried tart cherries Use 50 g / ⅓ cup. Sweeter and chewier, and the salad loses its burst of fresh acid; add 5 g / 1 tsp extra cider vinegar to the dressing.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → dijon mustard make it yourself Homemade dijon lets you set the heat and the acid, and a sharp batch gives the dressing a cleaner bite than a jarred one.
  • → apple cider vinegar make it yourself A home-fermented cider vinegar keeps more apple aroma than a pasteurized bottle, which matters in a raw dressing where nothing cooks it off.

Notes

Why this salad sits on a holiday table. Every other plate in a holiday spread tends toward fat, starch, and long cooking. This one is raw, acidic, peppery, and crisp, and it resets the palate between bites of gratin and roast meat. The arugula supplies the heat (glucosinolates break down into isothiocyanates when the leaf is chewed, the same pungency family as mustard), the cider and pomegranate supply the acid, and the almonds supply crunch.

Why white miso in the dressing. The salad needs an umami anchor, or it reads as sweet fruit on leaves. Shaved parmigiano would do the job, but a holiday table often already carries a cheese-heavy gratin, and goat cheese is already on the plate. One and a half teaspoons of white miso dissolves invisibly into the vinaigrette, adds glutamate and a faint savory roundness, and, with the dijon, helps keep the emulsion stable. Nobody tastes miso; they taste a dressing that seems more complete.

Why the 2:1 ratio. A classic vinaigrette runs 3 parts oil to 1 part acid. This one runs closer to 2:1 because the pears, maple, and goat cheese all blunt acidity, and because the salad is meant to cut through cream and butter on the same plate. Taste on a leaf, not off a spoon: dressing that tastes correct on its own will taste flat once it coats the greens.

Pear choice. Bosc holds its shape and slices cleanly with a dense, slightly grainy flesh and a honeyed flavor; Bartlett is juicier and more floral but softens fast once ripe. Press near the stem: it should give slightly. A pear that is soft at the belly is too ripe to slice thin.
