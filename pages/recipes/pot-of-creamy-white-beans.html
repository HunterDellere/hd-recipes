Recipe

Pot of creamy white beans

Dried cannellini brine-soaked overnight, boiled 10 minutes, then cooked at a bare tremor with onion, garlic, a Parmesan rind, and olive oil until the beans are custardy and the liquid thickens into a light cream. The base for bean-and-greens braises, soups, white chili, and purées.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Start the soak the night before. Cannellini have thin skins that burst if the inside is not fully hydrated before cooking.
  • Do not cook dried cannellini in a slow cooker from raw. Boil them on the stove for 10 minutes first; after that, a slow cooker is fine.

Step 1 · Brine soak

  1. 250 g dried cannellini beans, picked over and rinsed
  2. 1000 ml cold water, for soaking
  3. 8 g kosher salt, for the soak

Step 2 · Cook

  1. 1500 ml cold water, for cooking
  2. 3 g kosher salt, for cooking
  3. 100 g yellow onion, halved through the root, root left on — (½ large)
  4. 12 g garlic cloves, smashed — (3 cloves)
  5. 20 g Parmesan rind — (1 piece, about 5 cm / 2 in)
  6. 1 bay leaf
  7. 30 ml extra-virgin olive oil
  8. 2 g kosher salt, to adjust

Method

  1. 1
    Brine-soak. Dissolve the 8 g / 2 tsp kosher salt for the soak in the 1000 ml / 4 cups cold soaking water in a large bowl. Add the 250 g rinsed cannellini, cover with a plate, and leave at room temperature 8 to 24 hours. The brine softens the skins so they stay intact and tender while the insides turn creamy.8 h
  2. 2
    Drain and rinse. Drain the beans, discard the soaking water, and rinse well. Fully soaked cannellini are plump and smooth; any still wrinkled need another hour.
  3. 3
    Boil 10 minutes. Put the beans in a heavy pot with the 1500 ml / 6 cups cold cooking water, the 3 g / ¾ tsp kosher salt for cooking, the 100 g halved onion, the 12 g / 3 smashed garlic cloves, the 20 g Parmesan rind, the 1 bay leaf, and the 30 ml / 2 tbsp olive oil. Bring to a full boil and keep it there for 10 minutes, skimming the foam. This destroys the lectin in white kidney beans; it is short enough not to damage the skins.
  4. 4
    Cook at a tremor. Lower the heat until the surface barely moves, with only an occasional bubble, partly cover, and cook 45 to 75 minutes, stirring gently once or twice and topping up with hot water if the level drops to the beans. A rolling boil past the first 10 minutes tears the thin skins and turns the pot to mash. The olive oil coats the beans and calms the surface of the water, which also helps keep the skins whole.
  5. 5
    Check and season. Taste five beans; all should be uniformly creamy with no chalky core and tender skins. Taste the liquid and add up to 2 g / ½ tsp kosher salt if it tastes flat. For a thicker, creamier liquid, crush a ladleful of beans against the side of the pot and stir them back in.
  6. 6
    Cool in the liquid. Fish out the onion, garlic skins, the 1 bay leaf, and what is left of the rind. Let the beans cool in their liquid; they absorb it and improve over the first 24 hours. Refrigerate within 2 hours, still submerged.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Texture beans→great northern or navy beans

    Same method and amounts. Great northern beans are slightly firmer and finish in 45 to 60 minutes; navy beans are smaller and creamier and break down more readily, which suits soups and purées.

  • Regional flavor→Tuscan-style with sage

    Add 4 fresh sage leaves with the bay leaf. Serve the beans warm on toasted bread with a drizzle of olive oil and black pepper.

  • Dietary diet→vegan

    Leave out the Parmesan rind and add a 5 cm / 2 in strip of kombu in its place for the same background savoriness.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • Parmesan rind → leave it out The liquid tastes flatter; add an extra 15 ml / 1 tbsp olive oil and season a little more at the end.

Notes

Why cannellini need more care than other beans. Their skins are thin, so if the outside softens before the inside is hydrated, the skin splits as the interior swells. A full overnight soak and a bare tremor of heat after the initial boil are what keep them whole.

The Parmesan rind. The rind softens in the pot and releases glutamate and a little fat into the liquid, which gives the beans a savory depth without any cheese flavor in particular. Save rinds in the freezer for this.

Salt early, acid late. Salt in the soak and the cooking water seasons the beans and softens the skins. Acid does the opposite: tomatoes, wine, or lemon added before the beans are tender keep the skins firm, so they go in afterward.

The liquid is an ingredient. Cooled, it sets to a light, creamy gel. Use it to thicken soups and white chili, to braise greens, or to loosen a bean purée.

Storage. Beans in their liquid keep 5 days refrigerated or 6 months frozen, with enough liquid to cover.
