Recipe

Maryland crab cakes

Jumbo lump blue crab held together by the minimum binder that will hold it: one egg, a spoon of mayonnaise, dijon, Worcestershire, Chesapeake seasoning, and a handful of crushed saltines. Chilled to set, then seared hard in butter and oil so the outside goes deep golden while the inside stays loose, sweet, and almost all crab.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • The formed cakes need 30 minutes in the refrigerator (up to 8 hours) before they hit the pan. The chill firms the mayonnaise fat and lets the saltines hydrate into a glue; an unchilled cake slumps and sheds lumps when flipped.
  • Make the tartar sauce and the vinegar slaw first. Both improve after 30 minutes of rest, which is exactly how long the cakes chill.

Step 2 · Crab

  1. 450 g jumbo lump blue crab meat, refrigerated (not shelf-stable), spread on a sheet pan and picked for shell with a light touch

Step 3 · Sear

  1. 15 g neutral oil
  2. 28 g unsalted butter
  3. 1 lemon wedges, cut into 4 wedges

Method

  1. 1
    Pick the crab. Spread the 450 g / 1 lb crab meat in a single layer on a sheet pan. Run your fingertips lightly over it to find shell and cartilage; tilt the pan under a light and the shell shards catch the glare. Lift them out rather than stirring the meat; every stir breaks a lump, and the lumps are what you are paying for.
  2. 2
    Build the binder. In a large bowl, whisk the 50 g / 1 egg, 45 g / 3 tbsp mayonnaise, 8 g / 1½ tsp dijon mustard, 5 g / 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce, 5 g / 1¼ tsp Old Bay seasoning, 5 g / 1 tsp lemon juice, and 5 g / 2 tbsp parsley until smooth. Stir in the 24 g / 8 crushed saltines and let it sit 2 minutes so the crackers start to hydrate. No added salt: the crab, the seasoning, the Worcestershire, and the crackers already bring about 1% salt to the mix, which is the target.
  3. 3
    Fold. Tip the 450 g crab onto the binder. Fold with a flexible spatula, scooping from the bottom and turning the mass over itself, 6 to 8 strokes, until the binder coats the lumps and no dry crumbs remain. Stop there. The mixture will look barely held together; that is correct.
  4. 4
    Form and chill. Divide into 4 portions of about 140 g. Cup each in your palms and press gently into a cake 7 cm across and 3 cm tall; pack the edges more firmly than the center, since the edges take the spatula on the flip. Set on a parchment-lined plate, cover, and refrigerate at least 30 minutes.
  5. 5
    Sear. Heat a 30 cm stainless or cast-iron skillet over medium heat for 2 minutes. Add the 15 g / 1 tbsp neutral oil and the 28 g / 2 tbsp butter. When the butter foam subsides and the milk solids just begin to tan, lay in the cold cakes with space between them. Cook undisturbed 4 minutes, until the underside is deep golden. The oil raises the smoke point so the butter can brown the crust without scorching across the full cook. Technique: Shallow frying
  6. 6
    Flip and finish. Slide a thin fish spatula fully under each cake and turn it in one motion, steadying the top with your other hand. Cook another 3 to 4 minutes, basting the tops once or twice with the browned fat. The center should read 71 °C / 160 °F on an instant-read thermometer, the point at which the binder sets. If the crust is done before the center is, move the pan to a 200 °C / 400 °F oven for 4 to 5 minutes. Technique: Shallow frying
  7. 7
    Rest and serve. Drain the cakes 1 minute on a rack (paper towels steam the bottom crust soft). Serve with the 4 lemon wedges, tartar sauce, and vinegar slaw. Squeeze the lemon at the table, not before; acid on a hot crust softens it within a minute.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Texture cooking method→broiled crab cakes

    The other Baltimore standard, and lighter. Set the chilled cakes on a buttered sheet pan, brush the tops with 15 g / 1 tbsp melted butter, and broil 15 cm from the element for 8 to 10 minutes without flipping, until the tops are spotty brown and the center reads 71 °C. Less crust, more pure crab flavor, and no flip to break them.

  • Occasion format→crab cake sandwich

    Form 4 slightly flatter cakes (2 cm tall) so they sit flat on a soft potato roll or saltine-thin roll. Sear as written, then dress the roll with tartar sauce, a leaf of iceberg, and a thick tomato slice.

  • Occasion serving size→appetizer bites

    Form 8 cakes of about 70 g each, 2.5 cm tall. Sear 3 minutes per side; the smaller mass reaches 71 °C without the oven.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • jumbo lump blue crab meat → lump or backfin blue crab, or picked Dungeness Lump is the best value and still reads as crab cake. Backfin is finer and makes a denser, more uniform cake; add 5 g more saltines. Dungeness is sweeter and larger-flaked; drop the seasoning to 4 g, since the meat is milder in mineral salt.
  • saltine crackers → plain dry breadcrumbs or crushed oyster crackers Use the same weight. Saltines are traditional because the fat-laminated cracker hydrates into a tender glue rather than a bready one.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → Old Bay seasoning make it yourself Freshly ground celery seed and bay are far louder than a tin that has been open for a year, and you control the salt.
  • → mayonnaise make it yourself Fresh mayonnaise is eggier and binds slightly better, which lets you keep the cracker load low.
  • → dijon mustard make it yourself Fresh mustard has more of the sharp isothiocyanate bite that cuts the richness of the crab and mayo.

Notes

Crab cakes fail in one of two directions: too much filler, where the cake eats like a bread fritter, or too little chill, where it falls apart in the pan. This ratio (about 75 g of binder and crackers per 450 g of crab) sits at the low edge of what will hold, and the 30-minute chill is what makes that possible.

Do not add onion, celery, or bell pepper. They are the most common drift from the Maryland standard; they release water during the sear, loosen the cake, and pull attention away from the crab.

Pasteurized shelf-stable crab (the cans in the tuna aisle) is not a substitute. Pasteurization tightens and shreds the meat, and the cake loses its lumps.

Recommended plate: one cake per person, tartar sauce, vinegar slaw, and sweet corn on the cob in season. Sliced ripe tomatoes with flaky salt work in late summer.

  • Chesapeake Bay tradition (Baltimore and the Eastern Shore) — The Maryland standard is defined by what it leaves out: no bell pepper, no onion, no panko crust, no heavy breading. The binder exists only to keep the lumps from falling apart, and a good crab cake should look like crab pressed together rather than a fritter with crab in it.
