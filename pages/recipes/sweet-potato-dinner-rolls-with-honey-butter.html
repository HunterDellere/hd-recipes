Sweet potato dinner rolls with honey butter

Soft, pull-apart milk-bread rolls with roasted sweet potato worked into the dough. The sweet potato adds moisture, color and a gentle sweetness, and its starch keeps the crumb soft for a day. Served with whipped honey butter and flaky salt.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Roast the sweet potato first, up to 2 days ahead. It needs 50 to 60 minutes in the oven and must be cool before it goes into the dough, or it will kill some of the yeast.
  • Plan about 4 hours from mixing to baked rolls: 1 to 1½ hours for the first rise and 45 to 60 minutes for the second. For a holiday, shape the rolls the night before and refrigerate them in the pan; next morning they need 60 to 90 minutes at room temperature before baking.
  • Take the butter for the dough and the honey butter out of the fridge 30 minutes ahead so it is soft.

Method

  1. 1
    Roast the sweet potato. Prick the 300 g / 1 medium sweet potato a few times with a fork, set it on a foil-lined sheet pan, and roast at 200 °C / 400 °F until it collapses when squeezed and syrup leaks from the holes, 40 to 60 minutes depending on size. Roasting, not boiling, concentrates the sugars and keeps the flesh dry, so the dough does not turn sticky. Cool, peel, and mash 200 g / ¾ cup of the flesh smooth with a fork.
  2. 2
    Mix. In a stand mixer bowl, whisk the 450 g / 3½ cups plus 2 tbsp bread flour, 7 g / 2¼ tsp instant yeast and 9 g / 1½ tsp fine sea salt. Add the 200 g mashed sweet potato, the 120 g / ½ cup warm milk, the 50 g / 1 egg and the 30 g / 1½ tbsp dough honey. Mix with the dough hook on low until no dry flour remains, about 2 minutes.
  3. 3
    Knead and add the butter. Knead on medium-low 5 minutes, until the dough clears the sides of the bowl. Add the 50 g / 3½ tbsp softened dough butter a spoonful at a time, letting each piece disappear before adding the next, then knead 4 to 6 minutes more, until the dough is smooth, slightly tacky and stretches into a thin sheet you can see light through. Adding the butter after the gluten forms lets the gluten develop first; fat added at the start coats the proteins and slows that down. Technique: Enriched dough
  4. 4
    First rise. Shape the dough into a ball, set it in a lightly buttered bowl, cover with a fitted lid or a damp tea towel, and let it rise at warm room temperature until doubled, 1 to 1½ hours. Technique: Bulk fermentation
  5. 5
    Shape. Butter a 23 x 33 cm / 9 x 13 inch pan. Turn the dough out, press out the gas, and divide it into 15 pieces of about 60 g. Shape each into a tight ball by dragging it on an unfloured counter under a cupped hand, seam down. Set them in the pan in 3 rows of 5, slightly apart; they grow into each other as they rise.
  6. 6
    Second rise. Cover and let the rolls rise until puffy and touching, and a fingertip press springs back slowly, 45 to 60 minutes. Or refrigerate the covered pan overnight (up to 16 hours) and let it stand at room temperature 60 to 90 minutes the next morning. Heat the oven to 190 °C / 375 °F with a rack in the center for the last 20 minutes.
  7. 7
    Bake. Beat the 50 g / 1 egg for the wash with 15 g / 1 tbsp water and brush the tops gently with it and sprinkle with the 2 g / ½ tsp flaky salt for the tops. Bake 20 to 24 minutes, until deep golden and the center roll reads 90 °C / 195 °F. The sugars from the sweet potato and honey brown fast; if the tops are dark by 15 minutes, lay a sheet of foil loosely over them.
  8. 8
    Make the honey butter while the rolls bake. Beat the 115 g / 1 stick softened butter with the 40 g / 2 tbsp honey and the 2 g / ½ tsp flaky salt with a wooden spoon until pale and fluffy, about 2 minutes. The salt is what makes it taste of butter and not only of honey.
  9. 9
    Cool and serve. Cool the rolls in the pan 10 minutes, then turn them onto a rack. Serve warm with the honey butter. To make ahead, bake them the morning of, keep them under a clean tea towel, and rewarm at 175 °C / 350 °F for 5 minutes before serving.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • orange-fleshed sweet potato → canned sweet potato or pumpkin purée Use 200 g / ¾ cup, drained well. Canned purée is wetter; hold back 20 g of the milk and add it only if the dough feels stiff.
  • bread flour → all-purpose flour Same weight. The rolls are a little softer and less tall.
  • instant yeast → active dry yeast Use 9 g / 2½ tsp, dissolved in the warm milk for 5 minutes before mixing.

Notes

Why sweet potato makes a softer roll: its starch gelatinizes during roasting and holds water inside the crumb, which slows the starch in the flour from going stale. The rolls stay soft through the next day without a tangzhong.
