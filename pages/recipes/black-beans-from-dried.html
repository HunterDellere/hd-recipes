Black beans from dried

Dried black beans brine-soaked overnight, then simmered gently with onion, garlic, bay, and a strip of kombu until creamy inside, in a dark, seasoned bean liquid that is half the point. The base pot of beans for Mexican, Cuban, and Brazilian cooking.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Start the soak the night before. The beans need at least 8 hours in salted water.
  • Keep the cooking liquid. Recipes that call for beans from this pot often use the liquid too, for body and seasoning.

What to prep, in order 2 phases ▾

Step 1 · Brine soak

  1. 500 g dried black beans, picked over for stones and shriveled beans, rinsed
  2. 2000 ml cold water, for soaking
  3. 15 g kosher salt, for the soak

Step 2 · Simmer

  1. 2500 ml cold water, for cooking
  2. 6 g kosher salt, for cooking
  3. 200 g white onion, halved through the root, root left on — (1 medium)
  4. 12 g garlic cloves, smashed — (3 cloves)
  5. 2 bay leaves
  6. 5 g kombu — (one 5 cm / 2 in strip)
  7. 3 g kosher salt, to adjust

Method

  1. 1
    Brine-soak. Dissolve the 15 g / 1½ tbsp kosher salt for the soak in the 2000 ml / 8 cups cold soaking water in a large bowl. Add the 500 g rinsed black beans, cover with a plate, and leave at room temperature 8 to 24 hours. The sodium in the brine loosens the calcium and magnesium holding the pectin in the skins together, so the skins soften and stay whole while the inside cooks. The water turns dark purple as pigment leaches out; that is normal.8 h
  2. 2
    Drain and rinse. Drain the beans, discard the soaking water, and rinse well. The soaking water carries some of the oligosaccharides that cause gas, along with excess salt.
  3. 3
    Bring to a boil. Put the beans in a heavy 5 L pot with the 2500 ml / 10 cups cold cooking water, the 6 g / 1½ tsp kosher salt for cooking, the 200 g halved onion, the 12 g / 3 smashed garlic cloves, the 2 bay leaves, and the 5 g kombu. Bring to a boil over high heat, boil 5 minutes, and skim off the gray foam.
  4. 4
    Simmer gently. Lower the heat until only a few small bubbles break the surface, partly cover with the lid ajar, and cook 60 to 90 minutes, stirring gently every 20 minutes and topping up with hot water so the beans stay covered by at least 2 cm. Keep it gentle: a hard boil tumbles the beans and splits their skins. Old beans can take up to 2 hours.
  5. 5
    Check for doneness and season. Taste five beans from different parts of the pot; all five should be creamy all the way through with tender skins. If any are firm, keep simmering and check every 10 minutes. Taste the liquid: it should taste like a seasoned broth. Add up to 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt if it tastes flat, then simmer 5 minutes more so the beans absorb it.
  6. 6
    Cool in the liquid. Take the pot off the heat and discard the onion, the 2 bay leaves, and the kombu. Let the beans cool in their liquid for at least 30 minutes, during which they absorb more seasoning and firm up slightly. Refrigerate within 2 hours, still in the liquid.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion beans→no overnight soak

    Bring the beans and 2 L / 8 cups water with the 15 g soak salt to a boil, boil 2 minutes, cover, and leave off the heat for 1 hour. Drain and rinse, then continue from step 3. Unsoaked beans can also be simmered straight from dry for 2 to 2½ hours; more skins split.

  • Regional flavor→frijoles de olla with epazote

    Add 2 sprigs fresh epazote (or 1 tsp dried) for the last 15 minutes. Epazote is the traditional Mexican herb for black beans; it tastes resinous and slightly minty.

  • Regional flavor→Cuban-style seasoned beans

    When the beans are tender, stir in a sofrito of onion, green bell pepper, and garlic fried in olive oil with cumin and oregano, plus 15 ml / 1 tbsp dry sherry and 15 ml / 1 tbsp red wine vinegar. Simmer 20 minutes uncovered until thick.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • kombu → leave it out The beans soften slightly more slowly and the liquid tastes a little flatter. At this amount kombu does not taste of the sea.
  • white onion → yellow onion Same weight. Slightly sweeter.

Notes

Salt does not make beans tough. At these amounts, salting the soak and the cooking water softens the skins and seasons the beans all the way through. What does slow softening is acid and sugar, which is why tomatoes, vinegar, and sofrito go in after the beans are tender.

Why soak. Soaked beans cook in about half the time and more evenly, and fewer of them burst. Discarding the soaking water also removes some of the oligosaccharides responsible for gas.

Kombu. Kombu contributes glutamate, which gives the liquid a rounder, more savory taste, and its alginates are thought to help soften the skins. It is optional and not traditional.

The bean liquid. Keep it. It is a seasoned, starchy stock that thickens soups and chili, loosens refried beans, and can stand in for water when cooking rice.

If the beans will not soften. Very old beans and hard water are the usual causes. Add 0.5 g / ⅛ tsp baking soda per liter of cooking water; no more, or the beans turn mushy and taste soapy.

Storage. Beans in their liquid keep 5 days refrigerated, or 6 months frozen in 250 g / 1½ cup portions with enough liquid to cover. About 200 g dried beans gives 500 g cooked.
