Jalapeño cheddar skillet cornbread

Buttermilk cornbread baked in a preheated cast iron skillet, so the bottom and edges fry crisp in brown butter. The cornmeal soaks in the buttermilk first for a tender crumb. Sharp cheddar, seeded jalapeño and corn kernels run through it, and honey keeps it lightly sweet.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Soak the cornmeal in the buttermilk for 15 minutes before you mix the batter. Hydrated cornmeal bakes tender; dry cornmeal stays gritty.
  • Heat the skillet in the oven with the butter in it for at least 10 minutes. The batter must sizzle when it hits the pan, or the crust will not form.
  • Cornbread is best within 2 hours of baking. On a one-oven holiday, bake it on the top rack while the main reheats and serve it from the skillet.

Step 3 · Finish

Method

  1. 1
    Soak the cornmeal. Stir the 240 g / 1½ cups plus 2 tbsp cornmeal into the 420 g / 1¾ cups buttermilk in a large bowl and let it stand 15 minutes. The coarse cornmeal particles absorb water slowly; soaking softens them before they bake.
  2. 2
    Heat the skillet. Put a 25 cm / 10 inch cast iron skillet on the middle or top rack and heat the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F.
  3. 3
    Brown the butter. In a small saucepan over medium heat, melt 70 g / 5 tbsp of the 90 g butter and cook it, swirling, until the milk solids turn amber and smell of toasted hazelnut, 3 to 4 minutes. Pour it into a cup so it stops cooking and let it cool 5 minutes. Technique: Brown butter (beurre noisette)
  4. 4
    Mix the dry ingredients. Whisk the 90 g / ¾ cup flour, 8 g / 2 tsp baking powder, 3 g / ½ tsp baking soda and 6 g / 1 tsp fine sea salt together in a small bowl.
  5. 5
    Mix the batter. Whisk the 100 g / 2 eggs and the 40 g / 2 tbsp honey into the soaked cornmeal, then whisk in the brown butter. Fold in the flour mixture with a spatula until only a few dry streaks remain, then fold in 90 g / 1 cup of the cheddar, the 60 g / 2 minced jalapeños and the 150 g / 1 cup corn, just until no dry flour shows. The buttermilk acid reacts with the baking soda at once, so work without stopping from here.
  6. 6
    Bake. Pull out the hot skillet, add the remaining 20 g / 1½ tbsp butter and swirl it to coat the bottom and sides. Pour in the batter; it should sizzle at the edges. Scatter the remaining 30 g / ⅓ cup cheddar over the top. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until the top is golden, the edges pull away from the pan and a skewer in the center comes out clean.
  7. 7
    Rest and serve. Sprinkle the 1 g / ¼ tsp flaky salt over the top and rest the cornbread in the skillet 10 minutes, so the crumb sets. Cut it into 8 wedges and serve it from the skillet. Taste a wedge from the center: it should taste of corn and butter first, then cheese and a little heat, and be only faintly sweet.

Heavy cast iron skillet

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Heat heat→a mild cornbread for children

    Leave out the jalapeños and use 150 g / 1⅔ cups cheddar. Everything else stays the same.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • cultured buttermilk → whole milk soured with lemon juice Stir 15 g / 1 tbsp lemon juice into 405 g / 1⅔ cups whole milk and let it stand 5 minutes. Thinner, with a milder tang.
  • jalapeños → canned diced green chiles Use 60 g / ⅓ cup, drained and patted dry. Milder and more savory.
  • frozen corn kernels → leave them out The bread is less moist, with no sweet bursts of corn. Do not add more liquid.

Notes

Why a preheated skillet. Cast iron holds a lot of heat. Batter poured into a skillet at 220 °C fries in the butter on contact, which gives a crisp, browned bottom and edges. In a cold pan the batter sets slowly and the outside stays pale and soft.

Why the batter is mixed only briefly. Flour develops gluten when it is wet and stirred. A cornbread mixed like a cake turns tough and full of tunnels, so the flour goes in last and is folded only until it disappears.

Why buttermilk and baking soda. Buttermilk is acidic. The baking soda reacts with the acid to make carbon dioxide, which lifts the bread, and it neutralizes some of the acid, so the crumb browns better and tastes less sour. The baking powder adds lift that keeps working in the oven.
