Nuoc cham bun cha (Hanoi-style warm dipping broth)

The Hanoi version of nuoc cham, served warm in a deep bowl as a thin, light broth rather than a dip. Fish sauce, rice vinegar, and sugar are dissolved in a generous volume of hot water, then loaded with quick-pickled green papaya and carrot, raw garlic, and chili. Grilled pork and patties go straight into the bowl, and diners dip rice vermicelli and herbs through it.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Salt the papaya and carrot first. They need 15 minutes to wilt and drain before they go into the broth, which is the time it takes to fire the grill for the pork.
  • Serve the broth warm, around body temperature to 50 °C / 120 °F, so the grilled pork fat stays soft and glossy when it hits the bowl. Cold broth seizes the fat; boiling broth cooks the raw garlic and chili.

What to prep, in order 3 phases ▾

Step 1 · Quick pickle

  1. 120 g green papaya, peeled and seeded, sliced into thin 2 cm squares or short planks — (about a 4-inch / 10 cm wedge; kohlrabi is the common Hanoi winter swap)
  2. 50 g carrot, peeled, sliced thin on the diagonal, or cut into flower shapes — (1 small carrot)
  3. 4 g kosher salt

Step 2 · Broth

  1. 360 g hot water
  2. 50 g granulated sugar
  3. 60 g fish sauce
  4. 45 g rice vinegar
  5. 15 g fresh lime juice

Step 3 · Finish

  1. 10 g garlic cloves, peeled, minced fine — (2 to 3 cloves)
  2. 4 g fresh red Thai chilies, thinly sliced — (1 to 2 chilies)

Method

  1. 1
    Toss the 120 g sliced green papaya and the 50 g sliced carrot with the 4 g / 1 tsp kosher salt. Rest 15 minutes, then squeeze firmly in your hands and discard the liquid. Salting draws out water so the slices stay crisp and absorb the broth instead of diluting it.
  2. 2
    While the vegetables drain, stir the 50 g / ¼ cup granulated sugar into the 360 g / 1½ cups hot water until fully dissolved. Add the 60 g / ¼ cup fish sauce, the 45 g / 3 tbsp rice vinegar, and the 15 g / 1 tbsp lime juice.
  3. 3
    Taste. Hanoi bun cha broth should taste light enough to sip: mildly salty, gently sweet, clearly sour, and never syrupy. If it reads strong, add 30 g / 2 tbsp more hot water; if flat, 5 g / 1 tsp fish sauce.
  4. 4
    Add the drained papaya and carrot and rest 5 minutes so they pickle lightly in the warm broth. Just before serving, stir in the 10 g / 2 to 3 cloves minced garlic and the 4 g / 1 to 2 sliced chilies so their raw edge stays sharp.
  5. 5
    If the broth has cooled, warm it gently to about 50 °C / 120 °F, never to a simmer. Ladle into individual deep bowls, add the grilled pork straight from the grill, and serve with rice vermicelli and a plate of lettuce, perilla, mint, and rau kinh gioi on the side.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Regional seasonal vegetable→with kohlrabi in place of green papaya

    Use 120 g peeled kohlrabi cut the same way. Salt it for 20 minutes rather than 15; it holds more water. This is the standard winter swap in Hanoi.

  • Occasion occasion→as a dip for nem ran (Hanoi fried spring rolls)

    Halve the hot water to 180 g so the sauce is concentrated enough to dip, and skip the papaya. This is the same sauce Hanoi cooks set beside fried rolls.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • rice vinegar → distilled white vinegar Use 35 g; it is sharper than rice vinegar.

Notes

How it differs from southern nuoc cham. The southern sauce is concentrated, sweeter, and lime-forward because it is drizzled over bowls and used for dipping. Hanoi bun cha broth is roughly three times more diluted and is eaten like a light soup: the pork sits in it, the noodles go through it, and the bowl is drained by the end. Making it at southern strength produces a bowl too salty to finish.

Why vinegar. Rice vinegar gives a cleaner, more stable sourness than lime in a warm liquid; lime juice turns bitter and loses its aroma above about 60 °C. A splash of lime still goes in for the top note, but vinegar carries the acid.

Storage. The broth without garlic, chili, or vegetables keeps 5 days refrigerated. Add the aromatics and the pickled vegetables on the day of serving; the papaya goes soft after about a day in the liquid.
