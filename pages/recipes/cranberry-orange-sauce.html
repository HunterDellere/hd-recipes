Cranberry-orange sauce

The familiar whole-berry cranberry sauce, made in two stages. Two thirds of the berries cook until they burst and release their pectin, which sets the sauce. The last third goes in at the end and stays whole. Orange zest, fresh juice and a pinch of salt keep it bright rather than only sweet.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Make it 1 to 5 days ahead. It needs at least 4 hours in the fridge to set, and it tastes rounder after a day.
  • Serve it cool or at room temperature: take it out of the fridge 1 hour before dinner.

Produce 2

  1. 340 g fresh or frozen cranberries
  2. 1 Orange

Pantry 2

  1. 140 g Granulated sugar
  2. 1 g Kosher salt

Other 1

  1. 60 g water

Method

  1. 1
    Make the syrup. In a medium saucepan, stir the 140 g / ⅔ cup granulated sugar, about 40 g / 3 tbsp of the orange juice, the 60 g / ¼ cup water and the 1 g / ¼ tsp kosher salt over medium heat with a wooden spoon until the sugar dissolves and the syrup simmers.
  2. 2
    First stage. Add about 225 g / 2 cups (two thirds) of the 340 g cranberries and simmer, stirring now and then, until nearly all of them have burst and the liquid turns glossy and thick, 6 to 8 minutes. Bursting releases the pectin in the skins and flesh, and the sugar and acid let that pectin form a gel as it cools.
  3. 3
    Second stage. Add the remaining 115 g / 1 cup cranberries and the orange zest (about 3 g / 1 tbsp) and cook 2 minutes more, only until the new berries turn bright and the first few split. Most of them stay whole, which gives the sauce its texture.
  4. 4
    Finish off the heat. Stir in the remaining orange juice (about 40 g / 3 tbsp). Fresh juice added cold keeps the orange aroma that boiling drives off. The sauce will look loose; it sets as it cools.
  5. 5
    Taste and store. It should taste tart first, then sweet, with orange at the end. If it tastes only sweet, add fresh orange or lemon juice 5 g / 1 tsp at a time. Cool it 30 minutes, then transfer it to a glass jar, lid it and refrigerate at least 4 hours, up to 5 days.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion occasion→a spiced version

    Simmer 1 cinnamon stick and 2 thin slices of fresh ginger in the syrup, and take them out before the second stage of berries goes in.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • granulated sugar → light brown sugar or maple syrup Same weight. Darker and rounder in flavor, and the color turns brick red instead of bright red.
  • orange, zest and juice → a clementine or a blood orange Use 2 clementines for the same juice. Blood orange deepens the color.

Notes

Why two stages. Berries cooked until they burst give pectin and body but no texture. Berries added at the end give texture but no body. Splitting them gives a sauce that sets and still has whole berries in it.

Why there is so little water. Cranberries are about 87% water and release most of it as they burst. More water would make a thin sauce that needs a long reduction, and a long boil dulls the color and the flavor.

Why salt in a sweet sauce. A small amount of salt reduces how bitter and flat the cranberry skins taste, so the fruit tastes brighter. At 1 g per batch, nobody tastes the salt itself.
