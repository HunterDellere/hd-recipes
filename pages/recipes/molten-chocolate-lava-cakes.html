Molten chocolate lava cakes

Two 4-inch molten chocolate cakes built on brown butter and 70% dark chocolate, lifted by a ribbon-stage egg foam with an extra yolk for a custard-thick core, and baked hot and fast so the rim sets while the center stays liquid. About 30 minutes start to finish, one saucepan and one bowl.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) with a rimmed sheet tray on the middle rack before anything else. The preheated tray hits the pan bases with immediate heat, which sets the bottom crust fast enough to hold the liquid center when you invert.
  • Eggs must be at room temperature to whip to full volume. Cold eggs straight from the fridge go into a bowl of warm tap water for 5 minutes.
  • Move the ice cream from the freezer to the fridge when the cakes go into the oven. Ten to fifteen minutes softens it to clean scoops; rock-hard ice cream shatters the cake when you press a scoop against it.
  • Make-ahead option: the filled pans hold in the fridge, covered with a small plate, for up to 24 hours. Bake straight from cold and add 2 minutes.

Method

  1. 1
    Brush the inside of two 4-inch round cake pans with the 8 g / 2 tsp softened butter, covering the walls completely. Press a parchment circle onto each base, butter the parchment with what is left on the brush, then add the 5 g / 2 tsp Dutch-process cocoa, rotate to coat every surface, and knock out the excess. Cocoa instead of flour keeps the outside dark and adds no raw-flour taste. Set the pans aside.
  2. 2
    Melt the 100 g / 7 tbsp butter in a small light-colored saucepan over medium heat. It foams, the foam subsides, and the milk solids on the bottom turn amber and smell of toasted hazelnut, about 4 to 5 minutes. Pull the pan off the burner the moment it reaches amber; the solids go from brown to burnt within 20 seconds. Technique: Brown butter (beurre noisette)
  3. 3
    Let the brown butter stand off heat for 2 minutes so it drops below the scorch range for chocolate. Add the 120 g / 4¼ oz chopped dark chocolate and leave it untouched for 1 minute, then stir slowly with a wooden spoon until smooth and glossy. Dissolve the 1 g / ½ tsp instant espresso powder in the 2 g / ½ tsp vanilla extract and stir it in. The mixture should read 45 to 50°C (113 to 122°F): warm to the touch, not hot. If any chocolate remains unmelted, set the saucepan over the lowest heat for 10-second bursts. Technique: Chocolate-butter emulsion
  4. 4
    In a medium bowl, beat the 2 room-temperature eggs, 2 egg yolks, 50 g / ¼ cup granulated sugar, and 1.5 g / ¼ tsp fine sea salt with an electric hand mixer on high for 3 to 4 minutes (or a balloon whisk for about 6). Stop at the ribbon stage: the mixture is pale, roughly tripled, and a trail dropped from the beaters holds on the surface for 3 seconds. This foam is the only lift the cake gets, and the dissolved salt seasons the chocolate from the inside.
  5. 5
    Pour the warm chocolate mixture down the side of the bowl in two additions, folding with a large metal spoon after each: cut down through the center, sweep along the bottom, and turn the batter over. Then sift the 14 g / 1½ tbsp all-purpose flour over the surface and fold just until no dry streaks remain. Some loss of volume is normal; stop folding as soon as the batter is uniform.
  6. 6
    Divide the batter between the prepared pans, about 210 g each, which fills them roughly 1 inch deep. Set the pans on the preheated sheet tray.
  7. 7
    Bake 11 to 13 minutes (13 to 15 if the batter was refrigerated). Pull when the outer ring of about ¾ inch is set, risen, and matte, and has begun to pull away from the wall, while the center circle of about 2 inches is still slightly glossy and wobbles like a just-set custard when you nudge the tray. Check at 11 minutes the first time you bake this in your oven; 60 seconds over turns the center from liquid to fudge.
  8. 8
    Rest the cakes in their pans for exactly 1 minute so the crust firms enough to hold. Run a thin knife around each edge, set a plate upside down over the pan, invert, wait 10 seconds, and lift the pan straight up. Peel off the parchment.
  9. 9
    Sprinkle each cake with a 0.25 g pinch from the 0.5 g flaky sea salt and set one scoop from the 130 g vanilla bean ice cream beside it, not on top, so the cold does not set the lava before it reaches the plate. Serve immediately; the center keeps cooking from residual heat and is fully liquid only for the first 5 minutes or so.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Texture molten center→guaranteed ganache core

    Warm 30 g / 2 tbsp heavy cream until steaming and pour it over 30 g / 1 oz chopped dark chocolate; stir smooth, then freeze in two equal mounds on parchment for at least 1 hour (or keep a stash frozen for weeks). Fill each pan halfway, press a frozen ganache disc into the center, cover with the remaining batter, and bake 13 to 14 minutes. The core stays liquid even if the cake bakes a minute or two long, at the cost of a slightly less custardy center.

  • Occasion serving size→single cake

    Scale to 1 serving: 1 whole egg, 1 yolk, and half of every other quantity. Whipping a single egg plus yolk works better with a balloon whisk in a small bowl than with a hand mixer, which cannot reach that little volume. Bake time is unchanged.

  • Dietary flour→gluten-free

    Replace the 14 g / 1½ tbsp all-purpose flour with 12 g / 2 tbsp Dutch-process cocoa powder, sifted in the same way. The cake becomes slightly darker and more fragile at the rim, so rest it 90 seconds instead of 60 before inverting.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • dark chocolate (70% cacao) → 60 to 64% bittersweet chocolate Sweeter and softer. Cut the granulated sugar to 35 g so the cake does not read as candy. Avoid chocolate chips: their stabilizers resist melting into a smooth emulsion.
  • instant espresso powder → finely ground instant coffee Same function at the same 1 g. The coffee does not read as coffee at this level; it deepens the roasted notes of the cocoa.
  • large eggs → pasteurized-in-shell eggs The center of a lava cake is undercooked egg by design. For anyone who is pregnant, immunocompromised, very young, or elderly, pasteurized eggs remove the salmonella risk with no change in method.
  • Dutch-process cocoa powder (pan dusting) → granulated sugar A sugar coating gives a thin crackly crust instead of a matte dark one. Do not use flour, which leaves a pale, pasty film on a dark cake.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → vanilla bean ice cream make it yourselfstub A custard base with a full measure of vanilla bean paste carries far more aroma than most commercial ice cream, and it is the cold, perfumed counterweight this cake is built around.

Notes

Why the center stays liquid. A lava cake is a timing trick, not a different batter. Heat moves inward from the pan walls and the hot sheet tray, and egg proteins set progressively from the outside: yolks thicken from about 65°C and whole-egg custard firms near 80°C. At 425°F the rim passes that threshold in minutes while the center is still below it. Pulling the cake in that window, then resting only 60 seconds, captures a cake that is set at the edge and a warm custard at the core. A lower oven gives a more even cake and no lava.

Why the extra yolks. Yolks bring emulsifiers and fat without the extra water and stiff-setting proteins of the whites. Two whole eggs plus two yolks makes a center that is thick and glossy, like a pudding, instead of thin and runny.

Why brown butter. Browning drives off the butter's water and builds toasted, nutty compounds from the milk solids. Against 70% chocolate those notes read as depth rather than as a separate flavor. The hot butter also melts the chocolate on its own, so no double boiler is needed.

Seasoning. Salt goes into the egg foam, where it dissolves completely, and a few flakes go on top at the end. The first suppresses bitterness and makes the chocolate taste more like itself; the second gives bright contrast against the sweet ice cream. Espresso at 1 g is under the threshold where coffee is recognizable.

Why 4-inch pans, and how full. A 4-inch pan filled about 1 inch deep gives a wider liquid zone than a deep ramekin, which makes the lava spill across the plate when you cut it. Filling deeper than about 1¼ inches pushes the bake time up and risks a dome that cracks before the edge sets. For a fully baked cake in the same pans, the Individual decadent chocolate cakes recipe uses the same chocolate-butter base with more flour.

If it fails. A cake that collapses into a puddle on inversion came out 1 to 2 minutes early, or rested too briefly. A cake that is fudgy all the way through baked too long. Both are still very good with ice cream; adjust the time by 1 minute next round and note it for your oven.
