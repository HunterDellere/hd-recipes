Tuong ot (Vietnamese chili-garlic sauce, Chin-su style)

The smooth, glossy, sweet-hot chili-garlic sauce that sits in a squeeze bottle on every pho and banh mi counter in Vietnam, best known by the Chin-su brand. Fresh red chilies and a heavy garlic load are simmered in a light sugar-vinegar brine, blended, sieved, and set with a small tapioca slurry into a pourable, cling-to-the-spoon sauce. Sweeter, brighter, and more garlic-forward than Thai-style sriracha, with no fermentation and no tomato.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Wear gloves to stem and chop the chilies, and run the blender with the lid vented away from your face. Capsaicin aerosolizes when hot chili purée is blended.
  • The sauce tastes harsh and thin when hot. It needs 12 to 24 hours in the fridge for the garlic to mellow and the starch to fully set; make it a day before you need it.
  • Have a clean glass jar or squeeze bottle ready. The sauce keeps about 6 weeks refrigerated because of its vinegar, sugar, and salt load, but only if it goes into a clean container.

What to prep, in order 3 phases ▾

Step 1 · Chili base

  1. 220 g fresh red Fresno or long red chilies, stemmed and roughly chopped, seeds left in — (about 12 Fresnos; the fleshy chilies build body and the bright red-orange color)
  2. 30 g fresh red Thai chilies, stemmed
  3. 45 g garlic cloves, peeled — (about 10 to 12 cloves; the Chin-su profile is distinctly garlic-forward)
  4. 180 g water

Step 2 · Seasoning

  1. 70 g granulated sugar
  2. 9 g fine sea salt
  3. 60 g rice vinegar
  4. 1.5 g MSG

Step 3 · Set

  1. 8 g tapioca starch
  2. 30 g cold water

Method

  1. 1
    Combine the 220 g Fresno chilies, the 30 g Thai chilies, the 45 g / 10 to 12 garlic cloves, and the 180 g / ¾ cup water in a small heavy saucepan. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, cover, and cook 8 minutes, until the chilies are fully soft and the garlic yields to a spoon. This short cook tames raw allicin bite and softens chili skins so they purée smooth, but stops well short of browning: the target flavor is fresh chili, not roasted. Technique: Simmering
  2. 2
    Add the 70 g / ⅓ cup granulated sugar, the 9 g / 1½ tsp fine sea salt, and the 1.5 g / ¼ tsp MSG; stir until dissolved, about 30 seconds. Seasoning the hot liquid before blending dissolves everything fully and lets the salt pull pigment and capsaicin out of the chili flesh.
  3. 3
    Transfer to a blender with the 60 g / ¼ cup rice vinegar. Vent the lid, cover with a towel, and blend on high 60 to 90 seconds until completely smooth. Adding the vinegar off heat preserves its volatile sharpness; boiled vinegar reads flat.
  4. 4
    Press the purée through a fine-mesh sieve back into the rinsed saucepan, working it with a ladle or spatula. Discard the seeds and skin left behind (about 2 tbsp). Sieving is what separates Chin-su-style tuong ot from rustic chili paste: the sauce should be satin-smooth with no visible flecks.
  5. 5
    Whisk the 8 g / 1 tbsp tapioca starch into the 30 g / 2 tbsp cold water. Bring the sieved purée back to a bare simmer over medium-low, whisk in the slurry, and cook 1 to 2 minutes, whisking, until the sauce turns glossy and coats a spoon in an even, opaque layer. Tapioca gelatinizes clear and cuts cleanly from a squeeze-bottle tip; cornstarch sets duller and goes slightly gummy as it cools.
  6. 6
    Taste hot, knowing heat exaggerates sweetness. The balance should read sweet first, then garlic, then a building chili heat, with vinegar as a clean back note. If flat, add 1 g / a pinch of fine sea salt; if harsh, 5 g / 1 tsp granulated sugar; if cloying, 5 g / 1 tsp rice vinegar. Cool, transfer to a clean jar or squeeze bottle, and refrigerate at least 12 hours before using.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Heat heat→as a hotter northern-style bottle (tuong ot cay)

    Swap the ratio: 120 g Fresno or long red chilies and 100 g Thai chilies. Reduce the granulated sugar to 50 g. The color goes deeper red and the sauce reads lean and hot, closer to what central Vietnamese cooks keep for bun bo Hue.

  • Texture texture→as a rustic, unsieved chili-garlic sauce

    Skip the sieve and pulse rather than blend, leaving flecks of seed and skin. Drop the tapioca starch entirely; the unsieved pulp has enough body on its own. This is the house-made style many pho shops put out in a small dish alongside the bottled sauce.

  • Occasion occasion→as a dipping sauce for fried spring rolls or banh xeo

    Loosen 60 g / ¼ cup of the finished sauce with 15 g / 1 tbsp lime juice and 15 g / 1 tbsp water. The extra acid stands up to deep-fried wrappers.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • fresh red Fresno or long red chilies → red jalapeños or red cayenne chilies Red jalapeños are slightly more vegetal and give a more orange sauce; cayenne runs hotter, so cut the Thai chilies to 10 g.
  • tapioca starch → potato starch Same clear gloss; potato starch gelatinizes at a lower temperature, so pull it as soon as it thickens.
  • MSG → omit and add 3 g / ½ tsp more fine sea salt The sauce stays bright and hot but loses the rounded, finished quality of the bottled reference.

Notes

What defines the Chin-su style: smooth, glossy, bright red-orange, sweet before hot, and loaded with garlic. It is a table sauce meant to be squeezed into soup, onto banh mi, and beside fried food, so it has to hold its heat and color in hot broth without separating. The starch set is what lets it do that; an unthickened chili purée bleeds into a pho bowl as an orange slick.

Why no fermentation. Thai Si Racha and American sriracha start from a salted chili mash that sits for days or weeks, which builds lactic sourness and a funky depth. Vietnamese bottled tuong ot is cooked fresh and acidified with vinegar instead, so it tastes cleaner and fruitier. If you want the fermented profile, that is a different sauce.

Color holding. Capsanthin and the other red carotenoids in the chilies are fat-soluble and heat-stable, but long boiling dulls them to brick. The 8-minute covered simmer is enough to soften the fruit without cooking the color out; blending and sieving afterward does the rest of the work mechanically.

Storage. Refrigerated in a clean jar, the sauce holds its color and flavor for about 6 weeks. Failure looks like a dull brown top layer (oxidation, harmless but flat-tasting) or any fuzzy spotting or fizzing on opening, which means discard. A thin watery layer that separates after a few weeks is starch retrogradation; shake and use.
