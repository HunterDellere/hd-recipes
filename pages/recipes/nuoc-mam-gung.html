Nuoc mam gung (ginger fish sauce)

The ginger-heavy nuoc cham served with poached chicken, boiled duck, and steamed fish. Fresh ginger is pounded with sugar in a mortar until it weeps juice, then loosened with fish sauce, lime, and a little water. Thicker, hotter on the palate, and less diluted than everyday nuoc cham, so it clings to plain poached meat that has no seasoning of its own.

What to prep, in order 2 phases ▾

The paste

  1. 30 g fresh young ginger, peeled, thinly sliced across the grain — (a 2-inch / 5 cm knob; young ginger with thin skin is juicier and less fibrous)
  2. 5 g garlic clove, peeled — (1 clove)
  3. 4 g fresh red Thai chili — (1 to 2 chilies)
  4. 30 g granulated sugar

The liquid

  1. 45 g fish sauce
  2. 25 g fresh lime juice
  3. 30 g warm water

Method

  1. 1
    Put the 5 g / 1 garlic clove, the 4 g / 1 to 2 Thai chilies, and the 30 g / 2½ tbsp granulated sugar in a mortar. Pound 30 seconds until the garlic and chili break down into the sugar. Technique: Mortar pounding (clay vs granite)
  2. 2
    Add the 30 g sliced ginger and pound another 2 to 3 minutes, until it collapses into a wet, fibrous pulp and the sugar has turned to a syrup with the ginger juice. Pounding ruptures the cells and releases the gingerol-rich juice in a way that grating does not; the sugar crystals act as grit, and the dissolved sugar holds the volatiles in solution. Technique: Mortar pounding (clay vs granite)
  3. 3
    Stir in the 45 g / 3 tbsp fish sauce, the 25 g / 5 tsp lime juice, and the 30 g / 2 tbsp warm water until any remaining sugar dissolves. Leave the ginger pulp in; it is the point of the sauce.
  4. 4
    Rest 5 minutes, then taste on a piece of the chicken or fish it will be served with rather than off the spoon. It should read salty and sweet in equal measure with a sharp ginger burn and only a background sour note. If the ginger reads thin, pound in 5 g more; if too salty, 10 g / 2 tsp more warm water.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Protein duck→as nuoc mam gung for boiled duck (vit luoc)

    Increase the ginger to 45 g and add 5 g / 1 tsp of the duck poaching liquid in place of 5 g of the water. Duck fat coats the palate more than chicken and needs the stronger ginger edge.

  • Texture texture→as a grated-ginger version without a mortar

    Grate the ginger on a microplane, then muddle it with the granulated sugar in a bowl with the back of a spoon for 1 minute before adding the liquids. Slightly less juicy and more fibrous, but close.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • fresh lime juice → calamansi juice Rounder, more floral acid, common in southern Vietnam (quat). Use the same weight.

Notes

Why ginger with poached poultry. Poached chicken and boiled duck carry a faint gamy, sulfurous note from the fat and skin. Ginger's gingerols and shogaols read as heat and freshness that cut that note, and the Vietnamese pairing is so fixed that ga luoc served with plain nuoc cham reads as a mistake.

Concentration. This is deliberately a thicker, saltier sauce than nuoc cham. It is a dip, not a dressing, and plain meat dragged through it should come out fully seasoned. Do not dilute it toward the nuoc cham ratio.

Storage. Best the day it is made. By day 2 the ginger oxidizes and the sauce turns faintly bitter and loses its top note. Refrigerated it is safe for 4 to 5 days; past that the garlic sours.
