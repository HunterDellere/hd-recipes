Scallion pancakes (cong you bing)

A hot-water dough rolled thin, spread with a seasoned oil-flour paste (you mian) and scallions, rolled into a rope, coiled, and flattened, so every pancake fries up with dozens of flaky, chewy layers. Served with a soy and black vinegar dip.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • The dough needs to rest at least 30 minutes (up to 2 hours) after mixing, and the shaped coils need another 15 minutes before flattening. Mix the dough first, then slice the scallions and make the paste and dip while it rests.
  • Slice and dry the scallions well. Wet scallions steam the dough from inside, tear the sheets when you roll, and spatter in the pan.

What to prep, in order 4 phases ▾

Dough

  1. 300 g all-purpose flour (for the dough)
  2. 3 g fine sea salt (for the dough)
  3. 120 ml boiling water
  4. 80 ml room-temperature water

Oil paste (you mian) and filling

  1. 25 g all-purpose flour (for the paste)
  2. 5 g fine sea salt (for the paste)
  3. 1 g ground white pepper
  4. 50 g neutral oil (for the paste)
  5. 120 g scallions, washed, dried thoroughly, thinly sliced (white and green parts) — (about 10 scallions, 1 large bunch)

Frying

  1. 60 g neutral oil (for frying)

Dipping sauce

  1. 30 g light soy sauce
  2. 30 g Chinkiang (black) vinegar
  3. 3 g granulated sugar
  4. 5 g fresh ginger, peeled, cut into very fine matchsticks — (a thumbnail-size piece)
  5. 15 g chili oil optional

Method

  1. 1
    Make the hot-water dough. In a bowl, whisk the 300 g / 2⅓ cups flour for the dough with the 3 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt. Pour in the 120 ml / ½ cup boiling water while stirring with chopsticks or a wooden spoon, until shaggy flakes form. Boiling water gelatinizes part of the starch and weakens gluten, which makes a dough that rolls thin without springing back and fries up tender-chewy instead of tough. Add the 80 ml / ⅓ cup room-temperature water and stir until no dry flour remains.
  2. 2
    Knead and rest. When the dough is cool enough to handle, knead it in the bowl or on the counter for 3 to 4 minutes, until smooth and soft (it will be slightly tacky; dust with up to 10 g / 1 tbsp flour only if it sticks badly). Cover the bowl with a damp kitchen towel or an upturned plate and rest at room temperature for at least 30 minutes, up to 2 hours. Resting relaxes the gluten so the dough will roll paper-thin.
  3. 3
    Make the oil paste (you mian). In a small heatproof bowl, combine the 25 g / 3 tbsp flour for the paste, the 5 g / ¾ tsp fine sea salt, and the 1 g / ½ tsp ground white pepper. Heat the 50 g / ¼ cup neutral oil in a small saucepan until it shimmers (about 180 °C / 350 °F; a scallion slice dropped in sizzles hard at once), pour it over the flour mixture, and stir to a smooth, loose paste. The hot oil lightly toasts the flour. This paste carries all of the seasoning for the pancake interior, so taste a dab: it should be distinctly salty. Technique: Hot-oil finish (smoking-oil bloom over raw aromatics)
  4. 4
    Make the dip. Stir together the 30 g / 2 tbsp light soy sauce, the 30 g / 2 tbsp Chinkiang vinegar, and the 3 g / ¾ tsp granulated sugar until the sugar dissolves. Add the 5 g ginger matchsticks and, if using, the 15 g / 1 tbsp chili oil. Set aside so the ginger infuses.
  5. 5
    Roll the sheets. Cut the rested dough into quarters, about 125 g each, and keep the pieces you are not working with under the damp towel. Lightly oil the counter and a rolling pin with a few drops from the 60 g frying oil (oil instead of flour, so the surface stays tacky). Roll one piece into a rectangle about 20 by 35 cm, thin enough that you can almost see the counter through it.
  6. 6
    Laminate and coil. Spread a quarter of the oil paste (about 20 g / 1½ tbsp) over the sheet in a thin even layer, leaving a 1 cm border. Scatter a quarter of the scallions (30 g) evenly over it. Starting from a long side, roll the sheet up tightly into a long rope, pinching the seam closed. Gently stretch the rope a little longer, then coil it like a snail shell and tuck the outer end underneath. Repeat with the remaining 3 pieces, cover the coils with the towel, and rest 15 minutes.
  7. 7
    Flatten. Press each coil flat with your palm, then roll it gently into a round about 18 cm across and 5 mm thick. Roll from the center outward with light pressure; a little scallion breaking through the surface is fine, but pressing hard fuses the layers you just built.
  8. 8
    Pan-fry. Heat a 25 to 30 cm cast-iron or carbon-steel skillet over medium heat and add 15 g / 1 tbsp of the frying oil. Lay in one pancake and cook 2 to 3 minutes, until the underside is deep golden with darker spots. Flip with a metal spatula and cook the second side 2 to 3 minutes. Too high a heat browns the outside before the inner layers cook through; medium lets the steam from the dough puff the layers apart.
  9. 9
    Separate the layers. While the pancake is still in the pan, use two metal spatulas (or a spatula and a wooden spoon) to push the edges toward the center a few times, as if scrunching it. The pancake will compress and spring back with its layers loosened and visible. Flip once more for 30 seconds to crisp the newly exposed edges. Move to a wire rack set over a sheet pan and keep warm in a 95 °C / 200 °F oven while you fry the rest, adding 15 g / 1 tbsp oil for each pancake.
  10. 10
    Serve. Cut each pancake into 6 wedges with a sharp knife or kitchen shears and serve hot with the dip, which brings the acid (the 30 g black vinegar) and the glutamate (the 30 g light soy) that the pancake itself does not carry.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Regional fat→lard-based oil paste

    Replace the 50 g neutral oil in the paste with 50 g melted lard, heated the same way. This is the older Shanghai style: the layers are flakier and the flavor richer. The pancakes are no longer vegetarian.

  • Occasion make-ahead→frozen raw pancakes

    After flattening in step 7, stack the rounds between squares of parchment and freeze flat in a covered glass or metal container for up to 2 months. Fry straight from frozen over medium-low heat, about 4 minutes per side, covering the pan for the first 2 minutes.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • Chinkiang vinegar → rice vinegar plus a few drops of dark soy Sharper and less malty; use 25 g rice vinegar and 3 g dark soy sauce.
  • ground white pepper → ground toasted Sichuan peppercorn Use 1 g. Adds a faint numbing note, common in some northern versions.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → chili oil make it yourself Homemade chili oil has toasted-spice aroma and crunchy sediment that most jarred versions lack.
  • → light soy sauce make it yourselfstub In a raw dip, the soy is tasted straight, so a well-brewed one makes a visible difference.

Notes

Why the oil paste matters. Spreading plain oil between the layers works poorly: when the coil is flattened, the oil is squeezed toward the edges and the layers fuse. A flour-and-oil paste stays where you spread it, keeping the dough sheets separated so they puff apart in the pan. It is also where the salt and pepper go, so the interior is seasoned evenly.

Why hot water. Mixing part of the flour with boiling water pre-cooks some of its starch and limits gluten development. The result rolls very thin without shrinking back and stays tender and chewy after frying, instead of turning bready or tough.

Seasoning stages. The dough gets a little salt for flavor; the oil paste is deliberately salty because it seasons the inside; the dip adds soy for savoriness and black vinegar for acid. Taste the paste before you spread it, because once the pancakes are rolled there is no adjusting.

Vegan claim assumes plain neutral oil in the paste and a chili oil without animal fat.
