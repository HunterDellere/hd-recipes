Xinjiang cumin lamb skewers (yangrou chuanr)

Small cubes of lamb shoulder threaded with pieces of lamb fat, salted with grated onion, and cooked hard and fast over charcoal or under a broiler while a coarse mix of toasted cumin, chili flakes, and salt goes on in two passes. The fat crisps and bastes the lean; a sharp onion, tomato, and green pepper salad cuts it.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Salt the lamb with the grated onion 1 to 4 hours ahead and refrigerate it. Under an hour the salt has not moved past the surface.
  • If using bamboo skewers, soak them in water for at least 30 minutes so they do not burn through. Flat metal skewers need no soaking and keep the cubes from spinning when you turn them.
  • For the grill, light the charcoal 30 minutes before cooking; the coals are ready when covered in gray ash. For the broiler, preheat it for 5 minutes with the rack set so the meat sits about 8 cm / 3 inches below the element.

What to prep, in order 3 phases ▾

Step 1 · The lamb

  1. 800 g boneless lamb shoulder, cut into 2 cm cubes
  2. 120 g lamb fat, cut into 1.5 cm squares
  3. 9 g kosher salt (for the lamb)
  4. 150 g yellow onion, grated on the large holes of a box grater — (1 medium onion)
  5. 15 g neutral oil

Step 2 · The spice mix

  1. 15 g cumin seeds
  2. 8 g Chinese chili flakes (la jiao mian)
  3. 3 g kosher salt (for the spice mix)
  4. 1 g MSG

Step 3 · Onion salad

  1. 150 g red onion, halved and sliced thin — (1 medium onion)
  2. 150 g cherry tomatoes, halved
  3. 60 g mild long green pepper (Anaheim), seeded and sliced into thin rings — (1 pepper)
  4. 20 g rice vinegar
  5. 2 g kosher salt (for the salad)

Method

  1. 1
    Salt the lamb. In a glass or ceramic bowl, toss the 800 g lamb cubes and the 120 g lamb fat squares with the 9 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt and the 150 g grated yellow onion until every piece is coated. Cover with a fitted lid or a plate and refrigerate for 1 to 4 hours. The salt dissolves into the onion juice, carries the onion flavor into the meat, and dissolves some of the surface protein so the lamb holds its moisture over high heat.
  2. 2
    Make the spice mix. Toast the 15 g / 2 tbsp cumin seeds in a dry skillet over medium heat, shaking the pan, for about 90 seconds, until they smell nutty and darken one shade. Tip them into a mortar and pound to a coarse powder, leaving some seeds half-cracked; the coarse pieces cling to the fat. Mix in the 8 g / 1 tbsp chili flakes, the 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt, and the 1 g / ¼ tsp MSG. You will have about 27 g of spice mix. Technique: Dry-toasting (grains, spices, seeds, dried chilies)
  3. 3
    Dress the salad. About 20 minutes before cooking, toss the 150 g sliced red onion, the 150 g halved cherry tomatoes, and the 60 g green pepper rings with the 20 g / 4 tsp rice vinegar and the 2 g / ½ tsp kosher salt. The salt and acid soften the raw onion's bite while the lamb cooks.
  4. 4
    Skewer. Scrape the grated onion off the lamb with your fingers; the onion pulp burns long before the meat is done. Thread the 800 g lamb onto 12 skewers, with one 1.5 cm square of the 120 g lamb fat after every three lean pieces, so each skewer carries about 75 g. Push the pieces snug against each other without compressing them; packed too tight, the centers steam. Brush the lean pieces lightly with the 15 g / 1 tbsp neutral oil.
  5. 5
    Grill method. Spread the coals in a single, even layer and set the grate as close to them as it goes, about 5 to 8 cm above. Lay the skewers across the grate and turn them every minute. After the first turn, sprinkle about 18 g (two-thirds) of the spice mix over all sides; the rendering fat makes it stick and toasts it. Cook 6 to 8 minutes in total, moving any skewer away from flare-ups when fat drips, until the lean is browned with charred edges, the fat squares are translucent and crisp at the corners, and the center of a cube reads about 63 °C / 145 °F.
  6. 6
    Broiler method. Set the skewers on a wire rack over a foil-lined sheet pan so the fat drips away instead of stewing the meat. Broil about 8 cm / 3 inches below the element for 4 minutes, until browned on top. Turn the skewers, sprinkle about 18 g (two-thirds) of the spice mix over both sides, and broil another 3 to 4 minutes, until the fat squares are crisp at the edges and the center of a cube reads about 63 °C / 145 °F. The broiler gives less char than charcoal, so let the tops get properly dark before you turn them.
  7. 7
    Finish and serve. Sprinkle the remaining 9 g (one-third) of the spice mix over the hot skewers as they come off the heat; this second pass keeps the cumin aroma fresh, because the first pass has toasted. Serve at once with the onion salad, eaten in alternating bites so the vinegar resets the palate after the fat.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Regional seasoning→with toasted sesame seeds and Sichuan peppercorn

    Add 6 g / 2 tsp toasted white sesame seeds and 1 g / ½ tsp ground Sichuan peppercorn to the spice mix. Common at Beijing and northern Chinese skewer stalls; the sesame adds a toasted note and the peppercorn a light numbing edge.

  • Occasion occasion→with flatbread

    Serve with warm wheat flatbread (Uyghur nan if a market carries it) and slide the meat off the skewers into it with a bite of the onion salad.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • lamb fat → the fattiest pieces of the shoulder If no separate fat is available, buy 900 g / 2 lb shoulder and cut the fattiest seams into the fat squares. The skewers will be a little leaner. Do not use pork fat; the dish comes from a Muslim tradition.
  • Chinese chili flakes (la jiao mian) → gochugaru or Aleppo pepper Both are milder and fruitier than Chinese chili flakes; use 1.5 times the weight for similar heat.
  • boneless lamb shoulder → boneless leg of lamb Leaner and more tender; keep the lamb fat squares, which matter even more here, and pull at the same temperature.
  • mild long green pepper (Anaheim) → Cubanelle or a half green bell pepper Any mild, thin-walled green pepper works; the salad is about crunch and a vegetal edge, not heat.

Notes

Why small cubes. A 2 cm cube browns on the outside in the same time its center reaches medium, so the skewer comes off charred and still juicy. Larger cubes force a choice between a pale outside and a dry center.

Why lamb fat. The fat squares melt as the skewer cooks, baste the lean pieces next to them, and crisp at the edges. Vendors in Xinjiang often use fat from the tail of fat-tailed sheep; American lamb fat trim does the same job. The pieces should be eaten, not trimmed off.

Why the spice goes on during cooking. Seasoned before cooking, ground cumin scorches over charcoal and turns bitter. Sprinkled on after the first turn, it lands on rendering fat, sticks, and toasts. The final pass off the heat adds fresh cumin aroma on top of the toasted note.

Umami and acid. Lamb, browned fat, and the vendor''s pinch of MSG carry the savory side. The acid comes from the vinegar-dressed onion salad rather than from anything on the meat, which is how the dish is eaten in Xinjiang.

Doneness. Lamb whole-muscle cubes are safe at 63 °C / 145 °F. Xinjiang skewers are normally cooked through to that point or a little past it, with the char doing the work; this is not a rare-lamb dish.

Sources
  • Uyghur street-vendor tradition of Xinjiang, as sold across northern China — Xinjiang vendors grill small lamb pieces alternated with lamb fat over long charcoal troughs, seasoning with salt, cumin, and chili as the skewers cook. The seasoning is deliberately simple; the lamb, the fat, and the fire do the work. The onion, tomato, and pepper salad (pilahong in Uyghur-Chinese usage) is the usual side.
