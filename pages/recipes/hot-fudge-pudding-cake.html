Hot fudge pudding cake

A self-saucing chocolate cake: a stiff cocoa batter goes into the pan, dark brown sugar and cocoa are scattered on top, and hot water is poured over everything without stirring. In the oven the cake rises through the sugar syrup and the syrup sinks underneath, thickening into a hot fudge sauce. One dish, no frosting, pantry staples only.

Step 1 · Batter

  1. 45 g unsalted butter
  2. 120 g whole milk
  3. 5 g vanilla bean paste
  4. 125 g all-purpose flour
  5. 150 g granulated sugar
  6. 22 g Dutch-process cocoa
  7. 8 g baking powder
  8. 1.5 g fine sea salt
  9. 55 g dark chocolate, chopped

Step 2 · Sauce topping

  1. 200 g dark brown sugar, packed
  2. 22 g Dutch-process cocoa
  3. 1 g fine sea salt
  4. 360 g hot water or hot brewed coffee

Method

  1. 1
    Heat the oven to 350°F (177°C) with a rack centered. Melt the unsalted butter (3 tbsp / 45 g) in an ungreased 8-inch square baking dish in the oven as it heats, 3 to 4 minutes, then pour it into a medium bowl, leaving a film in the dish. Whisk in the whole milk (½ cup / 120 g) and vanilla bean paste (1 tsp / 5 g).
  2. 2
    In a second bowl, whisk the all-purpose flour (1 cup / 125 g), granulated sugar (¾ cup / 150 g), Dutch-process cocoa (¼ cup / 22 g), baking powder (2 tsp / 8 g), and fine sea salt (¼ tsp / 1.5 g). Add the milk mixture and stir until no dry flour remains, then fold in the chopped dark chocolate (2 oz / 55 g). The batter is thick, more like brownie batter than cake batter; that density is what lets it rise up through the sauce instead of dissolving into it.
  3. 3
    Spread the batter evenly in the buttered dish. In a small bowl, mix the dark brown sugar (scant 1 cup / 200 g), Dutch-process cocoa (¼ cup / 22 g), and fine sea salt (⅛ tsp / 1 g), breaking up lumps, and scatter it evenly over the batter.
  4. 4
    Pour the hot water or hot brewed coffee (1½ cups / 360 g) slowly over the back of a spoon so it spreads across the whole surface. Do not stir. It will look wrong, like batter drowning in a puddle; that is correct. The hot liquid dissolves the sugar and blooms the cocoa into a dense syrup on top. Technique: Blooming cocoa
  5. 5
    Bake 32 to 38 minutes, until the top is set, cracked, and dry-looking, and dark sauce bubbles up at the edges and through the cracks. As the 2 tsp / 8 g baking powder aerates the batter it becomes lighter than the sugar syrup and rises through it; the syrup sinks to the bottom, picks up starch and cocoa from the underside of the cake, and thickens into fudge sauce. A tester will not come out clean because of the sauce; press the cake surface instead, which should spring back.
  6. 6
    Rest 15 minutes before serving; the sauce thickens as it cools from bubbling. Spoon into bowls, cake side up, with plenty of sauce from the bottom. A splash of cold whole milk (2 tbsp / 30 g per bowl) poured over, or a scoop of ice cream, is the classic finish.
  7. 7
    The Adjustment: if the sauce is thin and watery, it was underbaked or served too hot; bake 3 to 5 minutes longer next time and let it rest the full 15 minutes. If there is barely any sauce, it was overbaked and the cake absorbed it; pull it earlier. If it reads too sweet, use coffee rather than water and add a second ⅛ tsp / 1 g fine sea salt to the topping.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion flavor→mocha

    Use hot brewed coffee for all of the liquid. It is the single best upgrade if you have coffee on hand; the roast deepens the chocolate without tasting like coffee.

  • Occasion serving size→individual ramekins

    Divide batter, topping, and hot liquid evenly among six 8 oz ramekins on a sheet pan. Bake 20 to 25 minutes.

  • Texture add-in→toasted nuts

    Fold ½ cup / 55 g toasted chopped walnuts or pecans into the batter with the chocolate.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • Dutch-process cocoa → natural cocoa (same amounts) Works 1:1 because baking powder is the only leavener. The sauce is redder and tangier.
  • dark brown sugar → light brown sugar (same weight) Lighter, less molasses depth in the sauce.
  • unsalted butter → neutral oil (3 tbsp / 40 g) Skip the melting step. Slightly less rich.
  • dark chocolate → chocolate chips (⅓ cup / 55 g) Or leave out entirely; the cake is still very chocolatey.
  • vanilla bean paste → vanilla extract 1:1 by volume. Fallback only: you lose the seeds and some aromatic depth.

Notes

How it sauces itself. The order matters: thick batter, dry sugar-cocoa layer, hot liquid on top. The water dissolves the sugar into a syrup denser than the batter will be once it starts to rise. In the oven, baking powder releases gas and the batter''s density drops below the syrup''s, so the cake floats up and the syrup trades places with it. At the bottom the syrup reduces and absorbs starch from the cake''s underside, which thickens it into a sauce. Stirring at any point breaks the layering and you get a soggy cake with no sauce.

Why dark brown sugar on top. The topping becomes the sauce, and dark brown sugar''s molasses gives it a deeper, more caramel fudge flavor than granulated would. The pinch of salt in the topping keeps the sauce from tasting flat-sweet.

Serve warm. The sauce is best warm and pourable. Leftovers reheat well in the microwave in 30-second bursts; the cake reabsorbs some sauce overnight, so add a spoonful of water before reheating.
