Tom kha gai (Thai chicken coconut galangal soup)

Coconut milk and chicken stock infused with a heavy hand of galangal, lemongrass, and makrut lime leaf, then used to poach thin-sliced chicken thigh and oyster mushrooms. Fish sauce seasons it in the pot; lime juice goes in off the heat so it stays bright, and the soup is held below a boil so the coconut stays creamy.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Start the jasmine rice first if serving it alongside; the soup takes about 25 minutes from cold pot to bowl.
  • Partly freeze the chicken for 15 minutes if you have time; firm meat slices thin and even, and thin slices poach in 5 minutes without toughening.

What to prep, in order 3 phases ▾

Step 1 · Aromatic infusion

  1. 400 g chicken stock, low-sodium or homemade
  2. 40 g galangal, sliced into 2 mm coins, unpeeled if the skin is thin — (a 7 cm / 3-inch piece; frozen works fine)
  3. 60 g lemongrass, tough outer layer and top third removed, bottom 12 cm bruised with the back of a knife and cut into 5 cm lengths — (2 stalks)
  4. 3 g makrut lime leaves, center ribs torn out, leaves torn in half — (8 leaves; frozen leaves keep nearly all their aroma)
  5. 40 g shallots, peeled, halved, and lightly smashed — (2 small)
  6. 2 × 13.5 oz / 400 ml can full-fat coconut milk

Step 2 · Chicken and mushrooms

  1. 450 g boneless skinless chicken thigh, trimmed, sliced across the grain into 5 mm thick bite-size pieces
  2. 200 g oyster mushrooms, torn into bite-size pieces along the gills
  3. 45 g fish sauce
  4. 8 g palm sugar, shaved fine

Step 3 · Off-heat finish

  1. 45 g lime juice, fresh
  2. 3 g Thai bird chilies, lightly smashed, left whole — (4 to 6 chilies; fewer for a mild soup)
  3. 10 g cilantro, leaves and tender stems, roughly chopped
  4. 30 g nam prik pao (Thai roasted chili jam) optional

Method

  1. 1
    Infuse the stock. In a 4-liter saucepan, combine the 400 g / 1⅔ cups chicken stock with the 40 g galangal coins, 60 g / 2 stalks bruised lemongrass, half of the 3 g / 8 torn lime leaves, and the 40 g smashed shallots. Bring to a simmer over medium-high heat and simmer 5 minutes. Steeping the aromatics in the thinner stock first, before the coconut fat goes in, extracts them faster, and the hard simmer is safe because there is no coconut milk yet.
  2. 2
    Add the coconut milk. Pour in the 800 g / 2 cans coconut milk and heat over medium until small bubbles break steadily at the edges, about 90 °C. From here on, keep the soup at a gentle simmer and never let it roll; a hard boil breaks the coconut emulsion and the soup turns oily and grainy.
  3. 3
    Poach the chicken. Add the 450 g sliced chicken thigh, 30 g / 1½ tbsp of the fish sauce, and the 8 g / 2 tsp palm sugar, and stir to separate the slices. Seasoning now lets the chicken absorb salt as it cooks. Hold at a gentle simmer 2 minutes, then add the 200 g torn oyster mushrooms and cook 3 to 4 minutes more, until the chicken is opaque through and reads 74 °C / 165 °F on an instant-read thermometer and the mushrooms are tender.
  4. 4
    Finish off the heat. Turn off the burner. Stir in the 45 g / 3 tbsp lime juice, the remaining lime leaves, and the 3 g / 4 to 6 smashed chilies. Lime juice boiled turns dull and faintly bitter, so it always goes in last, off the heat. Taste and adjust with the remaining 15 g / 1 tbsp fish sauce: the soup should taste salty and sour first, rich from the coconut, with sweetness in the background and chili heat building slowly. If it tastes flat, add fish sauce; if heavy, add 5 g / 1 tsp more lime juice.
  5. 5
    Serve. Ladle into bowls, scatter with the 10 g / ½ cup cilantro, and, if using, drizzle about 7 g / 1½ tsp nam prik pao over each bowl. Serve with jasmine rice. The galangal, lemongrass, and lime leaves stay in the soup for aroma but are pushed aside, not eaten.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Protein protein→tom kha goong (shrimp)

    Replace the chicken with 400 g peeled large shrimp. Add them with the mushrooms and pull the pot off the heat as soon as they turn opaque, about 2 minutes. Use 400 g / 1⅔ cups shrimp-shell stock or seafood stock in place of chicken stock.

  • Dietary vegetarian→tom kha het (mushroom and tofu)

    Use vegetable broth, 400 g mixed mushrooms, and 200 g cubed soft tofu. Replace the fish sauce with 30 g / 2 tbsp light soy sauce plus 3 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt; the mushrooms then carry most of the glutamate.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • galangal → frozen or dried galangal Frozen slices work the same as fresh. Dried galangal slices are acceptable at 15 g, simmered 10 minutes in step 1. Ginger is not a substitute: it is hot and sweet where galangal is piney and citrusy, and the soup becomes a different dish.
  • oyster mushrooms → canned straw mushrooms, halved, or fresh shiitake caps, sliced Straw mushrooms are the Thai default but canned ones taste of the can; rinse well. Shiitake are stronger and darker but add more glutamate.
  • chicken thigh → chicken breast Slice thin and pull at 74 °C / 165 °F without letting the soup simmer hard. Breast is leaner and dries faster.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → chicken stock, low-sodium or homemade make it yourself A gelatin-rich homemade stock gives the soup body under the coconut and lets you control the salt, which matters when fish sauce is the main seasoning.
  • → full-fat coconut milk make it yourselfstub Fresh-pressed coconut milk tastes cleaner and more fragrant than canned, and in a soup where coconut is half the liquid the difference is easy to taste.
  • → nam prik pao (Thai roasted chili jam) make it yourselfstub Homemade chili jam is smokier and less sugary than most jars, and a batch keeps for months in the fridge for soups, stir-fries, and dips.

Notes

Galangal is the point. The name means boiled galangal, and 40 g is deliberately a lot. Its piney, peppery, citrusy aroma is what separates tom kha from a coconut chicken soup. Slice it thin so more surface area meets the liquid.

Why the infusion starts in stock. The aromatic compounds in lemongrass, galangal, and lime leaf are only partly fat-soluble. A short hard simmer in stock pulls them out quickly. Coconut milk cannot take that boil, so it goes in second and is held gently.

Keeping the coconut smooth. Coconut milk is an emulsion of fat in water held together by coconut proteins. A rolling boil splits it, and the soup turns greasy with grainy flecks. For curries, Thai cooks crack coconut cream on purpose to fry paste in the released oil; for tom kha the goal is the opposite, a creamy, unbroken broth.

Balance. Tom kha is gentler than tom yum: less chili, less sour, with coconut richness in front. Fish sauce provides salt and glutamate, mushrooms add more, and lime is the finishing acid. Season fish sauce in two stages (with the chicken, then to taste at the end) because the chicken and mushrooms absorb salt as they cook.

Seasonality. Galangal, lemongrass, and makrut lime leaves are sold fresh or frozen year-round at Thai and Southeast Asian grocers in US cities. Buy extra and freeze them; all three keep for months in the freezer.
