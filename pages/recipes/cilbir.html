Recipe

Çılbır

Turkish poached eggs set on a bed of thick, garlicky strained yogurt at room temperature, then drowned in butter that has been lightly browned and stained brick red with pul biber. Finished with dill and eaten with bread to drag through the yogurt, yolk, and butter.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Take the yogurt out of the refrigerator 30 to 45 minutes ahead. Cold yogurt chills the eggs and butter on contact, so the butter seizes into flecks and the dish arrives lukewarm. Room-temperature yogurt keeps everything warm and silky.
  • Toast the bread and make the pepper butter before the eggs go into the water; the eggs take 4 minutes and the dish should go to the table the moment they come out.

What to prep, in order 4 phases ▾

Step 1 · Garlic yogurt

  1. 300 g strained full-fat yogurt (Greek or Turkish süzme)
  2. 4 g garlic clove, grated on a microplane — (1 small clove)
  3. 2 g fine sea salt

Step 2 · Pepper butter

  1. 45 g unsalted butter
  2. 4 g pul biber (Aleppo pepper)

Step 3 · Poached eggs

  1. 200 g large eggs, as fresh as possible — (4 large eggs, straight from the refrigerator)
  2. 1500 ml water (for poaching)
  3. 30 g white wine vinegar

Step 4 · To serve

  1. 4 g fresh dill, fronds picked and roughly chopped
  2. 1 g flaky salt — (a generous pinch)
  3. 200 g crusty bread (sourdough, pide, or simit), thick slices, toasted or grilled — (4 thick slices)

Method

  1. 1
    Season the yogurt. Stir the 4 g / 1 small grated garlic clove and 2 g / ⅓ tsp fine sea salt into the 300 g / 1¼ cups room-temperature yogurt until smooth. Let it sit while you work: 10 minutes takes the raw harshness off the garlic as its allicin spreads through the fat. Taste; it should be clearly garlicky and well salted, since it seasons the whole dish. Spread it in two shallow bowls, swooping a well into the center of each.
  2. 2
    Make the pepper butter. Melt the 45 g / 3 tbsp butter in a small saucepan over medium heat. Let it foam and cook, swirling, for 2 to 3 minutes until the foam subsides and the milk solids at the bottom turn pale tan and smell like toasted hazelnuts. Pull it off the heat immediately and stir in the 4 g / 2 tsp pul biber. The flakes sizzle and the butter turns brick red within 20 seconds as the pepper's fat-soluble pigments and aromas dissolve. Adding the pepper off the heat is the point: on the burner its sugars scorch and turn bitter in seconds. Set aside in a warm spot; it stays liquid. Technique: Brown butter (beurre noisette)
  3. 3
    Prepare the eggs. Crack each of the 4 eggs into a fine-mesh sieve set over a bowl and let the thin, watery outer white drain away for 20 seconds, then tip each egg into its own small cup. Removing the loose white is what prevents ragged wisps in the water; only the firm white that hugs the yolk remains.
  4. 4
    Poach. Bring the 1500 ml / 6 cups water to a bare simmer in a wide saucepan or deep skillet, about 6 cm / 2½ inches deep, and add the 30 g / 2 tbsp white wine vinegar. Lower the heat until only small bubbles rise from the bottom (about 85 °C / 185 °F). Lower the cups to the surface and slide the 4 eggs in one at a time, spaced apart. Poach 3½ to 4 minutes, until the whites are opaque and set but the yolks wobble when nudged with a slotted spoon. The vinegar speeds the setting of the white at the surface so it holds its shape; it leaves no taste at this dilution.
  5. 5
    Lift each egg out with a slotted spoon, let it drain, and blot the bottom briefly on a folded kitchen towel so no water dilutes the yogurt. Set 2 eggs in the well of each bowl of yogurt.
  6. 6
    Finish. Stir the pepper butter, then spoon about 22 g / 1½ tbsp of it over each bowl, over the eggs and into the yogurt, including the red sediment. Scatter the 4 g / 2 tbsp chopped dill over both bowls and sprinkle the 1 g pinch flaky salt over the eggs. Serve at once with the 200 g / 4 thick slices toasted bread.
Safety
  • Poached eggs with set whites and liquid yolks The yolk stays below the temperature and hold time that pasteurize it. For healthy adults with fresh eggs the risk is low; for pregnancy, young children, older or immunocompromised eaters, use pasteurized in-shell eggs (they poach the same way, with slightly looser whites) or poach 6 minutes for a firm yolk. Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Regional dill finish→dried mint butter

    Add 1 g / 1 tsp dried mint to the butter along with the pul biber, crumbled between your palms. The mint gives a cooler, more Anatolian finish; leave out the dill or keep a little of each.

  • Heat pul biber butter→urfa biber butter

    Use 3 g / 1½ tsp urfa biber in place of the pul biber. Urfa is darker, smoky, and raisin-like rather than bright; the butter turns deep maroon instead of red. A 50/50 blend of the two is also good.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • strained full-fat yogurt (Greek or Turkish süzme) → 500 g plain full-fat yogurt strained 2 hours in a cloth-lined sieve Unstrained yogurt is too thin: it runs into the butter and the eggs slide. Straining it yourself gives roughly the 300 g needed and a slightly tangier result.
  • pul biber (Aleppo pepper) → 2 g / 1 tsp sweet paprika plus 1 g / ½ tsp crushed red pepper A fallback only; pul biber is fruity and oily, and generic flakes are hotter and drier. Do not use smoked paprika, which makes the butter taste of bacon.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → strained full-fat yogurt (Greek or Turkish süzme) make it yourselfstub Home-strained yogurt can be taken exactly as thick as you want and is noticeably tangier and creamier than most supermarket Greek yogurt, which is sometimes thickened with added protein rather than strained.

Notes

The temperature contrast is the dish. Room-temperature yogurt, hot eggs, and hot butter: each layer is warm enough to keep the butter liquid but not so hot that the yogurt splits. Cold yogurt straight from the refrigerator makes the butter seize and the whole bowl lukewarm; warmed yogurt (heated over a pan) curdles. Room temperature is the target.

Why fresh eggs. As eggs age, the thick white thins and the yolk membrane weakens, which is why old eggs feather in the water. Straining off the thin white fixes most of this, but very fresh eggs give the tightest, roundest poach.

Why the butter is only lightly browned. Taking the butter to pale tan adds a nutty depth that plain melted butter lacks; going to full brown butter competes with the pepper. The butter is the dish's main source of savory depth.

Umami and acid. The tradition adds no separate glutamate source, and the dish does not need one: the browned milk solids, the yolk, and the long-fermented yogurt carry the savoriness. The yogurt's lactic acid is the acid, which is why no lemon is used.

Bread is part of the dish. Turkish tables serve çılbır with fresh bread to drag through the yogurt, yolk, and butter; without it, half the dish stays in the bowl.
