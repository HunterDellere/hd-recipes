Chipotle chicken tortilla soup

A broth built from bone-in chicken and charred onion and garlic, thickened with a purée of fried corn tortillas, chipotle in adobo, and cilantro, then fried in oil before the stock goes back in. Seared chipotle-marinated thighs go on top of each bowl with fresh tortillas fried into strips, melting Oaxaca cheese, avocado, crema, radish, and lime.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Start the broth first. It needs 45 to 60 minutes of simmering, and everything else (marinating the thighs, cutting and drying the tortilla strips, frying) fits inside that window.
  • The chipotle thighs need at least 30 minutes in the marinade. Overnight in the fridge is better: the salt diffuses through the whole thigh instead of seasoning only the outer few millimetres.
  • Fresh tortillas need 20 minutes spread on a rack to surface-dry before frying. Cut them as soon as the broth is simmering.

What to prep, in order 5 phases ▾

Step 1 · Broth

  1. 1100 g bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
  2. 250 g white onion, quartered through the root, skin on — (1 medium; 3 quarters go into the broth, 1 is reserved raw for the purée)
  3. 50 g garlic, whole head, halved crosswise, skin on — (1 head)
  4. 2400 ml water
  5. 10 g kosher salt

Step 2 · Chipotle chicken

  1. 700 g boneless, skinless chicken thighs

  4. 1 g Mexican oregano, crumbled between the palms

Step 3 · Tortillas

  1. 10 fresh corn tortillas — (15 cm / 6 inch; 2 fried whole for the purée, 8 cut into strips)
  2. 240 ml neutral oil (for frying)
  3. 2 g fine sea salt

Step 4 · Soup base

  1. 30 g chipotle in adobo (for the purée)
  2. 10 g cilantro (for the purée), leaves and tender stems
  3. 4 g kosher salt (final seasoning)
  4. 20 g lime juice — (1 lime)

Step 5 · Toppings

  1. 170 g queso Oaxaca, pulled into thin strands
  2. 300 g avocados, cubed just before serving — (2 medium Hass)
  3. 120 g Mexican crema
  4. 80 g radishes, sliced paper-thin — (about 6)
  5. 15 g cilantro (for serving), roughly chopped
  6. 2 lime wedges, each cut into 6 wedges — (2 limes)

Method

  1. 1
    Char the aromatics. Set a 6 to 7 L Dutch oven over medium-high heat for 2 minutes. Lay 3 quarters of the 250 g white onion and both halves of the 50 g garlic head cut side down in the dry pot and leave them for 4 to 5 minutes, until the cut faces are blackened in patches. Set the fourth onion quarter aside raw for the purée. The char adds roasted, faintly bitter pyrazine notes that a plain simmer cannot produce, and the browned residue on the pot floor dissolves into the broth. Technique: Comal charring (vegetables, chilies, tortillas)
  2. 2
    Simmer the broth. Add the 1100 g bone-in chicken thighs, 2400 ml / 10 cups water, and 10 g / 2½ tsp kosher salt. Bring to a bare simmer over medium-high, then lower the heat so only a few bubbles break the surface. Skim the grey foam that rises during the first 10 minutes. Simmer partially covered for 45 to 60 minutes, until the meat pulls easily from the bone. Keep it below a boil: rolling agitation emulsifies the rendered fat into the liquid and the broth turns cloudy and greasy. Salting now seasons the meat as it cooks rather than only the liquid around it.
  3. 3
    Marinate the chipotle chicken. While the broth simmers, stir together the 40 g minced chipotle in adobo, 10 g grated garlic, 1 g / 1 tsp crumbled Mexican oregano, 7 g / 1¾ tsp kosher salt, and 15 g / 1 tbsp neutral oil in a bowl. Add the 700 g boneless thighs and turn to coat every surface. Leave at room temperature for 30 minutes, or cover and refrigerate up to 24 hours (pull it out 30 minutes before searing).
  4. 4
    Cut and dry the tortillas. Set 2 of the 10 fresh corn tortillas aside whole. Stack the other 8 tortillas, halve the stack, and cut it into 6 mm / ¼ inch strips. Spread the strips and the 2 whole tortillas in a single layer on a wire rack and leave them uncovered for 20 minutes, until the surfaces feel dry and slightly leathery. Fresh tortillas are close to half water; frying them straight from the package makes the oil boil violently, the strips take longer to crisp, and they soak up more oil. In a hurry, dry them on a sheet pan in a 120°C / 250°F oven for 8 minutes instead.
  5. 5
    Fry the tortillas. Heat the 240 ml / 1 cup neutral oil in a 25 cm / 10 inch cast-iron or heavy skillet to 175°C / 350°F. Fry the 2 whole tortillas one at a time, 45 to 60 seconds per side, until rigid and golden; drain on a rack. Fry the strips in 3 batches, stirring with a spider, for 90 seconds to 2 minutes each. The cue is the sound and the bubbles: the vigorous fizz of escaping water quiets to small lazy bubbles when the strips are done. Lift them onto the rack and sprinkle with the 2 g / ½ tsp fine sea salt while still hot, so it sticks. Pour off all but 30 g / 2 tbsp of the oil into a heatproof jar and set the skillet aside unwashed for the chicken. Technique: Deep-frying
  6. 6
    Shred the chicken and strain the broth. Lift the bone-in thighs onto a plate and let them cool 10 minutes. Strain the broth through a fine-mesh sieve into a large bowl and discard the onion and garlic. You should have about 2 L. Pull the meat into bite-size shreds, discarding the skin and bones.
  7. 7
    Blend the soup base. In a blender, combine the reserved raw onion quarter from the 250 g white onion, the 30 g chipotle in adobo, the 10 g / ¼ cup cilantro, the 2 fried whole tortillas broken into pieces, and 500 ml / 2 cups of the hot broth. Remove the centre cap of the blender lid and cover the hole with a folded towel so steam can escape, then blend on high for 60 seconds until completely smooth. The fried tortillas carry nixtamalized corn starch that gelatinizes in the hot liquid, which gives the soup body and a toasted-corn flavor without any flour or cream.
  8. 8
    Fry the purée. Wipe out the Dutch oven and heat the reserved 30 g / 2 tbsp frying oil over medium-high until it shimmers. Pour in the purée (it will spatter) and cook, stirring constantly, for 4 to 5 minutes, until it darkens a shade, thickens to a paste, and smells toasted rather than raw. Frying drives off water and browns the chile and corn solids, which deepens the flavor in a way simmering cannot. Whisk in the rest of the broth, bring to a simmer, and cook 10 minutes. Stir in the shredded chicken.
  9. 9
    Sear the chipotle chicken. While the soup simmers, set the oiled skillet over medium-high heat for 2 minutes. Lay the 700 g marinated thighs in it smooth side down and leave them undisturbed for 5 to 6 minutes, until the edges char. The sugars and tomato in the adobo burn faster than bare chicken, so turn the heat to medium if the surface goes black before 4 minutes. Flip and cook 4 to 5 minutes more, to 80°C / 175°F at the thickest point; thigh meat keeps getting more tender up to that temperature as its connective tissue softens. Rest 5 minutes, then cut into 2 cm pieces. Pour a ladle of the soup into the hot skillet, scrape up the browned bits, and pour it back into the pot. Technique: Maillard sear
  10. 10
    Season and finish. Taste the soup and stir in the 4 g / 1 tsp kosher salt, a little at a time, until the broth tastes fully seasoned on its own; the toppings are lightly salted and will not fix a flat base. Turn off the heat and stir in the 20 g / 1 lime of lime juice. Acid goes in off the heat so its fresh aroma does not cook away.
  11. 11
    Assemble the bowls. Divide the 170 g pulled Oaxaca cheese and the 300 g cubed avocado among 6 wide bowls. Ladle the hot soup over them; the heat melts the cheese into strands. Top each bowl with the chopped chipotle chicken, a large handful of the tortilla strips, the 80 g sliced radishes, a spoonful of the 120 g Mexican crema, and the 15 g chopped cilantro. Serve the 2 limes of lime wedges at the table. Add the strips last and keep the extras in a bowl on the side: they soften within a few minutes in the broth, and each person adds more as they eat.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion broth→weeknight version with ready stock

    Skip phase 1. Char the onion and garlic as written, then simmer them in 2 L of homemade chicken stock for 15 minutes before straining. Increase the chipotle thighs to 1 kg so they are the only chicken in the bowl. Total time drops to about 50 minutes, most of it the tortilla drying and frying.

  • Regional chile profile→deeper and darker

    Toast 1 dried pasilla chile in a dry skillet for 20 seconds per side, stem and seed it, soak it in 120 ml hot broth for 10 minutes, and add it with its liquid to the blender. Pasilla adds a raisin-and-cocoa bitterness that sits under the chipotle smoke and moves the soup toward the Mexico City version.

  • Regional tomato→charred-tomato base

    Broil 2 Roma tomatoes (about 200 g) on a sheet pan, turning once, until blistered and soft, 8 minutes. Blend them with the purée. The soup turns redder and sharper, with more glutamate and acidity from the tomato; cut the final lime juice to half a lime.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • queso Oaxaca → low-moisture mozzarella or Monterey Jack Both melt into strands in the hot broth. Mozzarella is closest in texture; Jack is saltier and softer.
  • Mexican crema → sour cream thinned with 2 tbsp whole milk Crema is thinner and less sour than American sour cream; thinning gets the pourable consistency, and the slightly stronger tang is fine against the chipotle.
  • fresh corn tortillas → day-old packaged corn tortillas Skip the 20-minute drying step; day-old tortillas have already lost enough surface moisture. They crisp faster but taste less of corn.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → fresh corn tortillas make it yourself Tortillas pressed from masa the same day fry into strips with a far stronger toasted-corn flavor than packaged ones.
  • → chipotle in adobo (for the marinade) make it yourself A homemade batch lets you set the smoke, vinegar, and sugar levels yourself, and it keeps for weeks in the fridge for both the marinade and the soup base.

Notes

Two kinds of chicken, two jobs. The poached bone-in thighs exist to make the broth: their collagen turns to gelatin over the long simmer, and the shredded meat goes into the pot soft and broth-soaked. The seared chipotle thighs exist for the bowl: they bring charred, smoky, concentrated flavor that poached meat cannot have. Simmering the seared pieces in the soup would wash the crust off, which is why they go on top at the end.

Why the tortillas go in twice. Blended fried tortillas thicken the soup and give it a toasted-corn base; the strips on top are texture. Both come from the same oil, and the 2 tbsp of tortilla-flavored oil left in the skillet is what the purée fries in.

Fresh tortillas versus stale. Traditional cooks fry day-old tortillas because they are drier. Fresh tortillas taste better but carry more water, which is the whole reason for the 20-minute rack dry: it removes enough surface moisture that the strips crisp in under 2 minutes instead of turning chewy and greasy.

Storage. The soup (without toppings) keeps 4 days in the fridge and freezes for 3 months. Store the chipotle chicken separately and reheat it in a hot skillet, not in the soup. Tortilla strips keep 2 days at room temperature in a sealed glass jar; re-crisp them for 3 minutes in a 175°C / 350°F oven if they soften.
