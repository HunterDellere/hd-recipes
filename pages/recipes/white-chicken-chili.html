White chicken chili

Browned chicken thighs simmered in stock with broiled poblano, Anaheim, and jalapeño chiles and creamy white beans, then shredded. Mashed beans and a spoonful of crema give the broth body without flour, and lime keeps it bright. Turkey works the same way.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Cook the white beans a day or two ahead: 250 g dried cannellini gives the 700 g cooked beans and liquid needed here (see Make it from scratch). Canned beans work if you have to; use three 15 oz cans, rinsed, and replace the bean liquid with more stock.
  • Salt the chicken at least 30 minutes ahead, or up to a day.
  • The broiled chiles need 10 minutes covered before peeling. Broil them first, then brown the chicken while they steam.

Method

  1. 1
    Salt the chicken. Toss the 900 g chicken thighs with the 9 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt for the chicken. Refrigerate, covered, at least 30 minutes and up to 24 hours.
  2. 2
    Broil the chiles. Set an oven rack 10 cm / 4 in below the broiler and heat it to high. Broil the 300 g poblanos, the 250 g Anaheim chiles, and the 30 g jalapeño on a foil-lined rimmed sheet pan, turning every 3 to 4 minutes, until blistered and blackened in patches all over, about 12 minutes. Move them to a bowl and cover with a plate for 10 minutes. Rub off the skins (do not rinse), stem and seed the poblanos and Anaheims, and chop everything into 1 cm pieces. Keep the jalapeño seeds for more heat. Technique: High-heat dry roasting
  3. 3
    Brown the chicken while the chiles steam. Pat the chicken dry. Heat the 15 ml / 1 tbsp neutral oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Lay in the thighs in a single layer, in two batches if needed, and brown on one side only, about 5 minutes, without moving them. Transfer to a plate browned side up. The thighs finish cooking in the broth; browning one side builds the fond without drying them out. Technique: Maillard sear
  4. 4
    Cook the aromatics. Lower the heat to medium. Add the 250 g diced onion and the 2 g / ½ tsp kosher salt for the onion and cook 6 minutes, scraping up the fond, until soft and translucent. Add the 20 g minced garlic, 3 g / 1½ tsp ground cumin, 2 g / 1 tsp ground coriander, and 2 g / 2 tsp Mexican oregano and stir 1 minute until fragrant. Technique: Blooming aromatics
  5. 5
    Simmer the chicken. Pour in the 750 ml / 3 cups chicken stock, scrape the bottom clean, and add the chopped chiles. Return the chicken and its juices. Bring to a simmer, lower the heat so it bubbles gently, cover, and cook 20 to 25 minutes, until the thighs are tender and register at least 80 °C / 175 °F. Thighs are better taken past the usual 74 °C: their collagen keeps softening and they shred more easily.
  6. 6
    Shred. Lift the chicken onto a cutting board and pull it into bite-size shreds with two forks.
  7. 7
    Add the beans and thicken. Mash 150 g / 1 cup of the 700 g white beans to a coarse paste with a fork. Stir the mashed beans, the remaining whole beans, and the 250 ml / 1 cup bean cooking liquid into the pot with the shredded chicken. Simmer uncovered 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the broth turns creamy and slightly thick. The starch from the mashed beans does the thickening that flour does in many versions, without the pasty taste.
  8. 8
    Finish and season. Off the heat, stir in the 120 g Mexican crema, the 30 ml / 2 tbsp lime juice, and the 15 g chopped cilantro. Taste and add up to 3 g / 1 tsp kosher salt. Crema has enough fat that it will not curdle in the hot broth; if you use sour cream, temper it first by stirring in a ladle of broth.
  9. 9
    Serve in bowls topped with the 120 g grated Monterey Jack and the 2 diced avocados, with the 150 g tortilla chips and the 2 limes, in wedges, on the table.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Protein protein→turkey thighs

    Use 1.1 kg bone-in, skinless turkey thighs (about 2), salted with 9 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt. Brown on one side as for the chicken, then simmer covered 50 to 60 minutes, until the meat pulls easily from the bone. Shred, discard the bones, and continue from the beans. Turkey thighs are richer and gamier than chicken and stand up well to the green chiles.

  • Protein protein→ground turkey

    Use 900 g ground turkey, preferably 93% lean dark meat. Brown it in two batches in 30 ml / 2 tbsp oil over medium-high heat, pressing it flat and leaving it 4 minutes before breaking it into large pieces, so it browns instead of steaming gray. Ground turkey has little fat, so do not drain the pot. Season with 9 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt as it browns, then simmer in the stock for 15 minutes only.

  • Occasion protein→leftover roast turkey

    Skip salting and browning the meat. Simmer the stock, chiles, and aromatics for 15 minutes, then add 700 g shredded cooked turkey with the beans. Leftover turkey is already cooked, so heat it through only; longer simmering makes it stringy. A stock made from the turkey carcass in place of chicken stock is the best version.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • Anaheim or Hatch green chiles → canned roasted green chiles Two 4 oz cans, drained. Softer and slightly tinny, but convenient; skip the broiling.
  • Mexican crema → sour cream or cream cheese Use 120 g sour cream, tempered with a ladle of broth first, or 85 g cream cheese whisked in until smooth. Cream cheese makes the thickest, richest version.
  • Monterey Jack → pepper Jack Same weight. Adds a little more heat.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → cooked white beans, drained make it yourself Beans cooked from dried are creamy inside, and their starchy cooking liquid is what gives this chili its body.
  • → chicken stock make it yourself The broth is the backbone of a white chili; homemade stock brings gelatin and a clean chicken flavor that carton stock lacks.
  • → Mexican crema make it yourself Homemade crema is tangier than store-bought and stays smooth in hot broth.
  • → tortilla chips make it yourself Fresh tortillas cut into wedges and fried make chips that taste of toasted corn instead of stale oil.

Notes

Where the flavor comes from. White chili has no dried red chile to carry it, so the green chiles have to. Broiling them until blackened in patches gives a smoky, sweet flavor raw or sautéed peppers do not have, and peeling removes skins that would turn papery in the broth. Poblano brings depth, Anaheim brings bright green flavor, and jalapeño brings the heat.

Why thighs. Breast meat turns dry and fibrous in a simmered stew. Thighs have more fat and collagen, stay moist, and shred into tender pieces.

Thickening without flour. Many white chili recipes thicken with a flour roux or a lot of cream cheese, which can make the broth pasty or heavy. Mashed beans and their cooking liquid give a creamy body that tastes of the beans, and a modest amount of crema adds richness and a little tang.

Glutamate and acid. Browned chicken, chicken stock, the Parmesan rind in home-cooked white beans, and Monterey Jack at the table supply the savory base. Lime juice stirred in at the end, plus lime at the table, keeps a creamy pot from tasting flat.

Freezing. Freeze before stirring in the crema, cilantro, and lime, and add those after reheating; dairy can turn grainy when frozen and thawed.
