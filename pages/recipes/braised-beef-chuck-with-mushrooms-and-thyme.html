Braised beef chuck with mushrooms and thyme

A boneless chuck roast cut into large pieces, dry-brined, seared hard, and braised low in porter and stock with onions, garlic, tomato paste and thyme until it yields to a spoon. Browned cremini go in at the end and a splash of vinegar sharpens the sauce. Built to be cooked a day ahead and reheated on the stovetop.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Salt the beef the night before (or at least 1 hour ahead), uncovered on a rack in the fridge.
  • Braise it the day before you serve it. Chilled overnight, the fat sets on top and lifts off in one piece, and the meat reabsorbs sauce as it cools. Cool it in a shallow dish and refrigerate within 2 hours.
  • Reheating takes 45 to 60 minutes, covered, either on a burner over medium-low heat or in a 150 °C / 300 °F oven, whichever is free.

What to prep, in order 3 phases ▾

Step 1 · Salt (the night before)

  1. 1800 g boneless beef chuck roast, cut into 6 large pieces along the natural seams, patted dry
  2. 16 g kosher salt
  3. 3 g black pepper, freshly ground

Step 2 · Sear and braise

  1. 30 g neutral oil
  2. 400 g yellow onions, sliced, halved and sliced 5 mm thick — (2 medium onions)
  3. 24 g garlic cloves, smashed — (6 cloves)
  4. 1 × 6 oz / 170 g can tomato paste
  5. 20 g all-purpose flour
  6. 355 ml porter or stout
  7. 500 g chicken stock, unsalted
  8. 15 g soy sauce
  9. 6 g fresh thyme sprigs — (8 sprigs)
  10. 2 bay leaves

Step 3 · Mushrooms and finish

  1. 450 g cremini mushrooms, quartered
  2. 20 g unsalted butter
  3. 2 g kosher salt, for the mushrooms
  4. 10 g red wine vinegar
  5. 10 g flat-leaf parsley, chopped

Method

  1. 1
    Salt the beef (the night before). Season the 1800 g / 4 lb chuck pieces on all sides with the 16 g / 1 tbsp + 2½ tsp kosher salt and the 3 g / 1½ tsp black pepper. Refrigerate them uncovered on a wire rack over a plate, 12 to 24 hours (1 hour minimum). Technique: Dry brining12 h
  2. 2
    Sear. Heat the oven to 160 °C / 325 °F with a rack in the lower third. Pat the beef dry. Heat the 30 g / 2 tbsp neutral oil in a 6 to 7 litre / quart Dutch oven over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Sear the beef in 2 batches, without crowding, until deep brown on 2 or 3 sides, 4 to 5 minutes per side. Move the browned pieces to a plate. The brown crust and the dark film on the pot bottom are most of the flavor of the finished sauce. Technique: Maillard sear
  3. 3
    Build the base. Lower the heat to medium. Add the 400 g / 2 sliced onions and a pinch of the salt for the mushrooms, and cook, scraping the pot bottom with a wooden spoon, until soft and golden, 8 to 10 minutes. Add the 24 g / 6 smashed garlic cloves and the 30 g / 2 tbsp tomato paste and cook 2 minutes, until the paste darkens to brick red. Sprinkle in the 20 g / 2½ tbsp flour and stir 1 minute.
  4. 4
    Deglaze and braise. Pour in the 355 ml / 12 oz porter and scrape up every bit of the brown film. Simmer 3 minutes, then add the 500 g / 2 cups chicken stock, the 15 g / 1 tbsp soy sauce, the 6 g / 8 thyme sprigs and the 2 bay leaves. Return the beef and its juices; the liquid should come about halfway up the meat; top it up with water if it does not. Bring to a simmer, cover and braise in the oven 3 to 3½ hours, turning the pieces once halfway, until a fork slides into the thickest piece and twists out with no resistance. If the oven is busy, braise on the stovetop at the lowest bare simmer, turning the pieces every 45 minutes. Technique: Braising
  5. 5
    Cool and chill (if serving the next day). Discard the thyme stems and the 2 bay leaves. Transfer the beef and sauce to a shallow dish, cool 30 minutes on the counter, cover and refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking.
  6. 6
    Brown the mushrooms. On the day, heat the 20 g / 1½ tbsp butter in a wide skillet over medium-high heat. Add the 450 g / 1 lb quartered cremini in one layer and leave them alone 4 minutes, until browned underneath, then stir and cook 4 minutes more. Season with the rest of the 2 g / ¾ tsp salt for the mushrooms. Mushrooms browned separately keep their meaty bite; simmered in the braise from the start they turn soft and gray.
  7. 7
    Reheat and finish. Lift off the set fat from the chilled braise and discard it. Return the beef and sauce to the Dutch oven, cover and warm over medium-low heat (or in a 150 °C / 300 °F oven) 45 to 60 minutes, turning the pieces every 15 minutes, until the center of the largest piece reads 74 °C / 165 °F. Stir in the mushrooms and the 10 g / 2 tsp red wine vinegar. Taste the sauce: it should be savory and slightly bitter-sweet from the porter, with the vinegar lifting it. Add salt in small pinches if it tastes flat. Break the beef into large chunks, spoon the sauce over and scatter the 10 g / 3 tbsp parsley.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Dietary dietary→alcohol-free

    Replace the porter with 355 ml / 1½ cups more stock, 10 g / 2 tsp dark brown sugar and 10 g / 2 tsp balsamic vinegar. The sugar and vinegar replace the malt sweetness and the slight bitterness of the beer.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • porter or stout → dry red wine Same volume. Brighter and more acidic; skip the vinegar at the end and taste first.
  • boneless beef chuck roast → bone-in beef short ribs, 2.5 kg / 5½ lb Richer and more expensive; the extra weight is bone. Same timing.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → chicken stock, unsalted make it yourself Homemade stock is full of gelatin, which gives the braising liquid body without extra flour.

Notes

Why chuck. Chuck is a heavily worked shoulder muscle laced with connective tissue. Braised slowly between 85 and 95 °C inside the meat, that collagen turns into gelatin, which holds water and coats the fibers, so the meat tastes moist even though it is cooked far past well done. Lean roasts like round lack the collagen and turn dry and stringy in a braise. Chuck is also the beef cut most reliably found in any supermarket.

Why a day ahead. Gelatin sets as the braise cools, and the meat reabsorbs liquid as it rests. The flavors of the onion, porter and tomato paste also blend overnight. The chilled fat cap lifts off cleanly, which leaves a sauce that is rich without being greasy.

Umami and acid. Tomato paste, soy sauce and the browned mushrooms supply glutamate and give the sauce its depth. The red wine vinegar at the end is the finishing acid; without it the sauce tastes heavy.

Alcohol. Braising removes much of the alcohol in the porter but not all of it; after about 3 hours, roughly 5 to 10% may remain. For children or guests who avoid alcohol, use the alcohol-free modification.
