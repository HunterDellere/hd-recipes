Briam

Greek oven-roasted summer vegetables: potatoes, zucchini, red onion, and bell pepper layered in grated tomato and a generous pour of olive oil with garlic, rigani, and parsley, roasted slowly until the tomato has reduced into the oil and the top layer is browned at the edges. A lathera dish, served warm or at room temperature. Tomato and tomato paste carry the glutamate, a splash of red wine vinegar the acid.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • Salt the zucchini first (step 1) and let it drain 20 minutes while you cut everything else.
  • This is an 80-minute oven side, so start it well before the fish. It is better at room temperature an hour later, so it can be made first and held while the oven is raised for the fish.

Step 1 · Vegetables

  1. 500 g zucchini, cut into 1.5 cm rounds — (2 medium)
  2. 5 g kosher salt, for the zucchini
  3. 500 g Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled, cut into 6 mm slices — (3 medium)
  4. 200 g red onion, cut into 1 cm wedges — (1 medium)
  5. 250 g bell peppers, red or green, cut into 2 cm strips — (2 medium)
  6. 8 g kosher salt, for the vegetables

Step 2 · Tomato-oil bath

  1. 600 g ripe tomatoes, halved and grated on a box grater, skins discarded
  2. 1 × 6 oz / 170 g can tomato paste
  3. 110 g extra-virgin olive oil
  4. 20 g garlic cloves, thinly sliced — (5 cloves)
  5. 3 g dried Greek oregano (rigani)
  6. 1 g black pepper, freshly ground
  7. 20 g flat-leaf parsley, chopped, divided

Step 3 · Finish

  1. 10 g red wine vinegar
  2. 1 g flaky salt — (a pinch, to taste)

Method

  1. 1
    Salt the zucchini. Toss the 500 g / 2 zucchini rounds with the 5 g / 1½ tsp kosher salt for the zucchini in a colander and let drain 20 minutes. Salt pulls water out by osmosis; without this step the zucchini floods the pan and the briam stews instead of roasting. Heat the oven to 190 C / 375 F.
  2. 2
    Build the bath. Stir together the 600 g grated tomatoes, 15 g / 1 tbsp tomato paste, 110 g / ½ cup olive oil, 20 g / 5 sliced garlic cloves, 3 g / 1 tbsp rigani, 1 g / ½ tsp pepper, and half the 20 g parsley.
  3. 3
    Toss and layer. Pat the zucchini dry. In a large metal roasting pan (about 33 x 23 cm / 13 x 9 in), toss the 500 g potato slices, 200 g / 1 onion, 250 g / 2 peppers, and zucchini with the 8 g / 2½ tsp kosher salt for the vegetables and the tomato bath. Settle the potatoes toward the bottom where the liquid is deepest, and the zucchini on top, where it will brown.
  4. 4
    Roast covered. Cover tightly with foil and roast 40 minutes. The trapped steam cooks the potatoes through in the tomato liquid before anything can brown or dry out.
  5. 5
    Roast uncovered. Remove the foil, gently turn the vegetables once with a spatula, and roast another 35 to 40 minutes until the liquid has reduced to a thick tomato-oil sauce at the bottom of the pan and the top edges of the 500 g zucchini and 250 g peppers are browned. A knife should slide through a potato slice with no resistance. Technique: High-heat dry roasting
  6. 6
    Finish and rest. Sprinkle the 10 g / 2 tsp red wine vinegar over the pan and scatter the remaining parsley. Rest at least 15 minutes; briam is better warm than hot, and best at room temperature. Taste and add the 1 g pinch of flaky salt if the vegetables need it.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion main→briam with feta

    Lay 200 g sliced feta over the vegetables for the last 15 minutes of the uncovered roast; serve with bread as a vegetarian main.

  • Texture eggplant→briam with eggplant

    Replace half the zucchini with 1 medium eggplant in 2 cm cubes, salted and drained alongside the zucchini. Eggplant drinks the oil and makes the dish richer.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • ripe tomatoes → canned crushed tomatoes Use 400 g plus 100 ml water outside peak season. Grated fresh summer tomatoes are lighter and sweeter.
  • Yukon Gold potatoes → red-skinned potatoes Leave the skins on; same timing.

Notes

Why salt the zucchini. Zucchini is about 95% water. Without pre-salting, it releases that water in the oven, diluting the tomato bath so it takes twice as long to reduce and leaves the potatoes boiled rather than roasted. Twenty minutes in salt removes enough to make the difference.

Cut by density. Potatoes cut thin (6 mm), onions and peppers medium, zucchini thick (1.5 cm). A dense potato and a watery zucchini cut the same thickness finish 30 minutes apart.

Lathera. The half cup of olive oil is the point of the dish. It emulsifies with the reduced tomato into the sauce, carries the oregano and garlic, and is why briam tastes better at room temperature: the oil-tomato sauce sets and the flavors settle.

Umami and acid. Grated ripe tomato and a spoon of paste carry the glutamate; the vinegar at the end lifts what slow roasting has rounded off.

Sources
  • Adapted from Greek lathera tradition — Briam is the Greek cousin of ratatouille and tumbet, but where those cook vegetables separately, briam roasts everything together in a tomato-and-oil bath until the liquid reduces into the vegetables. Refinements: zucchini salted first to pull water, so the dish roasts rather than stews; vegetables cut by density (potatoes thinnest, zucchini thickest) so everything finishes together; a covered first half and uncovered second half so it cooks through, then browns.
