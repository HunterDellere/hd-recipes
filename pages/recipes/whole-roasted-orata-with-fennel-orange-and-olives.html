Recipe

Whole roasted orata with fennel, orange, and olives

Two whole sea bream roasted on a bed of fennel that has been given a head start until its edges caramelize, with orange wedges, black olives, and an anchovy-garlic oil spooned over. The Sicilian combination of anise, orange, and brine around a mild white fish. The anchovy melts into the pan juices for glutamate; orange and a splash of white wine vinegar supply the acid.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

The fennel roasts alone for 20 minutes before the fish joins it. Use that window to clean, score, and season the fish, and to make the anchovy oil.

What to prep, in order 3 phases ▾

Step 1 · Fennel bed

  1. 600 g fennel bulbs, trimmed, fronds reserved, cut through the root into 1.5 cm wedges — (2 medium bulbs)
  2. 150 g red onion, cut into 8 wedges through the root — (1 small)
  3. 40 g extra-virgin olive oil, for the fennel
  4. 6 g kosher salt, for the fennel

Step 2 · Fish

  1. 1100 g whole orata (gilthead sea bream), scaled, gutted, gills removed, patted dry, 3 slashes on each side
  2. 11 g kosher salt, for the fish
  3. 4 g fennel seed, lightly crushed
  4. 250 g orange, zested first; half sliced into rounds for the cavities, half cut into wedges for the pan — (1 large navel or blood orange)
  5. 14 g extra-virgin olive oil, for the fish

Step 3 · Anchovy oil and finish

  1. 40 g extra-virgin olive oil, for the anchovy oil
  2. 12 g anchovy fillets, oil-packed, finely chopped — (4 fillets)
  3. 12 g garlic cloves, thinly sliced — (3 cloves)
  4. 1 g red pepper flakes
  5. 80 g black olives, pitted, halved; Gaeta, Niçoise, or Kalamata
  6. 10 g white wine vinegar

Method

  1. 1
    Start the fennel. Heat the oven to 220 C / 425 F. On a rimmed sheet pan toss the 600 g / 2 bulbs fennel wedges and 150 g / 1 small red onion wedges with the 40 g / 3 tbsp olive oil for the fennel and the 6 g / 2 tsp kosher salt for the fennel. Spread cut-side down and roast 20 minutes until the undersides are browned and the wedges are starting to soften. Technique: High-heat dry roasting
  2. 2
    Season the fish while the fennel roasts. Season the 1100 g / 2 scored orata inside, outside, and into the slashes with the 11 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt for the fish and the zest of the 250 g orange. Fill each cavity with half the 4 g / 1½ tsp crushed fennel seed and half of the orange rounds. Rub the outside with the 14 g / 1 tbsp olive oil for the fish. Let sit at room temperature until the fennel is ready. Technique: Fish skin scoring
  3. 3
    Make the anchovy oil. In a small saucepan over low heat, warm the 40 g / 3 tbsp olive oil for the anchovy oil with the 12 g / 4 chopped anchovy fillets, the 12 g / 3 sliced garlic cloves, and the 1 g / ½ tsp pepper flakes. Mash until the anchovies dissolve and the garlic is soft and pale gold, 3 to 4 minutes. Off heat, stir in the 10 g / 2 tsp white wine vinegar. Technique: Blooming aromatics
  4. 4
    Roast the fish. Flip the fennel, push it to the edges, and lay the fish in the center. Tuck the orange wedges and the 80 g / ½ cup olives around them. Spoon half the anchovy oil over the fennel (not the fish skin, which should stay dry to crisp). Roast 16 to 20 minutes until the thickest part of the fish reads 55 C / 131 F. Orata is deeper-bodied than branzino and takes a few minutes longer.
  5. 5
    Finish. Squeeze 2 of the roasted orange wedges over the fennel; the caramelized juice mixes with the anchovy oil and fish drippings into a pan sauce. Spoon the remaining anchovy oil over the fish, scatter the chopped fennel fronds, and serve from the pan. Taste the fennel with a piece of fish and olive: the olive and anchovy should read as savory salt, the orange as bright sweetness. Add a few drops more vinegar if it reads sweet.
Safety
  • orata pulled at 55 C / 131 F (below USDA published-safe 145 F for fish) Standard Mediterranean doneness for lean whole fish; carryover adds 1 to 2 degrees. Cooks who are pregnant, immunocompromised, very young, or elderly should roast to 63 C / 145 F, about 4 minutes longer. Reference →

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Regional citrus→lemon and capers

    Replace the orange with lemon and the olives with 2 tbsp rinsed capers for a sharper, less sweet plate that reads more Ligurian.

  • Occasion add a starch→fennel and potato bed

    Add 400 g halved baby potatoes to the pan with the fennel and extend the head start to 30 minutes so they are tender when the fish goes in.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • whole orata → whole branzino, porgy, or black sea bass Branzino is slimmer and finishes 2 to 4 minutes sooner; porgy is a near-identical bream.
  • black olives → Castelvetrano green olives Milder and butterier; the plate loses some brine depth.
  • anchovy fillets → 1 tbsp white miso mashed into the oil Keeps the glutamate without fish; less briny.

Notes

Staggered roast. Fennel is fibrous and needs time for its pectin to soften and its sugars to caramelize; a whole bream needs only 18 minutes. Roasting them together from the start gives you either raw fennel or overcooked fish. The 20-minute head start lines them up.

Fennel twice. The bulb gives sweetness and anise after caramelization; the seed in the cavity gives a sharper, more concentrated anethole note that perfumes the flesh. Fronds at the end bring it back fresh.

Keeping the skin dry. Anchovy oil spooned over the skin before roasting keeps it soft. Oil the skin lightly, sauce the fennel, and save the rest of the anchovy oil for after the fish comes out.

Sources
  • Adapted from Sicilian whole-fish roasting with fennel and citrus — Fennel, orange, and olive is a Sicilian trio found in salads (insalata di arance) and around roasted fish. The structural move is the staggered roast: fennel needs about 35 minutes to soften and caramelize, a 550 g bream about 18, so the fennel goes in first. The fish is roasted with fennel seed in the cavity to echo the bulb.
