Recipe

Patates antinaxtes

Cypriot crushed potatoes: small new potatoes cracked open with a mallet, browned hard in olive oil, then braised covered in dry red wine with crushed coriander seed until tender, and uncovered to reduce the wine to a sticky, purple glaze. The cracked surfaces brown and drink the wine; coriander gives a warm citrus note. Finished with a grating of aged halloumi for salt and savor, and a squeeze of lemon.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

Use a wide skillet with a tight lid: the potatoes need a single layer to brown, then a covered braise to cook through. If you only have a lid that fits a smaller pan, cook in two batches.

What to prep, in order 3 phases ▾

Step 1 · Potatoes

  1. 1000 g baby new potatoes, scrubbed, skins on, each struck once with a mallet or the base of a heavy pan to crack it open without breaking it apart
  2. 80 g extra-virgin olive oil
  3. 9 g kosher salt

Step 2 · Wine braise

  1. 8 g coriander seeds, cracked coarsely in a mortar
  2. 200 ml dry red wine
  3. 60 ml water

Step 3 · Finish

  1. 30 g aged halloumi, finely grated
  2. 50 g lemon, cut into wedges — (½ lemon)
  3. 5 g flat-leaf parsley or cilantro, chopped

Method

  1. 1
    Crack the potatoes. Strike each of the 1000 g baby potatoes once with a meat mallet or the bottom of a heavy pan, hard enough to split the skin and open fissures in the flesh but not so hard that it falls apart. The cracked surfaces are where the browning and the wine will go.
  2. 2
    Brown hard. Heat the 80 g / ⅓ cup olive oil in a 30 cm / 12 in skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Add the potatoes in one layer, season with the 9 g / 1 tbsp kosher salt, and fry 10 to 12 minutes, turning occasionally, until deeply golden on most sides. They will not be cooked through; this is only for crust. Technique: Shallow frying
  3. 3
    Bloom the coriander. Lower the heat to medium, scatter the 8 g / 1 tbsp cracked coriander seeds over the potatoes, and shake the pan for 30 seconds until fragrant. Coriander seed is rich in linalool, which reads as warm citrus once toasted in fat. Technique: Blooming aromatics
  4. 4
    Braise in wine. Pour in the 200 ml / ¾ cup red wine and 60 ml / ¼ cup water (it will hiss and spit), shake the pan, cover tightly, and simmer over medium-low 12 to 15 minutes until a knife slides into the largest potato without resistance. Shake the pan once or twice. Technique: Braising
  5. 5
    Reduce to a glaze. Uncover, raise the heat to medium-high, and cook 3 to 5 minutes, shaking the pan, until the wine has reduced to a sticky dark glaze clinging to the potatoes and the oil starts to sizzle again. Taste: the wine should read as fruity acid, not raw alcohol. Technique: Reduction
  6. 6
    Finish. Transfer to a platter, grate over the 30 g / ⅓ cup aged halloumi, and scatter the 5 g / 2 tbsp parsley. Serve with the 50 g lemon wedges to squeeze at the table.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Occasion oven version→roasted antinaxtes

    For a crowd, crack 2 kg potatoes, toss with oil and salt on a sheet pan, and roast at 220 C / 425 F for 35 minutes. Add coriander and wine, roast 15 more minutes until the wine is absorbed and glazed.

  • Regional purist→traditional Cypriot version

    Omit the halloumi and lemon. Season with a little extra salt at the end; the wine supplies the acid.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • aged halloumi → pecorino romano or kefalotyri Any hard, salty sheep's-milk cheese grates and seasons the same way.
  • dry red wine → dry white wine Lighter and less tannic; the glaze is golden rather than purple.

Notes

Why crack instead of cut. A cut potato has a smooth face that browns evenly but takes up liquid only at the surface. A cracked potato has a fissured, irregular interior that gives more surface for browning and channels for the wine to soak in, and it holds together better than a halved potato through the shaking.

The order matters. Browning first, braising second. If you add wine to raw potatoes, they steam and never brown; the Maillard crust only forms in oil at temperatures above 140 C. Once the crust is set it survives the braise and then re-crisps a little as the glaze reduces.

Wine as the acid. The reduced red wine carries both tartaric acid and fruit sugars, which caramelize into the glaze. Cook off the raw alcohol fully in the reduction; under-reduced, the dish tastes boozy.

Sources
  • Adapted from the Cypriot meze dish patates antinaxtes — Antinaxtes means roughly "shaken" or "tossed": the potatoes are cracked, fried, and shaken in the pan with wine and coriander, the signature spice of Cypriot cooking. The traditional dish is just potatoes, oil, coriander, wine, and salt. The halloumi finish is a house divergence to give the side a glutamate source; it is Cypriot in spirit (grated dried halloumi goes over pasta there) but not part of the classic dish. Omit it for the purist version.
