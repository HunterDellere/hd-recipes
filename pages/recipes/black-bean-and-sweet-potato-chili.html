Black bean and sweet potato chili

Black beans simmered in a purée of toasted ancho and chipotle, built on cremini mushrooms cooked until they brown and stick, which gives the pot the savory depth that meat would. Sweet potato is roasted separately so it caramelizes and keeps its shape instead of dissolving.

Before you start

Read this first — the long-lead and coordination steps that need to be moving before you start prepping.

  • If you are cooking the beans from dried, do it the day before; the bean cooking liquid is part of this recipe. With canned beans, use four 15 oz cans, drained, and replace the bean liquid with more vegetable broth.
  • Get the sweet potato into the oven first. It roasts for about 30 minutes while you build the pot on the stove.

Method

  1. 1
    Roast the sweet potato. Heat the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F. Toss the 600 g cubed sweet potato with the 30 ml / 2 tbsp roasting oil and 4 g / 1 tsp kosher salt on a parchment-lined rimmed sheet pan and spread in a single layer with space between cubes. Roast 25 to 30 minutes, turning once, until browned at the edges and tender through. Roasting drives off surface water and caramelizes the sugars; simmered from raw, the cubes turn soft and bland and cloud the pot. Technique: High-heat dry roasting
  2. 2
    Toast the chiles, cumin, and pepitas. Heat a dry cast-iron skillet over medium heat. Press the 30 g ancho chiles flat against the pan for about 15 seconds per side until fragrant and a shade darker; pull them before they smell sharp. Toast the 4 g / 2 tsp cumin seeds for 1 minute until fragrant and tip them into the blender jar. Toast the 40 g pepitas for about 3 minutes, shaking, until they puff and start to pop, then set aside for serving. Technique: Dry-toasting (grains, spices, seeds, dried chilies)
  3. 3
    Soak the chiles in the 500 ml boiling water, weighted under a small plate, for 20 minutes. Drain and discard the water. Add the chiles to the blender with the cumin, the 20 g / 1 chipotle in adobo with its sauce, and the 400 g canned tomatoes with their juices. Blend on high 2 minutes until smooth.
  4. 4
    Brown the mushrooms while the chiles soak. Heat the 30 ml / 2 tbsp oil for the pot in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the 340 g chopped cremini and the 2 g / ½ tsp kosher salt for the mushrooms. Cook, stirring every minute or so, about 12 minutes: first they release a lot of water, then the pot goes dry and they start to stick and turn deep brown. Wait for that brown layer on the bottom; it is the savory base of the whole chili. Technique: Maillard sear
  5. 5
    Add the vegetables and aromatics. Lower the heat to medium and add the 300 g diced onion and 180 g diced red bell pepper. Cook 7 minutes, scraping the browned mushroom layer off the bottom as the vegetables release water, until soft. Add the 25 g minced garlic, 30 g / 2 tbsp tomato paste, 4 g / 2 tsp smoked paprika, and 2 g / 2 tsp Mexican oregano and stir 2 minutes until the paste darkens. Technique: Blooming aromatics
  6. 6
    Fry the purée. Pour in the chile and tomato purée and cook, stirring, about 5 minutes, until it darkens and thickens enough that the spoon leaves a trail.
  7. 7
    Simmer. Add the 1000 g black beans, 250 ml / 1 cup bean cooking liquid, 500 ml / 2 cups vegetable broth, and 8 g / 1 tbsp cocoa powder. Bring to a simmer, partly cover, and cook 30 minutes, stirring occasionally. In the last 5 minutes, mash about a cupful of beans against the side of the pot with the back of a spoon and stir them in; the released starch thickens the chili without flour or masa.
  8. 8
    Fold in the sweet potato. Gently stir in the 600 g roasted sweet potato and simmer 5 minutes, just long enough to heat through without breaking the cubes.
  9. 9
    Finish and season. Off the heat, stir in the 30 ml / 2 tbsp lime juice. Taste and add up to 4 g / 1 tsp kosher salt; beans absorb a lot of salt, so this pot usually needs most of it. Without the lime the chili tastes heavy and muddy, so do not skip it.
  10. 10
    Serve in bowls topped with the 2 diced avocados, the 15 g chopped cilantro, and the toasted pepitas, with the 2 limes, in wedges, at the table.

Modifications

Pivots that change the dish's character — protein swaps, regional variants, dietary recastings.

  • Texture beans→three-bean

    Replace half the black beans with an equal weight of cooked pinto and kidney beans. Pintos break down slightly more and make the chili creamier.

  • Regional vegetable→butternut squash

    Use 600 g butternut squash in 2 cm cubes in place of the sweet potato, roasted the same way. Less sweet, a little firmer.

  • Dietary diet→vegetarian with dairy

    Top with crumbled cotija or sour cream instead of, or along with, the avocado.

Substitutions

1:1 ingredient swaps where the dish stays the same dish.

  • cremini mushrooms → white button mushrooms Same weight. Slightly less savory; brown them a few minutes longer.
  • smoked paprika → one more chipotle in adobo More heat as well as more smoke.

Make it from scratch

Store-bought is fine, but each of these has a homemade version that earns the time.

  • → cooked black beans, drained make it yourselfstub Beans cooked from dried hold their shape better than canned and come with a seasoned, starchy cooking liquid that gives this chili its body.
  • → chipotle chiles in adobo, with sauce make it yourself Homemade adobo is less sweet and less vinegary than canned, so the smoke reads more clearly.
  • → vegetable broth make it yourself Carton vegetable broth is often dominated by celery and salt; homemade is cleaner and lets the chiles lead.

Notes

Where the depth comes from. A meat chili gets its savory base from browned beef and fond. Here, finely chopped cremini mushrooms do that job: once their water cooks off they brown and stick to the pot, building a fond rich in glutamate and Maillard compounds. Tomato paste fried until brick red adds more glutamate, and a small amount of cocoa adds bitterness that reads as depth rather than chocolate.

Why roast the sweet potato separately. Sweet potato simmered in the pot from raw softens unevenly, sheds starch that clouds the sauce, and tastes boiled. Roasting at high heat browns the outside and concentrates the sugars, and folding it in at the end keeps the cubes intact.

Acid matters more here. Beans and sweet potato are both dense and sweet. The lime at the end and at the table is what keeps the bowl from tasting heavy.
