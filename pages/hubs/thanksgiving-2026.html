2026 Thanksgiving

Thursday 26 November 2026, for 6 adults and a child. A Thanksgiving that keeps the familiar notes (turkey with sage gravy, a sharp cranberry relish, creamy potatoes, a glazed root vegetable, an apple dessert) and adds a roast rack of lamb. Two mains, three sides, soft rolls and two desserts. One oven, with the meatballs, the cake and every sauce made before Thursday.

main Herb-crusted rack of lamb with cranberry mostarda Main 1. Two racks, about 40 minutes of hands-on cooking on the day. The mostarda is the table's cranberry sauce for both mains, and it is made up to a week ahead.
main Turkey and sage meatballs with brown butter gravy Main 2, the turkey and the gravy. Cooked completely the day before and reheated on the stovetop, so it never needs the oven on Thursday.
side Hasselback potato gratin The rich side: a creamy center with crisp upright edges. It bakes first, then re-crisps beside the lamb.
side Maple-cumin roasted carrots The sweet-and-warm side. Miso for depth, lemon and pistachio to finish. Good at room temperature, which frees the oven for the lamb.
side Arugula, pear and pomegranate salad with goat cheese The sharp side, and the only raw, crisp thing on the table. Pepper, acid and crunch against the gratin, the gravy and the lamb fat.
bread Parker House rolls The bread, and something for the gravy. Shaped Wednesday night, baked Thursday morning while the oven is free, rewarmed for 5 minutes before dinner.
dessert Apple crumble with brown butter oat topping The warm dessert. It goes into the oven as everyone sits down and comes out warm for dessert.
dessert Tres leches cake The cold dessert, baked and soaked the day before. One 9 x 13 cake gives 12 squares, so there are leftovers.
made at home Crème fraîche Made at home for the whipped cream on both desserts. Start it by Monday.

Backups

Not on the menu. Swap one in if a dish fails, a guest needs it, or you change your mind.

backup main Whole roasted red snapper with sourdough-fennel stuffing The pescatarian option if a guest needs one, and the stuffing the menu otherwise leaves out. It roasts at 220 °C / 425 °F beside the lamb.
backup main Coq au vin The safety net: braised on Wednesday and reheated on the stovetop, so it needs no oven on Thursday. Use it if either main fails or runs short. Serves 6.
backup main Maple and mustard glazed turkey breast A carved turkey in place of the lamb, roasted in the lamb's afternoon oven time. Serves 6.
backup main Beef Wellington with red wine jus The showpiece in place of the lamb. Most of the work is on Wednesday, and it bakes from 3:55 pm at the lamb's temperature, with the gratin re-crisping above it.
backup side Roasted delicata squash with hot honey and pepitas Swap it in for the carrots if they look poor; it roasts at the same temperature and also holds at room temperature.
backup bread Sweet potato dinner rolls with honey butter Same schedule as the Parker House rolls: shaped Wednesday night, baked Thursday morning.
backup dessert Pumpkin Basque cheesecake Replaces the tres leches if you want the pumpkin note; baked 1 to 2 days ahead, so it uses no oven on Thursday.

Game plan

When to make each part, so everything lands hot at the same time.

Shopping notes

  • Ask the butcher for two frenched racks of lamb, about 800 g / 8 ribs each. With the meatballs beside it, that is 2 chops per guest.
  • Buy ground turkey from thigh meat or an 85/15 or 93/7 blend. Avoid 99% lean breast, which stays dry even with the gelatin.
  • Buy the cream for the crème fraîche as pasteurized, not ultra-pasteurized, if your store has it, and check that the buttermilk says "live cultures".

For 6 adults and a child

  • No recipe needs scaling. Every dish is written for 8, or for 6 as the only main, and with two mains on the table that leaves a margin of about a fifth for seconds and leftovers. The shopping list stays as it is.
  • For the child: the meatballs, gratin, rolls and carrots are the easy plate. Serve the mostarda on the side, because it has real mustard heat. Cut the child a slice from the end of a lamb rack, which cooks more than the center. If the Wellington replaces the lamb, give the child an end slice and check that it reads 63 °C / 145 °F. The Wellington jus and the coq au vin are wine-based, and cooking removes most but not all of the alcohol.

If you cannot find it

  • Rack of lamb: a 1.6 kg / 3½ lb boneless leg of lamb, rolled and tied. Sear and coat it the same way, then roast it at 220 °C / 425 °F to 52 °C inside, 45 to 55 minutes, from about 3:55 pm. Lay foil over the crust for the last 10 minutes if it darkens. Re-crisp the gratin after the leg comes out.
  • Ground turkey: ground chicken thigh, same weight and method.
  • Fresh sage: dried rubbed sage, 3 g / 1½ tsp in the meatballs. Skip the fried-leaf garnish.
  • Fresh cranberries: frozen cranberries work without thawing. If there are none at all, the lamb recipe gives a dried tart cherry version of the mostarda.
  • Pomegranate: 80 g / ½ cup dried cranberries or dried tart cherries in the salad.
  • Ripe Bosc pears: a crisp, sweet apple such as Honeycrisp, or a Fuyu persimmon, sliced thin.
  • Baby arugula: watercress, or a mix of frisée and baby spinach.
  • Gruyère: Comté, Emmental or an aged white cheddar.
  • Goat cheese: crumbled feta or a mild blue.
  • Rainbow carrots: orange carrots, or half carrots and half parsnips.
  • Crème fraîche, if the home batch fails or there is no time: a store-bought tub, or sour cream for the same tang.
  • Honeycrisp apples: Pink Lady, Braeburn, Jonagold or Gala. Keep the Granny Smith half.

Using a backup

  • Snapper (adds a pescatarian main): order two fish of 1.1 to 1.4 kg the week before, no longer than about 45 cm so both fit on a half sheet pan, and pick them up Wednesday. Cube the sourdough Wednesday and leave it out to stale. Make the stuffing Thursday morning and refrigerate it. At 3:15 pm salt the fish and refrigerate them on a rack. At 3:45 pm, once the carrots are out, rewarm the stuffing covered in its dish in the 220 °C oven to at least 60 °C. At 4:05 pm stuff the fish with the hot stuffing and roast them on the lower rack. The fish is done at 57 °C, and the stuffing in the cavity must reach 74 °C / 165 °F. With three dishes in the oven, everything takes about 5 minutes longer, so go by the thermometer and serve at about 5:10 pm.
  • Coq au vin (adds a main, or replaces one that fails): decide by Tuesday evening. Marinate the chicken in the wine Tuesday evening, 12 to 24 hours before cooking, then braise it Wednesday, before or after the tres leches. Cool it in shallow dishes within 2 hours and refrigerate. On Thursday at about 3:45 pm, reheat it on its own burner over medium-low heat, 45 to 60 minutes, stirring from the bottom every 10 minutes, until the chicken reads 74 °C / 165 °F, then hold it covered on low.
  • Turkey breast (replaces the lamb): salt it Wednesday between 3:30 and 9:30 pm and refrigerate it uncovered on a rack (18 to 24 hours). On Thursday, start the gratin at 12:45 pm instead of 12:30. Take the turkey out at 2:40 pm, and at 3:00 pm make the herb butter and work it under the skin. When the carrots come out at about 3:25, roast the turkey 20 minutes at 220 °C / 425 °F, then at 165 °C / 325 °F to 65 °C inside, mixing the glaze meanwhile. Glaze and roast to 71 °C, about 4:35. Rest it 20 minutes. As it comes out, raise the oven to 220 °C and re-crisp the gratin on the top rack, then rewarm the rolls at 190 °C. Dinner is at about 5:05.
  • Beef Wellington (replaces the lamb): salt the beef by 8 am Wednesday (or Tuesday night). Wednesday evening, sear it, make the duxelles, wrap it in prosciutto and duxelles, and make the jus base. On Thursday, wrap it in pastry by 3:00 pm and chill it. When the carrots come out at about 3:25, put a sheet pan in the 220 °C / 425 °F oven to heat. At 3:45 egg wash it, chill 5 minutes, wash again and score. Bake from 3:55 pm on the middle rack to 48 °C inside, checking from 4:25. Put the gratin on the top rack at 4:25, leaving room for the 14 cm tall Wellington. At 4:35 finish the jus. Rest the Wellington 10 minutes, then follow the main timeline from 4:45.
  • Delicata squash (replaces the carrots): roast it in the carrots' 2:50 to 3:40 pm slot. Make the hot honey and toast the pepitas on Tuesday.
  • Sweet potato rolls (replace the Parker House rolls): roast the sweet potato on Tuesday, then follow the same Wednesday evening and Thursday morning schedule. Take the butter for the honey butter out of the fridge 30 minutes before you beat it.
  • Pumpkin Basque cheesecake (replaces the tres leches): bake it Tuesday or Wednesday; it keeps 4 days. Take it out of the fridge 1 hour before dessert. Without the tres leches, the crème fraîche is only needed for the crumble.

By Monday 23 November

  • Start the crème fraîche. It cultures 18 to 36 hours on the counter, then needs at least 12 hours in the fridge.
  • Make the cranberry mostarda (up to a week ahead). It improves for the first 2 to 3 days.

Tuesday 24 November

  • Move the crème fraîche to the fridge once it is thick.
  • Make the salad vinaigrette and toast the almonds.
  • Toast the pecans, then make the crumble topping and keep it in the fridge.

Wednesday 25 November, daytime

  • Make the meatballs and the gravy. Cool them in shallow dishes and refrigerate them within 2 hours of cooking.
  • Bake the tres leches sponge, soak it, and refrigerate it overnight.
  • Mix the herb crust for the lamb and refrigerate it.

Wednesday 25 November, evening

  • After 4:30 pm, dry-brine the lamb racks uncovered on a rack in the fridge. The brine works best at 12 to 24 hours, and the racks roast at 4:25 pm Thursday.
  • Between 5:30 pm and midnight, make the roll dough, shape the rolls, and refrigerate them overnight under a fitted lid or an inverted sheet pan.

Thanksgiving morning (dinner at 5 pm)

  • At about 8:30, take the rolls out of the fridge to proof 60 to 90 minutes. Bake them and cool them on a rack, then hold them under a clean tea towel. Finish by 11:30 so the oven is free.
  • Peel and cut the carrots. Peel and slice the apples, toss them with the lemon juice, and refrigerate them covered with a plate.
  • Seed the pomegranate and grate the gruyère and parmigiano.

12:00 to 12:30 pm

  • Whip the crème fraîche cream, spread it on the tres leches, and return the cake to the fridge. The topping holds up to 6 hours.
  • Infuse the cream for the gratin.

12:30 to 2:45 pm

  • Slice and assemble the gratin. Bake it at 190 °C / 375 °F, 40 minutes covered and 35 to 45 minutes uncovered, then rest it uncovered on the counter. It can wait up to 2 hours before it re-crisps.

2:50 to 3:45 pm

  • Raise the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F with racks in the top and middle positions. Roast the carrots with their glaze, about 32 minutes, then hold them at room temperature. Leave the lemon, pistachios and herbs for serving.
  • At 3:20, take the lamb racks and the mostarda out of the fridge to temper.
  • At 3:30, start reheating the meatballs: add the held-back stock, cover, and warm over medium-low heat 45 to 60 minutes, stirring from the bottom every 10 minutes, until the largest reads 74 °C / 165 °F. Then hold them covered on low heat.

4:05 to 4:25 pm

  • Sear the lamb racks one at a time in cast iron, let them cool 5 minutes, then coat them with dijon and the herb crust.

4:25 to 4:45 pm

  • Roast the lamb on the middle rack at 220 °C / 425 °F to 52 °C inside, 15 to 20 minutes.
  • At the same time, re-crisp the gratin on the top rack for 15 to 20 minutes.

4:45 to 5:00 pm

  • Rest the lamb 10 minutes, uncovered. Take out the gratin and turn the oven down to 190 °C / 375 °F.
  • Rewarm the rolls loosely covered with foil for 5 to 6 minutes, and brush them with the remaining melted butter.
  • Toss the apples with the sugar, cornstarch and spice and top them with the crumble.
  • Finish the carrots with lemon, pistachios and herbs. Scatter parsley and the fried sage over the meatballs. Put the mostarda on the table for both mains, then carve the lamb into chops. Dress the salad at the table.

At dinner

  • As everyone sits down, put the crumble into the 190 °C oven for 45 to 50 minutes, so it comes out warm for dessert.
  • While the crumble rests, whip its crème fraîche cream. Serve the tres leches straight from the fridge, dusted with cinnamon.
