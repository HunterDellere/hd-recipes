2026 Thanksgiving

Thursday 26 November 2026, for 6 adults and a child. A Thanksgiving that keeps the familiar notes (turkey with sage gravy, a sharp cranberry relish, creamy potatoes, a glazed root vegetable, an apple dessert) and adds cider-glazed ribs, smoked outside or baked the day before. Two mains, three sides, soft rolls and two desserts. One oven, with the meatballs, the cake and every sauce made before Thursday.

main Cider-glazed baby back ribs Main 1. Two racks: smoke them on Thursday, bake them on Wednesday and glaze them in the oven on Thursday, or do one rack each way. Sage, fennel and mustard in the rub tie them to the turkey and the gravy.
main Turkey and sage meatballs with brown butter gravy Main 2, the turkey and the gravy. Cooked completely the day before and reheated on the stovetop, so it never needs the oven on Thursday.
side Hasselback potato gratin The rich side: a creamy center with crisp upright edges. It bakes first, then re-crisps while the ribs glaze.
side Maple-cumin roasted carrots The sweet-and-warm side. Miso for depth, lemon and pistachio to finish. Good at room temperature, which frees the oven for the ribs.
side Arugula, pear and pomegranate salad with goat cheese The sharp side, and the only raw, crisp thing on the table. Pepper, acid and crunch against the gratin, the gravy and the pork fat.
bread Parker House rolls The bread, and something for the gravy. Shaped Wednesday night, baked Thursday morning while the oven is free, rewarmed for 5 minutes before dinner.
dessert Apple crumble with brown butter oat topping The warm dessert. It goes into the oven as everyone sits down and comes out warm for dessert.
dessert Tres leches cake The cold dessert, baked and soaked the day before. One 9 x 13 cake gives 12 squares, so there are leftovers.
made at home Cranberry mostarda The table's cranberry sauce, for the ribs and the meatballs. Made up to a week ahead; it needs at least 12 hours in the fridge.
made at home Crème fraîche Made at home for the whipped cream on both desserts. Start it by Monday.

Backups

Not on the menu. Swap one in if a dish fails, a guest needs it, or you change your mind.

backup main Braised beef chuck with mushrooms and thyme The most reliable backup: supermarket ingredients, braised on Wednesday and reheated on a burner on Thursday, so it needs no oven. Use it in place of the ribs, or beside them if more people come.
backup main Coq au vin The other stovetop safety net: braised on Wednesday and reheated on a burner. Decide by Tuesday evening, because the chicken marinates overnight. Serves 6.
backup main Maple and mustard glazed turkey breast A carved turkey in place of the ribs, roasted in the afternoon oven slot. Serves 6.
backup main Beef Wellington with red wine jus The showpiece in place of the ribs. Most of the work is on Wednesday, and it bakes from 3:55 pm at 220 °C / 425 °F, with the gratin re-crisping above it.
backup main Whole roasted red snapper with sourdough-fennel stuffing The pescatarian option if a guest needs one, and the stuffing the menu otherwise leaves out. It roasts at 220 °C / 425 °F on the lower rack.
backup side Roasted delicata squash with hot honey and pepitas Swap it in for the carrots if they look poor; it roasts at the same temperature and also holds at room temperature.
backup bread Sweet potato dinner rolls with honey butter Same schedule as the Parker House rolls: shaped Wednesday night, baked Thursday morning.
backup dessert Pumpkin Basque cheesecake Replaces the tres leches if you want the pumpkin note; baked 1 to 2 days ahead, so it uses no oven on Thursday.

Game plan

When to make each part, so everything lands hot at the same time.

Pick the rib method

  • Smoker: both racks smoke on Thursday from 11 am, outside, so they take no oven time. Best if a smoker or a charcoal grill is at the house and someone can tend it for 5 hours.
  • Oven: both racks bake on Wednesday at 135 °C / 275 °F for 3 hours, then reheat and glaze on Thursday in the 220 °C oven from 3:50 pm. Best if the weather is bad or nobody wants to tend a fire.
  • One of each: smoke one rack and bake the other. If the smoker runs late or fails, the oven rack is already cooked. On the ribs page, switch on both methods.

Shopping notes

  • Buy 2 racks of baby back ribs, about 1.5 kg / 3¼ lb each, from any supermarket meat case. St. Louis-cut spare ribs also work but need 1 to 1½ hours longer.
  • For the smoker: 4 fist-sized apple or cherry wood chunks, plus charcoal or pellets for the smoker you have. Check the fuel on Wednesday.
  • Buy fresh unfiltered apple cider (about 540 g / 2¼ cups for the ribs), not hard cider and not apple juice.
  • Buy ground turkey from thigh meat or an 85/15 or 93/7 blend. Avoid 99% lean breast, which stays dry even with the gelatin.
  • Buy the cream for the crème fraîche as pasteurized, not ultra-pasteurized, if your store has it, and check that the buttermilk says "live cultures".

For 6 adults and a child

  • No recipe needs scaling. Every dish is written for 8, or for 6 as the only main, and with two mains on the table that leaves a margin of about a fifth for seconds and leftovers. Two racks give about 26 ribs, 3 or 4 per adult beside the meatballs.
  • For the child: the meatballs, gratin, rolls, carrots and ribs are the easy plate. The rib rub has only a little cayenne; for a mild rack, leave it out of that rack's rub. Serve the mostarda on the side, because it has real mustard heat. The Wellington jus, the coq au vin and the beef braise are made with wine or beer, and cooking removes most but not all of the alcohol; the beef recipe has an alcohol-free version.

If you cannot find it

  • Baby back ribs: St. Louis-cut spare ribs, with 1 to 1½ hours more cooking. If there are no ribs at all, make the braised beef chuck from the Backups.
  • Wood chunks: chips or pellets, or no wood and the oven method; the smoked paprika in the rub gives a smoky note.
  • Fresh apple cider: unsweetened apple juice, reduced the same way, with an extra 5 g / 1 tsp cider vinegar in the glaze.
  • Ground turkey: ground chicken thigh, same weight and method.
  • Fresh sage: dried rubbed sage, 3 g / 1¼ tbsp in the meatballs. Skip the fried-leaf garnish.
  • Fresh cranberries: frozen cranberries work without thawing. If there are none at all, the mostarda recipe gives a dried tart cherry version.
  • Brown mustard seeds: use all yellow mustard seeds in the mostarda.
  • Pomegranate: 80 g / ½ cup dried cranberries or dried tart cherries in the salad.
  • Ripe Bosc pears: a crisp, sweet apple such as Honeycrisp, or a Fuyu persimmon, sliced thin.
  • Baby arugula: watercress, or a mix of frisée and baby spinach.
  • Gruyère: Comté, Emmental or an aged white cheddar.
  • Goat cheese: crumbled feta or a mild blue.
  • Rainbow carrots: orange carrots, or half carrots and half parsnips.
  • Crème fraîche, if the home batch fails or there is no time: a store-bought tub, or sour cream for the same tang.
  • Honeycrisp apples: Pink Lady, Braeburn, Jonagold or Gala. Keep the Granny Smith half.

Using a backup

  • Braised beef chuck (replaces the ribs, or adds a main): salt the beef Tuesday evening. Braise it Wednesday at 160 °C / 325 °F for 3 to 3½ hours; if the oven-method ribs need the oven, braise it on the stovetop at the lowest simmer. Cool it in a shallow dish and refrigerate within 2 hours. On Thursday, brown the mushrooms at about 3:00 pm, then from 3:45 reheat the braise on its own burner over medium-low heat, 45 to 60 minutes, until the largest piece reads 74 °C / 165 °F. Hold it covered on low. At 4:45, stir in the mushrooms and vinegar and scatter the parsley.
  • Coq au vin (adds a main, or replaces one that fails): decide by Tuesday evening. Marinate the chicken in the wine Tuesday evening, 12 to 24 hours before cooking, then braise it Wednesday, before or after the tres leches. Cool it in shallow dishes within 2 hours and refrigerate. On Thursday at about 3:45 pm, reheat it on its own burner over medium-low heat, 45 to 60 minutes, stirring from the bottom every 10 minutes, until the chicken reads 74 °C / 165 °F, then hold it covered on low. Add the garnish just before serving, as the recipe says.
  • Turkey breast (replaces the ribs): salt it Wednesday between 3:30 and 9:15 pm and refrigerate it uncovered on a rack (18 to 24 hours). On Thursday, start the gratin at 1:00 pm instead of 12:45. Take the turkey out at 2:40 pm, and at 3:00 pm make the herb butter and work it under the skin. When the carrots come out at about 3:30, roast the turkey 20 minutes at 220 °C / 425 °F, then at 165 °C / 325 °F to 65 °C inside, mixing the glaze meanwhile. Glaze and roast to 71 °C, about 4:35. Rest it 20 minutes. As it comes out, raise the oven to 220 °C and re-crisp the gratin on the top rack, then rewarm the rolls at 190 °C. Dinner is at about 5:05.
  • Beef Wellington (replaces the ribs): salt the beef by 8 am Wednesday (or Tuesday night). Wednesday evening, sear it, make the duxelles, wrap it in prosciutto and duxelles, and make the jus base. On Thursday, wrap it in pastry by 3:00 pm and chill it. When the carrots come out at about 3:30, put a sheet pan in the 220 °C / 425 °F oven to heat. At 3:45 egg wash it, chill 5 minutes, wash again and score. Bake from 3:55 pm on the middle rack to 48 °C inside, checking from 4:25. Put the gratin on the top rack at 4:25, leaving room for the 14 cm tall Wellington. At 4:35 finish the jus. Rest the Wellington 10 minutes, then follow the main timeline from 4:45.
  • Snapper (adds a pescatarian main): order two fish of 1.1 to 1.4 kg the week before, no longer than about 45 cm so both fit on a half sheet pan, and pick them up Wednesday. Cube the sourdough Wednesday and leave it out to stale. Make the stuffing Thursday morning and refrigerate it. At 3:15 pm salt the fish and refrigerate them on a rack. At 3:45 pm, once the carrots are out, rewarm the stuffing covered in its dish in the 220 °C oven to at least 60 °C. At 4:05 pm stuff the fish with the hot stuffing and roast them on the lower rack. The fish is done at 57 °C, and the stuffing in the cavity must reach 74 °C / 165 °F. With three dishes in the oven, everything takes about 5 minutes longer, so go by the thermometer and serve at about 5:10 pm.
  • Delicata squash (replaces the carrots): roast it in the carrots' 2:50 to 3:40 pm slot. Make the hot honey and toast the pepitas on Tuesday.
  • Sweet potato rolls (replace the Parker House rolls): roast the sweet potato on Tuesday, then follow the same Wednesday evening and Thursday morning schedule. Take the butter for the honey butter out of the fridge 30 minutes before you beat it.
  • Pumpkin Basque cheesecake (replaces the tres leches): bake it Tuesday or Wednesday; it keeps 4 days. Take it out of the fridge 1 hour before dessert. Without the tres leches, the crème fraîche is only needed for the crumble.

By Monday 23 November

  • Start the crème fraîche. It cultures 18 to 36 hours on the counter, then needs at least 12 hours in the fridge.
  • Make the cranberry mostarda (up to a week ahead). It tastes best after 2 or 3 days.

Tuesday 24 November

  • Move the crème fraîche to the fridge once it is thick.
  • Make the salad vinaigrette and toast the almonds.
  • Toast the pecans, then make the crumble topping and keep it in the fridge.
  • Make the rib glaze and refrigerate it in a jar.
  • Oven-method ribs: in the evening, peel the membrane, salt the racks and refrigerate them uncovered on a rack.

Wednesday 25 November, daytime

  • Make the meatballs and the gravy. Cool them in shallow dishes and refrigerate them within 2 hours of cooking.
  • Bake the tres leches sponge, soak it, and refrigerate it overnight.
  • Oven order: the meatballs first (they roast hot), then the cake at 175 °C, then the ribs at 135 °C.
  • Oven-method ribs: after the cake, mix the rub, brush the racks with the Dijon, rub them, and bake them at 135 °C / 275 °F, 30 minutes uncovered and 2 to 2½ hours wrapped, until a probe slides between the bones. Cool them in their foil and refrigerate them within 2 hours.

Wednesday 25 November, evening

  • Smoker-method ribs: by 7 pm, peel the membrane, salt the racks and refrigerate them uncovered on a rack. Check the smoker fuel and the wood.
  • Between 5:30 pm and midnight, make the roll dough, shape the rolls, and refrigerate them overnight under a fitted lid or an inverted sheet pan.

Thanksgiving morning (dinner at 5 pm)

  • At about 8:30, take the rolls out of the fridge to proof 60 to 90 minutes. Bake them and cool them on a rack, then hold them under a clean tea towel. Finish by 11:30 so the oven is free.
  • Peel and cut the carrots. Peel and slice the apples, toss them with the lemon juice, and refrigerate them covered with a plate.
  • Seed the pomegranate and grate the gruyère and parmigiano.

Smoker-method ribs, 10:00 am to 4:45 pm

  • At 10:00, light the smoker for 121 °C / 250 °F. At 10:30, mix the rub, brush the racks with the Dijon, and rub them.
  • At 11:00, put the ribs on. Spray them every 45 minutes after the first hour.
  • At about 1:30, wrap them in foil with the butter, honey and cider.
  • At about 3:15, when a probe slides between the bones, leave the racks sealed in their foil and hold them in a cooler warmed with hot water, wrapped in towels. In cold weather everything runs later; the cooler gives up to 2 hours of slack.
  • At 4:15, unwrap them and glaze them twice on the smoker, 25 to 30 minutes. If the fire has gone out, glaze them in the oven beside the oven rack from 4:25.

12:15 to 2:40 pm

  • Infuse the cream for the gratin.
  • At 12:45, slice and assemble the gratin. Bake it at 190 °C / 375 °F, 40 minutes covered and 35 to 45 minutes uncovered, then rest it uncovered on the counter. It can wait up to 2 hours before it re-crisps.
  • While the gratin bakes, from about 1:00 pm, whip the crème fraîche cream, spread it on the tres leches, and return the cake to the fridge. The topping holds up to 6 hours, which covers dessert.

2:40 to 3:45 pm

  • Raise the oven to 220 °C / 425 °F with racks in the top and middle positions. Roast the carrots with their glaze, about 30 minutes, and take them out at about 3:30. Hold them at room temperature. Leave the lemon, pistachios and herbs for serving.
  • At 3:30, start reheating the meatballs: add the held-back stock, cover, and warm over medium-low heat 45 to 60 minutes, stirring from the bottom every 10 minutes, until the largest reads 74 °C / 165 °F. Then hold them covered on low heat.
  • At 3:45, take the mostarda out of the fridge.

3:45 to 4:45 pm

  • Oven-method ribs: at 3:45, put the sealed foil packets on the middle rack for 35 to 45 minutes, until the thickest part reads 74 °C / 165 °F. At about 4:25, open them, pour off the juices, brush with glaze and roast 6 minutes, then brush again and roast 6 to 8 minutes more.
  • At 4:25, re-crisp the gratin on the top rack for 15 to 20 minutes.
  • At 4:30, take the salad dressing out of the fridge.

4:45 to 5:00 pm

  • Rest the ribs 10 minutes, then cut both racks between the bones and brush the cut ribs with the last of the glaze.
  • Take out the gratin and turn the oven down to 190 °C / 375 °F. Rewarm the rolls loosely covered with foil for 4 to 5 minutes.
  • Slice the pears and assemble the salad undressed.
  • Toss the apples with the sugar, cornstarch and spice and top them with the crumble.
  • Finish the carrots with lemon, pistachios and herbs. Scatter parsley and the fried sage over the meatballs. Put the mostarda on the table for both mains. Dress the salad at the table.

At dinner

  • As everyone sits down, put the crumble into the 190 °C oven for 45 to 55 minutes, then rest it at least 15 minutes, so it is warm for dessert.
  • While the crumble rests, whip its crème fraîche cream. Serve the tres leches straight from the fridge, dusted with cinnamon.
